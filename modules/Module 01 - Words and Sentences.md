# Module 1: Words and Sentences

الكَلِمَةُ وَالجُمْلَةُ

## Before you begin

You should be able to recognise Arabic letters and read short vowelled words. No previous grammar is assumed. Work through one lesson at a time. Attempt the exercises before opening the accompanying answers.

In each analysis, ask: **What kind of word is this? What is its role? What is it connected to? What explains its ending?** This is the beginning of **تَرْكِيب** (*tarkīb*): explaining how the parts of an expression work together.

Early examples have vowel marks and English help. Later exercises remove some of that support. When adding endings, give the grammatical ending used in connected speech; do not replace it with a pause-ending.

By the end, you should be able to classify words and sentences, locate expressed and understood subjects, analyse phrases and their attachments, and distinguish three ways in which grammatical inflection appears.

## Lesson 1: Words and nouns

### The core idea

A **كَلِمَة** (*kalimah*, word) is a single meaningful unit: **قَوْلٌ مُفْرَدٌ**.

| Term | Meaning here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| لَفْظ (*lafẓ*) | A sound uttered by the mouth, meaningful or otherwise | A spoken sound |
| قَوْل (*qawl*) | Meaningful utterance | كِتَابٌ, “a book” |
| مُفْرَد (*mufrad*) | A unit whose component sounds do not separately express parts of its intended meaning | The letters of رَجُلٌ do not each mean part of “a man” |
| مُرَكَّب (*murakkab*) | An expression with meaningful components | كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ, “the boy’s book” |

Here **مفرد** means “a single unit.” In discussions of number it can instead mean “singular.” Learn the meaning required by the discussion.

Some expressions formed by joining words function as a single unit: a blended place-name such as **بَعْلَبَكّ**, and compound numerals from eleven to nineteen. A number such as **أَحَدٌ وَعِشْرُونَ**, twenty-one, instead contains coordination with **وَ**, “and.” Recognise the distinction; their detailed endings are not needed here.

Every word belongs to one of three classes:

- **اِسْم** (*ism*): a noun or noun-class word. Its meaning is not tied to tense through a verbal form.
- **فِعْل** (*fiʿl*): a verb. Its form connects its meaning with time.
- **حَرْف** (*ḥarf*): a particle. Its grammatical meaning is completed through another word or a sentence.

Arabic **اسم** includes names, ordinary nouns, descriptive words, and pronouns. Thus **خَالِدٌ** (Khalid), **عِلْمٌ** (knowledge), **صَالِحٌ** (righteous), and **هُوَ** (he) are all nouns in this classification.

### Recognising a noun

A **عَلَامَة** (*ʿalāmah*) is a diagnostic sign. A noun need not display every sign.

| Sign | Example | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| جَرّ: being in the genitive | فِي البَيْتِ — in the house | البَيْتِ is governed by فِي |
| أَلْ لِلتَّعْرِيف: the definite article | الكِتَابُ — the book | أَلْ makes this noun definite |
| تَنْوِين: an added final n-sound | كِتَابٌ — a book | The doubled vowel sign represents tanwīn |
| إِسْنَاد إِلَيْهِ: having something predicated of it | خَالِدٌ صَالِحٌ — Khalid is righteous | Something is said about خَالِدٌ |
| نِدَاء: being addressed | يَا خَالِدُ — O Khalid! | خَالِدُ is the مُنَادَى, the addressee |
| إِضَافَة: being the first member of a noun-link | كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ — the boy’s book | كِتَابُ is مُضَاف; الوَلَدِ is مُضَاف إِلَيْهِ |

**إضافة** (*iḍāfah*) links two nouns, often with a meaning like “of.” Its second member is genitive. The first member’s case depends on its role in the larger sentence. In the ordinary construction here, the first member has neither **أل** nor tanwīn: **كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ**, not **كِتَابٌ الوَلَدِ**.

Two cautions prevent false identifications:

- The mere presence of **يَا** does not make the following word a noun. Before **لَيْتَ**, “if only,” **يا** may introduce attention, or an addressee may be understood.
- A preposition before a larger expression does not put an individual verb or particle into the genitive. The whole expression may function as a noun.

The tanwīn test concerns grammatical tanwīn, not an extra sound used merely for poetic rhythm.

### Two ways of classifying nouns

**By expression:** an **اِسْم صَرِيح** (*ism ṣarīḥ*) is an explicit noun, such as **القِرَاءَةُ**, “reading.” An **اِسْم مُؤَوَّل** (*ism muʾawwal*) is a particle with its following clause understood as a noun. For example:

**أَنْ تَقْرَأَ نَافِعٌ.** — Your reading is beneficial.

Here **أَنْ تَقْرَأَ**, “that you read,” functions like **قِرَاءَتُكَ**, “your reading.” **أَنْ** is a **حَرْف مَصْدَر**; the entire combination is also called a **مَصْدَر مُؤَوَّل**. A **مَصْدَر** names an action without assigning it a verbal tense. The final fatḥah of **تَقْرَأَ** will be explained when we study government.

**By meaning:** distinguish the entity or quality being described from the descriptive word.

| Division | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| مَوْصُوف: اِسْم عَيْن | An entity that exists in its own right | رَجُلٌ — a man |
| مَوْصُوف: اِسْم مَعْنًى | An abstract meaning or quality, existing in something else | عِلْمٌ — knowledge |
| صِفَة | A word expressing a description of something | عَالِمٌ — knowledgeable; a learned person |

These classify meaning. They do not yet tell you a word’s sentence role. A descriptive word can, for example, be a predicate.

### Worked analysis

**كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ** — the boy’s book

1. This is **مركب**: both members contribute meaning.
2. Both members are **اسمان**, two nouns.
3. **كتاب** is **مضاف**; **الولد** is **مضاف إليه**, genitive.
4. The expression identifies a book but does not yet say anything about it. It is not a complete statement.

Read: **الكَلِمَةُ ثَلَاثَةُ أَقْسَامٍ: اِسْمٌ وَفِعْلٌ وَحَرْفٌ.**

Vocabulary: **أقسام** = divisions; **نحو** = for example; **يدلّ على** = indicates.

## Lesson 2: Verbs, particles, and the forms you need

### Verbs express an event through a time-bearing form

A verb brings together **حَدَث** (an event or action), **زَمَان** (time), and a connection to a **فَاعِل** (the subject to whom the action is attributed).

| Form | Term | Basic meaning |
|---|---|---|
| كَتَبَ | فِعْل مَاضٍ (*māḍī*) | He wrote |
| يَكْتُبُ | فِعْل مُضَارِع (*muḍāriʿ*) | He writes / is writing / will write, according to context |
| اُكْتُبْ | فِعْل أَمْر (*amr*) | Write! — to one male |
| لَا تَكْتُبْ | نَهْي (*nahy*) | Do not write! — to one male |

**نهي** is made here from prohibitive **لا** and a **مضارع**; it is not a fourth basic verb form. A past form can receive a future sense in a conditional sentence. Form and contextual time must therefore be distinguished.

**صَرْف** (*ṣarf*) studies word formation and changes of form. The verb has **مَادَّة**, its root material, and **هَيْئَة**, its form or pattern. In **نَصَرَ**, “he helped,” **ن ص ر** supplies the central meaning and the pattern **فَعَلَ** supplies its past verbal form. Do not assume that every verb has identical internal vowels.

Words such as **أَمْسِ** (yesterday), **الآنَ** (now), and **غَدًا** (tomorrow) name time; this does not make them verbs. Their time-meaning belongs to the word itself, not to a verbal tense-pattern. Likewise, a descriptive noun can suggest an action without becoming a verb.

### Recognising verbs

The ability to take related past, present/future, command, and prohibition forms is a general clue. Another is attachment of subject pronouns: **كَتَبْتُ**, “I wrote,” and **اُكْتُبُوا**, “write!” to a male or mixed group. Some verbs have restricted forms, so do not require every verb to pass every test.

| Diagnostic feature | Example | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| تَاء التَّأْنِيث السَّاكِنَة: the feminine marker تْ | كَتَبَتْ — she wrote | A past verb; تْ is not itself the subject |
| لَمْ | لَمْ يَكْتُبْ — he did not write | A مضارع form despite the past meaning |
| لَنْ | لَنْ يَكْتُبَ — he will not write | A مضارع |
| سَـ or سَوْفَ | سَيَكْتُبُ / سَوْفَ يَكْتُبُ — he will write | A مضارع |
| A genuine حَرْف مُضَارَعَة | أَكْتُبُ، نَكْتُبُ، يَكْتُبُ، تَكْتُبُ | The prefixes أ، ن، ي، ت within a مضارع form |
| قَدْ | قَدْ كَتَبَ / قَدْ يَكْتُبُ | It can precede past or مضارع verbs |
| A request, together with acceptance of يَاء المُخَاطَبَة | اُكْتُبْ → اُكْتُبِي | An imperative; the second form addresses one female |

**قَدْ كَتَبَ** can mean “he has written”; **قَدْ يَكْتُبُ** can mean “he may write.” Its force depends on context.

**نُونَا التَّأْكِيد**, the heavy **نَّ** and light **نْ** of emphasis, also occur with verbal forms: **لَيَكْتُبَنَّ / لَيَكْتُبَنْ**, “he will certainly write.” They can occur with commands too. Recognise these endings here; you do not yet need to construct them.

An initial **ي** is not enough to prove that a word is a verb: **يَدٌ**, “a hand,” is a noun. Similarly, the command-like meaning of a word is not by itself sufficient: both meaning and grammatical behaviour matter.

### Particles need a connection

A **حرف** is recognised by lacking the grammatical signs of nouns and verbs. Its meaning is relational, not meaningless:

- **فِي البَيْتِ**: **في** expresses “in” through its relation to the house.
- **قَدْ كَتَبَ**: **قد** modifies the force of the verb.
- **إِنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ**: **إنّ**, “indeed,” gives emphasis to a whole assertion.

Learn particles in their actual use. Some written forms have different grammatical classifications in different contexts.

**حَرْف** also has a second use:

- **حُرُوف المَبَانِي**: alphabet letters, such as ب، ت، م.
- **حُرُوف المَعَانِي**: meaningful grammatical particles, such as فِي، هَلْ، بَلْ.

For reading, distinguish **حَرْف مَدّ** from **حَرْف لِين**. A long-vowel letter follows its matching vowel: **قَالَ، يَقُولُ، قِيلَ**. A **لين** letter is silent **وْ** or **يْ** after fatḥah: **خَوْفٌ**, fear; **خَيْرٌ**, good. These are descriptions of letters and sounds, not additional word classes.

### Worked analysis

**لَمْ يَكْتُبْ خَالِدٌ.** — Khalid did not write.

- **لم**: a particle of negation which governs the following verb.
- **يكتب**: a **فعل مضارع**; its form is identified by its verbal prefix and acceptance of **لم**.
- **خالد**: an **اسم**; the writing is attributed to him.
- The meaning is past because of **لم**. The verb’s grammatical classification remains **مضارع**.

Read: **الفِعْلُ يَدُلُّ عَلَى مَعْنًى مُقْتَرِنٍ بِزَمَانٍ.**

**مقترن بـ** = connected with. Explain why this does not classify **غدًا** as a verb.

2. Match **لم، لن، سوف، قد** with: “may precede either past or مضارع,” “negates the future,” “marks future time,” “negates an action in the past using a مضارع.”

## Lesson 3: Building a sentence and beginning tarkīb

### The two pillars

A **جُمْلَة** (*jumlah*) contains a predication: something is attributed to something else. Its two pillars are:

| Term | Question | In a basic nominal sentence | In a basic verbal sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| مُسْنَد إِلَيْهِ (*musnad ilayh*), also مَحْكُوم عَلَيْهِ | About whom or what? | مُبْتَدَأ (*mubtadaʾ*) | فَاعِل (*fāʿil*) |
| مُسْنَد (*musnad*), also مَحْكُوم بِهِ | What is attributed? | خَبَر (*khabar*) | فِعْل |

Both pillars are **عُمْدَة** (*ʿumdah*), structural essentials. Other elements are called **فَضْلَة** (*faḍlah*) or **قَيْد** (*qayd*), qualifications of the basic predication. “Faḍlah” does not mean useless or always removable without affecting the intended meaning.

**خَالِدٌ جَالِسٌ.** — Khalid is sitting.

**خالد** is the **مبتدأ**, the person spoken about. **جالس** is the **خبر**, what is said about him. Arabic needs no present-tense word for “is” here.

**جَلَسَ خَالِدٌ.** — Khalid sat.

**جلس** is the verb and **مسند**. **خالد** is its **فاعل** and **مسند إليه**.

### The endings needed now

Distinguish a grammatical status from its sign. For the ordinary singular nouns used in these first analyses:

| Status | Description of the word | Usual sign here |
|---|---|---|
| رَفْع (*rafʿ*) — nominative | مَرْفُوع | ضَمَّة: ـُ, or ـٌ with tanwīn |
| نَصْب (*naṣb*) — accusative | مَنْصُوب | فَتْحَة: ـَ, or ـً with tanwīn |
| جَرّ (*jarr*) — genitive | مَجْرُور | كَسْرَة: ـِ, or ـٍ with tanwīn |

The **مبتدأ**, ordinary **خبر**, and **فاعل** are nominative. A **مَفْعُول بِهِ** (*mafʿūl bihi*, direct object) is accusative. A noun governed by a preposition, and the second member of an iḍāfah, are genitive. Other noun patterns have other signs; these vowels are not a universal rule for every noun.

### Find the subject even when it is not a separate word

A **ضَمِير** (*ḍamīr*) is a pronoun. It is a noun-class word. Useful forms are **هُوَ** (he), **هِيَ** (she), **أَنَا** (I), **نَحْنُ** (we), **أَنْتَ** (you, male singular), and **أَنْتِ** (you, female singular).

| Expression | Subject | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| هُوَ جَالِسٌ — he is sitting | هُوَ is the مبتدأ | ضَمِير مُنْفَصِل: detached pronoun |
| كَتَبْتُ — I wrote | ـتُ is the فاعل | ضَمِير مُتَّصِل بَارِز: attached, expressed pronoun |
| كَتَبْتَ / كَتَبْتِ — you wrote | ـتَ / ـتِ is the فاعل | Attached pronoun: male / female singular |
| أَكْتُبُ — I write | Understood أَنَا is the فاعل | ضَمِير مُسْتَتِر: implicit pronoun |
| اُكْتُبْ — write! | Understood أَنْتَ is the فاعل | Implicit pronoun |

In **كَتَبَتْ**, “she wrote,” **تْ** marks feminine gender; the subject is understood **هي**. In **كَتَبَتْ هِنْدٌ**, “Hind wrote,” **هند** is the expressed subject. Do not add a second subject-pronoun to that sentence.

### Worked tarkīb

**كَتَبَ خَالِدٌ الدَّرْسَ.** — Khalid wrote the lesson.

| Word | Analysis | Relationship or ending |
|---|---|---|
| كَتَبَ | فعل ماضٍ; مسند | Its final fatḥah belongs to its fixed form, not accusative case |
| خَالِدٌ | فاعل; مسند إليه | مرفوع بالضمة: nominative with ḍammah |
| الدَّرْسَ | مفعول به; فضلة | منصوب بالفتحة: accusative with fatḥah |

**كَتَبْتُ الدَّرْسَ.** contains the same subject–verb relationship. The subject is now **تُ**. Arabic writing can join grammatical components in one written form.

Read: **خَالِدٌ فَاعِلٌ مَرْفُوعٌ، وَالدَّرْسَ مَفْعُولٌ بِهِ مَنْصُوبٌ.**

The word being analysed is cited with its own ending: **الدَّرْسَ**. The following Arabic words explain its role.

## Lesson 4: Sentence structure and sentences inside sentences

### Nominal and verbal structure

A **جُمْلَة اِسْمِيَّة** (*jumlah ismiyyah*) has a nominal foundation. A **جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة** (*jumlah fiʿliyyah*) has a verbal foundation. Find the underlying construction; do not simply inspect the first printed word.

| Sentence | Analysis |
|---|---|
| خَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ — Khalid is truthful | Nominal: مبتدأ + خبر |
| صَدَقَ خَالِدٌ — Khalid spoke truthfully | Verbal: فعل + فاعل |
| الدَّرْسَ كَتَبَ خَالِدٌ — It was the lesson that Khalid wrote | Verbal: الدرسَ is a fronted object; كتب خالد is the foundation |

A **نَاسِخ** (*nāsikh*) enters a nominal construction and changes its grammatical treatment. Learn these two patterns:

| Pattern | Meaning | Roles and endings |
|---|---|---|
| إِنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ | Indeed, Khalid is truthful | خالدًا: اسم إنّ منصوب; صادقٌ: خبر إنّ مرفوع |
| كَانَ خَالِدٌ صَادِقًا | Khalid was truthful | خالدٌ: اسم كان مرفوع; صادقًا: خبر كان منصوب |

The term **اسم إنّ** means “the noun governed as the subject of إنّ”; it does not mean that **إنّ** is a noun. **إنّ** is a particle; **كان** is a verb.

We classify both patterns as **جُمْلَة اِسْمِيَّة مَنْسُوخَة**, a nominal sentence affected by a nāsikh. **خَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ** is **اسمية غير منسوخة**. Thus, for this structural classification, **كان** does not turn its underlying nominal construction into an ordinary verbal sentence.

### A conditional construction

**إِنْ تَصْدُقْ تَنْجَحْ.** — If you speak truthfully, you will succeed.

**إِنْ**, “if,” introduces a **شَرْط** (condition); **تَنْجَحْ** is its **جَوَاب** (response). The conditional construction here is verbal. The subject of each verb is understood **أنت**.

Sometimes a noun appears immediately after a conditional particle which requires a verb. The analysis then supplies an omitted verb; the visible noun does not establish a nominal foundation. For recognition:

**إِنْ ضَيْفٌ حَضَرَ أَكْرَمْتُهُ.** — If a guest comes, I will honour him.

**ضيفٌ**, “a guest,” is the subject of an understood **حضر** before it; the expressed **حضر** explains that omitted verb. In **أكرمتُه**, **تُ** means “I” and object **هُ** means “him.” This example teaches the structural principle; producing this pattern is not required yet.

### A sentence can fill a role inside another sentence

**خَالِدٌ يَكْتُبُ.** — Khalid is writing.

- The outer construction is nominal: **خالدٌ + [يكتبُ]**.
- Inside **[يكتب]** is a verbal sentence: verb + understood subject **هو**, referring to Khalid.
- That entire verbal sentence supplies the outer **خبر**.

The containing sentence is **الجُمْلَة الكُبْرَى** (*al-jumlah al-kubrā*). The sentence occupying a word-like position inside it is **الجُمْلَة الصُّغْرَى** (*al-jumlah al-ṣughrā*). These are relative labels about containment, not sentence length.

Do not give **خالد** two direct roles. In **يَكْتُبُ خَالِدٌ**, it is **فاعل**. In **خَالِدٌ يَكْتُبُ**, it is the outer **مبتدأ**, linked to the inner verb by its understood subject.

Read: **الجُمْلَةُ الكُبْرَى تَشْتَمِلُ عَلَى جُمْلَةٍ صُغْرَى.**

**تشتمل على** = contains; **تقع موقع** = occupies the position of.

## Lesson 5: Meaning, complete speech, and independent classifications

### Reporting and performing an utterance

A **جُمْلَة خَبَرِيَّة** (*khabariyyah*) makes an assertion that can be assessed as true or false. A **جُمْلَة إِنْشَائِيَّة** (*inshāʾiyyah*) performs something such as asking, ordering, or wishing; the utterance itself is not a truth-claim.

Do not confuse **خبرية**, a classification of the whole utterance, with **خبر**, the predicate of a nominal sentence.

**البَابُ مَفْتُوحٌ.** — The door is open: **خبرية**.

**اِفْتَحِ البَابَ.** — Open the door: **إنشائية**. The command is **اِفْتَحْ**; its final consonant takes a connecting kasrah before **الـ** in continuous pronunciation. That kasrah is not genitive case.

Recognise these uses of **إنشاء**. The examples establish meaning; their specialised word-by-word analyses are not required here.

| Use | Example and meaning |
|---|---|
| اِسْتِفْهَام — questioning | هَلْ حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ؟ — Has Khalid arrived? |
| تَمَنٍّ — wishing | لَيْتَ خَالِدًا حَاضِرٌ! — If only Khalid were present! |
| نِدَاء — calling | يَا خَالِدُ! — O Khalid! |
| تَحْذِير — warning | النَّارَ! — Beware the fire! |
| إِغْرَاء — urging someone towards something | العِلْمَ العِلْمَ! — Pursue knowledge! |
| أَمْر — command | اُكْتُبْ! — Write! |
| نَهْي — prohibition | لَا تَكْذِبْ! — Do not lie! |
| دُعَاء — supplication or invocation | غَفَرَ اللهُ لَكَ. — May Allah forgive you. |
| مَدْح وَذَمّ — praise and blame | نِعْمَ الخُلُقُ الصِّدْقُ! — What an excellent character trait truthfulness is!; بِئْسَ الخُلُقُ الكَذِبُ! — What an evil character trait lying is! |
| تَوَجُّع وَتَفَجُّع — pain and grief | يَا حَسْرَتَا! — Alas! |
| قَسَم — oath | وَاللهِ! — By Allah! |

Form and intended use can differ. **غَفَرَ اللهُ لَكَ** uses a past verbal form but, as a prayer, does not report a past event. Conversely, a command-form may convey a warning about what will happen rather than an instruction to be carried out. Interpret the utterance in context. **الحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ**, “Praise belongs to Allah,” can state a fact or perform praise.

### A sentence is not always complete speech

**كَلَام** (*kalām*) is **قَوْل مُفِيد**: an utterance complete enough for the listener not to need an essential continuation.

- **صَدَقْتَ.** — You spoke truthfully. A sentence and complete speech.
- **إِنْ تَصْدُقْ…** — If you speak truthfully… A predication occurs, but the condition needs its response.
- **إِنْ تَصْدُقْ تَنْجَحْ.** — If you speak truthfully, you will succeed. Complete speech.

Every **كلام** contains sentence structure, but not every **جملة** by itself is **كلام**. Even **اُكْتُبْ** is complete: its subject **أنت** is understood.

### Affirmative and non-affirmative speech

**كَلَام مُوجَب** contains no negation, prohibition, or question. **كَلَام غَيْر مُوجَب** contains one of these: **نَفْي، نَهْي، اِسْتِفْهَام**.

These are separate classifications:

| Utterance | خبر / إنشاء | موجب / غير موجب |
|---|---|---|
| حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ — Khalid arrived | خبرية | موجب |
| مَا حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ — Khalid did not arrive | خبرية | غير موجب: negation |
| اُكْتُبْ — Write! | إنشائية | موجب |
| لَا تَكْتُبْ — Do not write! | إنشائية | غير موجب: prohibition |

Read without a word-by-word gloss: **كُلُّ كَلَامٍ جُمْلَةٌ، وَلَيْسَ كُلُّ جُمْلَةٍ كَلَامًا.** Explain the distinction with a conditional example.

## Lesson 6: Phrases and what they attach to

### Two forms of شبه الجملة

A **شِبْه جُمْلَة** (*shibh jumlah*) takes either of these forms:

| Form | Example | Components |
|---|---|---|
| جَارّ وَمَجْرُور | فِي البَيْتِ — in the house | A preposition and its governed noun |
| ظَرْف وَمُضَاف إِلَيْهِ | عِنْدَ البَابِ — by the door | An adverbial noun and its iḍāfah complement |

Common adverbial nouns include **فَوْقَ** (above), **تَحْتَ** (below), and **عِنْدَ** (at/by). In these adverbial uses they are accusative; their following iḍāfah noun is genitive. **عند** is an **اسم**, whereas **في** is a **حرف**.

An ordinary iḍāfah such as **كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ** is not automatically a **شبه جملة**. In the second pattern above, the first noun is specifically adverbial.

**ظَرْف** (*ẓarf*) can mean an adverbial role (**مفعول فيه**), an adverbial word such as **فوق**, or, more broadly, a **شبه جملة**. In that broad use it includes prepositional phrases whose meaning is not literal time or place.

### Ask what the phrase completes

In **جَلَسَ خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ**, “Khalid sat in the house,” the phrase answers “Where did he sit?” It attaches to **جلس**.

- **فِي البَيْتِ** is the **مُتَعَلِّق** (*mutaʿalliq*), the attached phrase.
- **جَلَسَ** is its **مُتَعَلَّق** (*mutaʿallaq*), what it attaches to: its governing element.

The vowel difference in these two technical terms matters. In a running analysis, say **مُتَعَلِّقٌ بِـ…**, “attached to…”

Also distinguish two levels: **في** governs the noun **البيتِ** internally; the whole phrase attaches to **جلس** in the sentence.

### Expressed or understood government

| Type | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| الظَّرْف اللَّغْو (*al-ẓarf al-laghw*) | Its governor is expressed, or a particular governor is omitted but recoverable | جَلَسَ خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ: attached to جلس |
| الظَّرْف المُسْتَقَرّ فِيه (*al-ẓarf al-mustaqarr fīh*), shortened to الظرف المستقرّ | Its governor is omitted and has the general sense of being, existing, or being situated | خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ: “Khalid is in the house” |

**لَغْو** here is a technical label, not “meaningless.” A specifically understood action differs from general existence:

- Asked **أَيْنَ جَلَسَ خَالِدٌ؟**, “Where did Khalid sit?”, the answer **فِي البَيْتِ** supplies the understood particular action **جلس**: **لغو**.
- In **خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ**, no particular action such as sitting is stated or required. Understand **كَائِنٌ** or **مُسْتَقِرٌّ**, “being/situated,” or a corresponding verb such as **استقرّ**: **مستقرّ**.

Thus an omitted governor does not by itself make a phrase **مستقرّ**. The kind of meaning supplied is decisive.

### Worked tarkīb

**القَلَمُ فَوْقَ الكِتَابِ.** — The pen is above the book.

1. **القلمُ**: مبتدأ مرفوع بالضمة.
2. **فوقَ**: ظرف منصوب بالفتحة، وهو مضاف.
3. **الكتابِ**: مضاف إليه مجرور بالكسرة.
4. **فوق الكتاب**: شبه جملة متعلق بمحذوف عام، تقديره **كائنٌ**. It supplies the predicate position: **في محل رفع خبر**.
5. The sentence is nominal. The phrase is **ظرف مستقرّ**.

The understood predicate also carries the subject-link **هو**, referring to the pen. This is why such a phrase can perform work comparable to a sentence. The understood governor and its pronoun are included in the analysis; they are not extra words to insert into the original sentence.

## Lesson 7: The roles of an implied-predicate phrase

### Recognise the role before supplying the implied word

A **ظرف مستقرّ** can occur as a predicate, a circumstance, a description, a relative connection, or in the dependent construction called **اعتماد**. Learn the role names through these limited patterns.

**مَعْرِفَة** means definite: here, a personal name or a noun with **أل**. **نَكِرَة** means indefinite, such as **رَجُلٌ**, “a man.”

| Role | Example | Analysis of the phrase |
|---|---|---|
| خَبَر — predicate | الوَلَدُ فِي البَيْتِ — The boy is in the house | It tells where the boy is; understood كائنٌ |
| حَال — circumstance of a participant | رَأَيْتُ خَالِدًا فِي السُّوقِ — I saw Khalid while he was in the market | With this intended meaning, it describes Khalid’s circumstance; understood كائنًا |
| نَعْت — description of a noun | رَأَيْتُ رَجُلًا فِي السُّوقِ — I saw a man who was in the market | With this intended meaning, it describes رجلًا; understood كائنًا |
| صِلَة — relative connection | جَاءَ الَّذِي فِي البَيْتِ — The one who is in the house came | الذي means “the one who”; the phrase supplies its relative connection |

For **حال**, learn **ذُو الحَال / صَاحِب الحَال**, the participant whose circumstance is described. For **نعت**, learn **مَنْعُوت / مَوْصُوف**, the noun described. A **حال** is accusative; a **نعت** follows its noun’s case. The phrase examples have a grammatical position corresponding to that role, while their internal nouns retain the case required inside the phrase.

The two **رأيت** examples also show why meaning matters. “I saw in the market” could attach the phrase to the actual seeing, making it **لغو**. “Khalid/a man situated in the market” gives the implied-predicate reading shown in the table. Do not choose the attachment only by proximity.

In a **صلة**, supply a **verb**, such as **استقرّ**, rather than only the descriptive noun **مستقرّ**: the relative connection requires sentence structure. Elsewhere the general omitted governor may be a suitable verb or a noun with verbal meaning, such as **كائن، موجود، ثابت، مستقرّ**.

The predicate use includes predicates affected by a **ناسخ**:

- **إِنَّ الوَلَدَ فِي البَيْتِ**: the phrase occupies **رفع خبر إنّ**.
- **كَانَ الوَلَدُ فِي البَيْتِ**: it occupies **نصب خبر كان**.

This does not alter **البيتِ**, which remains genitive after **في**.

A phrase can also fill a position that originates as a predicate: **ظَنَنْتُ خَالِدًا فِي البَيْتِ**, “I thought Khalid was in the house.” Here **خالدًا** is the first object of **ظننتُ**, and **في البيت** occupies the second-object position. Their underlying relationship is still “Khalid is in the house.” Recognise this extension; the full two-object pattern will be developed later.

### اعتماد: a phrase followed by a nominative noun

**أَفِي البَيْتِ رَجُلٌ؟** — Is there a man in the house?

The **أَ** asks a question. The phrase **في البيت** relies on that questioning context; this is **اِعْتِمَاد** (*iʿtimād*). Two analyses are possible:

1. Supply the general verb **استقرّ**: **رجلٌ** is the **فاعل** of that understood verb. The governing action belongs to the implied element.
2. Take **في البيت** as a fronted predicate, **خبر مقدّم**, and **رجلٌ** as a delayed subject, **مبتدأ مؤخّر**.

An adjectival estimate, such as **مستقرّ**, also permits the technical analysis **مبتدأ مكتفٍ بمرفوعه**: a subject whose raised dependent supplies what is needed without a separate predicate. For now, explain the first or second analysis consistently.

The contexts supporting **اعتماد** are a question, negation, or a preceding relative, described noun, circumstance-holder, or predicate-holder. Recognise the pattern in each:

| Supporting context | Example; the later nominative noun |
|---|---|
| اِسْتِفْهَام | أَفِي البَيْتِ رَجُلٌ؟ — Is there a man in the house?; رجلٌ |
| نَفْي | مَا فِي البَيْتِ أَحَدٌ — There is no one in the house; أحدٌ |
| مَوْصُول | جَاءَ الَّذِي عِنْدَهُ كِتَابٌ — The one who has a book came; كتابٌ |
| مَوْصُوف | رَأَيْتُ رَجُلًا عِنْدَهُ كِتَابٌ — I saw a man who has a book; كتابٌ |
| ذُو حَال | جَاءَ خَالِدٌ مَعَهُ كِتَابٌ — Khalid came with a book; كتابٌ |
| صَاحِب خَبَر | خَالِدٌ عِنْدَهُ كِتَابٌ — Khalid has a book; كتابٌ |

Here **ـهُ** means “him/his”; **عنده** literally means “at/with him,” and **معه** means “with him.” In the relative example, the pronoun refers back to **الذي**. In the other examples it refers to the preceding person. These links make the relationships explicit.

### Particular omitted governors: recognition notes

A **ظرف لغو** normally has an expressed governor. Some established constructions require its omission. Recognise the following; do not extend them as unrestricted patterns:

- **Proverbial expressions:** **الكِلَابَ عَلَى البَقَرِ**, understood with **أَرْسِلْ**, “set the dogs upon the cattle.”
- **Established expressions of devotion:** **بِأَبِي أَنْتَ وَأُمِّي**, expressing “May you be ransomed with my father and mother”; the particular sense **مَفْدِيٌّ**, “ransomed,” is understood.
- **Oaths introduced with وَ or تَ:** in **وَاللهِ** or **تَاللهِ**, the oath-action **أُقْسِمُ**, “I swear,” is omitted. With oath **بِـ**, it may be expressed or omitted.
- **لَام الجُحُود:** the lām in patterns such as **مَا كَانَ خَالِدٌ لِيَكْذِبَ**, “Khalid would not lie.” When the lām is analysed as an original preposition, its governor is a necessarily omitted particular predicate such as **قَاصِدًا**, “intending.” An alternative analysis treats that lām as additional and does not posit this attachment. Detailed analysis of the construction comes later.

The practical distinction remains: omitted **particular action** gives **لغو**; omitted **general existence** gives **مستقرّ**.

## Lesson 8: Governing and being governed

### Three connected terms

- **عَامِل** (*ʿāmil*): a governing element which requires a grammatical status.
- **مَعْمُول** (*maʿmūl*): the element receiving that status.
- **إِعْرَاب** (*iʿrāb*): the effect of government at a word’s ending, or in its grammatical position when an ending cannot show it.

**لَنْ يَكْتُبَ خَالِدٌ.** — Khalid will not write.

For the verb’s ending, **لن** is the **عامل** and **يكتب** its **معمول**. The resulting status is **نصب**, shown by fatḥah.

For the subject, **يكتب** is the governor and **خالد** its governed **فاعل**, in **رفع**. An element may govern something while itself being governed.

### Governing and non-governing elements

All verbs belong to the governing class. Some nouns and some particles govern; others do not. A noun in iḍāfah governs its following genitive: **كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ**. **في، إنّ، لن** are governing particles.

In **مَا حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ**, **ما** negates but does not impose a case or mood on **حضر**. Here it is **غَيْر عَامِل**, also called **عَاطِل** or **مُهْمَل**. This classification applies to **ما** in this use, not automatically to every occurrence of the written form.

Whole sentences and **شبه الجملة** are not independent governors in this scheme. If a phrase is said to raise a following noun in **اعتماد**, the precise governing element is its understood governor.

Sometimes government is structural rather than an expressed word: **عَامِل مَعْنَوِيّ**. The basic nominal construction, **اِبْتِدَاء**, accounts for the رفع of the ordinary مبتدأ and خبر. A regular مضارع is مرفوع when free from a **ناصب** or **جازم**. You need not invent an omitted particle to explain these endings.

### Verb mood: the small pattern needed here

For these regular مضارع forms ending in a sound consonant, with no attached ending changing the pattern:

| Expression | Mood | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| يَكْتُبُ | مَرْفُوع | ضَمَّة |
| لَنْ يَكْتُبَ | مَنْصُوب | فَتْحَة |
| لَمْ يَكْتُبْ | مَجْزُوم | سُكُون: absence of a final short vowel |
| أَنْ يَكْتُبَ | مَنْصُوب | فَتْحَة after مصدرية أن |
| لَا تَكْتُبْ | مَجْزُوم | سُكُون after prohibitive لا |

**نَاصِب** means a governor of **نصب**; **جَازِم** means a governor of **جَزْم** (*jazm*, jussive mood). Nouns have **رفع، نصب، جرّ**; inflected verbs have **رفع، نصب، جزم**. A verb is not genitive, and a noun is not jussive.

Compare **لَا تَكْتُبُ**, “you do not write,” with **لَا تَكْتُبْ**, “do not write.” The first **لا** merely negates in this use; the second prohibits and governs jussive mood. The ending helps reveal the intended grammar.

### What can receive a grammatical position?

| Element | Governed status |
|---|---|
| Nouns | They receive grammatical roles, except **أسماء الأفعال** |
| مضارع verbs | They receive رفع, نصب, or جزم, visibly or otherwise |
| Past verbs | They can occupy نصب or جزم positions when an appropriate governing element precedes them; their form remains fixed |
| Imperatives; ordinary ungoverned past verbs | The verb itself has no case/mood position |
| Particles | They never receive case/mood positions themselves |
| Sentences | Some have a grammatical position; others do not |
| شبه الجملة | They are treated as governed units through their attachment or sentence role |

An **اِسْم فِعْل** is a noun standing for a verb, such as **صَهْ**, “be silent!” It governs like its verb but does not itself receive a case position. Recognition of this exception is enough here.

Do not confuse **غير عامل** with **غير معمول**. **لن** governs the next verb but is not itself governed. A noun can receive a role without governing another word.

### Worked analysis

**لَنْ يَفْتَحَ خَالِدٌ البَابَ.** — Khalid will not open the door.

1. **لن**: governing particle, **حرف نفي ونصب واستقبال** — negation, naṣb, and future reference; no case position itself.
2. **يفتحَ**: مضارع منصوب بلن، وعلامة نصبه الفتحة. It also governs its subject and object.
3. **خالدٌ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة.
4. **البابَ**: مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة.
5. The sentence is verbal, a negative report, **غير موجب**, and complete speech.

Read: **العامل يوجب حالة، والمعمول يقبلها.**

**يوجب** = requires; **يقبل** = receives/accepts; **حالة** = grammatical status. Explain both directions using **لن يفتح**.

## Lesson 9: Visible, estimated, and positional iʿrāb

### One role can be expressed in different ways

**مُعْرَب** (*muʿrab*) means grammatically inflectable. **مَبْنِيّ** (*mabnī*) means having a fixed form rather than changing its ending in response to the governors of case or mood.

Fixed form does not mean “no grammatical role.” Compare an inflectable noun with a pronoun, and distinguish the following three kinds of **إعراب**.

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| إِعْرَاب لَفْظِيّ (*lafẓī*) | The sign is expressed in pronunciation | خَالِدٌ / خَالِدًا / خَالِدٍ |
| إِعْرَاب تَقْدِيرِيّ (*taqdīrī*) | The sign is grammatically required but a feature of the ending prevents its pronunciation | الفَتَى, “the young man,” ending in long ā |
| إِعْرَاب مَحَلِّيّ (*maḥallī*) | A grammatical position is assigned, although the word or unit does not take that inflection on its ending | هُوَ as a مبتدأ; a sentence serving as خبر |

An unvowelled written word does not automatically have estimated inflection. **خالد** may simply be **خالدٌ** with the written vowel omitted. **لفظي** concerns the pronounced grammatical form.

### A. Visible inflection

**يَكْتُبُ خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ.** — Khalid writes in the house.

- **يكتبُ**: فعل مضارع مرفوع، وعلامة رفعه الضمة الظاهرة.
- **خالدٌ**: فاعل مرفوع، وعلامة رفعه الضمة الظاهرة.
- **في**: حرف جرّ، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **البيتِ**: اسم مجرور بفي، وعلامة جرّه الكسرة الظاهرة.
- **في البيت**: شبه جملة متعلق بيكتب؛ ظرف لغو.

**ظَاهِرَة** means expressed; **عَلَامَة رَفْعِهِ** means “the sign of its rafʿ.”

### B. Estimated inflection

**يَسْعَى الفَتَى إِلَى الهُدَى.** — The young man strives towards guidance.

These words end in long **ā**, written **ى**. A short case or mood vowel cannot be pronounced on that final alif: **تَعَذُّر**.

- **يسعى**: مضارع مرفوع بضمة مقدرة على الألف؛ منع من ظهورها التعذر.
- **الفتى**: فاعل مرفوع بضمة مقدرة على الألف؛ منع من ظهورها التعذر.
- **إلى**: حرف جرّ، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **الهدى**: اسم مجرور بإلى بكسرة مقدرة على الألف؛ منع من ظهورها التعذر.
- **إلى الهدى**: متعلق بيسعى؛ ظرف لغو.

**مُقَدَّرَة** = estimated; **مَنَعَ مِنْ ظُهُورِهَا** = prevented its appearance. The unchanged shape of **الفتى** does not make it **مبنيّ**: its case is inflected by estimation.

For recognition, **الفتى** also has an estimated fatḥah when it is an object: **رَأَيْتُ الفَتَى**, “I saw the young man.” Other reasons for estimated endings exist; the exercises here use final alif only.

### C. Positional inflection

**هُوَ يَكْتُبُ.** — He is writing.

- **هو**: ضمير منفصل مبني على الفتح في محل رفع مبتدأ.
- **يكتبُ**: فعل مضارع مرفوع بالضمة الظاهرة.
- Its subject: **ضمير مستتر تقديره هو في محل رفع فاعل**.
- **جملة يكتب**: **في محل رفع خبر**.

The expressed final vowel of **هو** belongs to its fixed form, **بناء**. It is not an accusative ending. Its sentence position is nominative. The inner sentence likewise occupies a nominative position as a whole; its own words keep their individual analyses.

Compare **هُوَ**, **بِهِ** (with him), and the object **ـهُ** in **أَكْرَمْتُهُ** (I honoured him): pronouns receive different roles without ordinary noun case endings.

### A fixed verb can also occupy a mood position

Learn one further pronoun: **نُون النِّسْوَة**, the attached subject **نَ** for a female plural group.

**يَكْتُبْنَ** — they, female, write.

The verb is **مبني على السكون** because of the attached **نون النسوة**. The nūn is a pronoun **في محل رفع فاعل**. The verb’s mood is positional:

| Form | Analysis of the verb |
|---|---|
| يَكْتُبْنَ | مبني على السكون في محل رفع |
| لَنْ يَكْتُبْنَ | مبني على السكون في محل نصب |
| لَمْ يَكْتُبْنَ | مبني على السكون في محل جزم |

The visible final **نَ** is the subject-pronoun, not a fatḥah indicating naṣb. Do not confuse **نون النسوة** with **نون التوكيد**.

A past verb can similarly remain fixed while occupying a governed position: in **إِنْ حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ…**, **حضر** is **فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح في محل جزم فعل الشرط**. After **أن المصدرية**, a past verb can occupy a naṣb position. Neither statement requires changing the past verb’s fixed ending.

### When there is no case or mood position

Say **لَا مَحَلَّ لَهُ مِنَ الإِعْرَابِ** for a particle, an ordinary past verb not in a governed mood position, an imperative verb, or an independent sentence as a whole. This does not remove the roles of words inside the sentence.

**جَلَسَ خَالِدٌ.**

- **جلس**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **خالد**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة.
- The independent sentence: **لا محل لها من الإعراب**.

For the forms used in this module, a past verb with subject **تُ / تَ / تِ** is fixed on sukūn before that pronoun: **كَتَبْتُ**. The simple singular command **اُكْتُبْ** is also fixed on sukūn. Both have a subject; “no position” describes the verb itself, not the subject-pronoun.

### A full tarkīb method

1. Read for meaning; identify words and attached pronouns.
2. Locate the **مسند إليه** and **مسند**, including understood elements.
3. Identify the sentence’s foundation and any **ناسخ**.
4. Assign roles to the remaining nouns and units.
5. Find each governing relationship and phrase attachment.
6. Give case/mood and its sign: **ظاهر، مقدّر، في محلّ**; distinguish **بناء**.
7. State the role of any embedded sentence or phrase, then classify the whole utterance.

### Worked passage

**خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ. يَكْتُبُ الدَّرْسَ. لَمْ يَخْرُجْ.**

Khalid is in the house. He is writing the lesson. He did not go out.

**First sentence:** خالدٌ مبتدأ مرفوع بالضمة. في حرف جرّ. البيتِ مجرور بفي بالكسرة. The phrase is attached to omitted general **كائنٌ**, occupies the predicate position **في محل رفع خبر**, and is **مستقرّ**. The sentence is nominal, reportive, and **موجب**.

**Second:** يكتبُ مضارع مرفوع بالضمة. Its subject is مستتر تقديره هو, referring to Khalid, **في محل رفع فاعل**. الدرسَ مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة. The sentence is verbal, reportive, and **موجب**.

**Third:** لم حرف نفي وجزم وقلب: it negates, governs jussive mood, and gives the مضارع past-time reference. يخرجْ مضارع مجزوم بلم، وعلامة جزمه السكون. Its subject is مستتر تقديره هو, referring to Khalid. The sentence is verbal, reportive, and **غير موجب**.

Each independent sentence is complete speech and has no case position as a whole. Its nouns, verbs, and internal units still receive their own analyses.
