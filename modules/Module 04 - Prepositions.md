# Module 4: Prepositions

حُرُوفُ الجَرِّ

## Before you begin

A preposition does more than give the next noun a kasrah. It connects meanings: where an action begins, what it affects, why it happens, or how it is performed. Learn to answer three questions separately: **What does the preposition mean? What is the noun’s ending? What does the phrase do in the sentence?**

You should recognise **فعل، فاعل، مفعول به، مبتدأ، خبر، مضاف إليه**, and distinguish **رفع، نصب، جرّ**. The necessary additional terms are introduced below. Work through one lesson at a time; attempt the exercises before opening the separate answers. Later exercises supply fewer vowels and less English. Read case endings in connected speech.

## Lesson 1: The noun and the whole phrase

### Core rule and terminology

A **حَرْفُ جَرٍّ** (*ḥarf jarr*) governs one noun in **جرّ**. That noun is its **مَجْرُور** (*majrūr*); together they are **الجَارّ وَالمَجْرُور**, a **شِبْه جُمْلَة**. Alternative names are **حروف الخفض / الخوافض** and **حروف الإضافة**: they bring the preceding meaning into a relationship with the following noun. Here **إضافة** does not mean that the construction is an ordinary **مضاف ومضاف إليه** pair.

**كَتَبَ خَالِدٌ بِالقَلَمِ.** — Khalid wrote with the pen.

- **كتبَ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح.
- **خالدٌ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة.
- **بـ**: حرف جرّ؛ **القلمِ**: اسم مجرور بالباء، وعلامة جرّه الكسرة.
- **بالقلم**: جارّ ومجرور متعلّق بكتب، بمعنى الآلة. It identifies the instrument of writing.

**تَعَلُّق** (*taʿalluq*) is the connection of the phrase to a governor, its **مُتَعَلَّق**. A governor may be a verb or **شِبْه الفعل**, a noun with verbal force, such as **كاتب** “writing/writer” or **تائب** “repenting.”

### Three classes to keep distinct

| Class | Meaning and attachment |
|---|---|
| حرف جرّ أصليّ | Supplies a specific relationship and attaches to a verb or its equivalent |
| حرف جرّ شبيه بالزائد | Supplies a specific meaning, but the preposition and noun do not form a dependent complement governed by a verb or its equivalent |
| حرف جرّ زائد | Adds grammatical emphasis; it does not supply a separate dependent relationship |

“**زائد**” does not mean meaningless or a mistake. All three classes can produce a genitive form. The classification depends on the particular use, not merely the particle’s spelling.

### Meaning and grammatical role

An original prepositional phrase governed by a verb or its equivalent can express an accusative complement relationship, **منصوب محلًّا**, while its internal noun remains genitive. Name the particular relationship where the meaning supports it:

| Term | Question answered |
|---|---|
| مفعول به غير صريح / بواسطة حرف جرّ | What does the action reach through a preposition? |
| مفعول فيه غير صريح | Where or when? |
| مفعول له غير صريح | For what reason? |
| مفعول معه غير صريح | Accompanied by what? |
| حال غير صريح | In what state? |
| مفعول مطلق غير صريح | What manner or kind of the action? |
| تمييز غير صريح | In respect of what, or consisting of what? |
| آلة / عوض | By what instrument / in exchange for what? |

The instrument and exchange relationships need not be forced into one of the five familiar **مفاعيل**. **غير صريح** here marks expression through a preposition. It does not change the noun’s genitive ending. A shorter analysis, **جارّ ومجرور متعلّق بالفعل**, establishes attachment; the fuller analysis also explains meaning.

### A prerequisite for endings and attachment

An ordinary singular noun normally shows kasrah after a preposition. A pronoun is fixed: **بِهِ** = **الباء حرف جرّ، والهاء ضمير متصل مبني على الكسر في محل جرّ بالباء**. Its kasrah is a fixed vowel, not a changing case ending. Compare **بِهَا، بِكَ، بِهِمْ، بِنَا**.

With **مِنْهُ، إِلَيْهِ، عَنْهُ، عَلَيْهِ، لَهُ، فِيهِ**, identify the pronoun’s referent as well as its role. Notice **إلى → إليه** and **على → عليه** before a pronoun. A later restriction on a particular preposition may exclude pronouns.

Compare **جَلَسَ خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ** with **خَالِدٌ فِي البَيْتِ**. In the first, the phrase attaches to **جلس** and locates the sitting: **ظرف لغو**. In the second, it attaches to an omitted general expression such as **كائنٌ** and supplies **في محل رفع خبر**: **ظرف مستقرّ**. Do not give every prepositional phrase the same outer position.

Read: **الاسم مجرور، والجارّ والمجرور متعلّق بالفعل.** Explain which statement concerns a word and which concerns a relationship.

## Lesson 2: Bā and the relationship it expresses

### Core rule and vocabulary

The central meaning of **الباء** is **الإِلْصَاق**, connecting or joining one thing to another. Context makes the particular relationship clear. Do not translate every **بـ** as “with” and stop there.

| Use | Example and meaning | Phrase relationship |
|---|---|---|
| التعدية / النقل | ذَهَبْتُ بِزَيْدٍ — I took Zayd away | مفعول به غير صريح |
| الظرفية | جَلَسْتُ بِالمَسْجِدِ — I sat in the mosque | مفعول فيه للمكان |
| الظرفية | سَافَرْتُ بِاللَّيْلِ — I travelled at night | مفعول فيه للزمان |
| السببية / التعليل | عَاقَبْتُهُ بِذَنْبِهِ — I punished him because of his offence | مفعول له غير صريح |
| المصاحبة | دَخَلُوا بِالكُفْرِ — They entered accompanied by disbelief | مفعول معه غير صريح |
| الحال / المصاحبة | دَخَلَ بِسَلَامٍ — He entered in peace | حال غير صريح |
| الاستعانة | كَتَبْتُ بِالقَلَمِ — I wrote with the pen | آلة |
| المقابلة / البدل / العوض | اشْتَرَيْتُ الكِتَابَ بِدِرْهَمٍ — I bought the book for a dirham | عوض |

Two further relationships to recognise are **خُذِ الكِتَابَ بِقُوَّةٍ**, “Take the book firmly”: **بقوة** can describe the manner of taking, **مفعول مطلق غير صريح**, or the taker’s state, **حال**. In **يَمْلَأُ الخُلُقُ الحَسَنُ المِيزَانَ بِالحَسَنَاتِ**, “Good character fills the scale with good deeds,” **بالحسنات** specifies what fills it: **تمييز غير صريح**.

### Distinctions that affect analysis

**التعدية** means making an action reach an object. Compare **ذَهَبَ زَيْدٌ** with **ذَهَبْتُ بِزَيْدٍ**. The latter presents Zayd as being taken away. This **باء التعدية** can overlap with the causative pattern **أَفْعَلَ**: **أَذْهَبْتُ زَيْدًا**. Accompanying the person is common with the bā construction, but not obligatory in every use; do not turn the common implication into a universal rule.

**عوض** identifies an exchange; **سبب** identifies a cause. Naming a price does not by itself say that the purchase actually took place. Nor should **آلة** automatically be relabelled **مفعول مطلق**: the pen is what one writes with, not the writing itself.

### Worked analysis with an alternative

**دَخَلَ خَالِدٌ بِسَلَامٍ.** — Khalid entered in peace.

**دخلَ** is past; **خالدٌ** is its nominative subject. **الباء حرف جرّ أصليّ، وسلامٍ اسم مجرور بالكسرة**. The phrase describes Khalid’s state: **حال غير صريح**, explained by **مُلْتَبِسًا بسلامٍ**. Identify the person whose state is described: **صاحب الحال خالد**.

Some short phrases admit more than one supported interpretation. **فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ** can present praising as the worshipper’s state, specify the kind of glorification, or express a means of glorifying. The analysis must explain the intended meaning; the kasrah alone cannot decide it. Similarly, in **سبحانك اللهم وبحمدك**, the wāw can be additional, or coordinating with an omitted **سبّحتُ**; the bā phrase retains these possible readings.

Read: **تختلف وظيفة شبه الجملة باختلاف المعنى.** **تختلف** = differs; **باختلاف** = according to the difference in.

## Lesson 3: Min and ilā

### Beginning and endpoint

**مِنْ** expresses **ابْتِدَاء الغَايَة**, the beginning of an extent. **إِلَى** expresses **انْتِهَاء الغَايَة**, its endpoint. Although **غاية** can mean “end,” **ابتداء الغاية** names the starting limit of a journey, period, or other extent.

**سِرْتُ مِنَ البَيْتِ إِلَى المَسْجِدِ.** — I walked from the house to the mosque.

Both nouns are genitive. Both phrases attach to **سرتُ** and express place, **مفعول فيه غير صريح**; one marks the starting point and the other the destination. The fatḥah in connected **مِنَ البيت** belongs to the particle’s pronunciation, not to a noun’s case.

### More than physical movement

| Particle and relationship | Example | What to recognise |
|---|---|---|
| من with a mediated object | أَنَا تَائِبٌ مِنْ كُلِّ ذَنْبٍ — I repent of every sin | **من كل ذنب** attaches to **تائب**, a شبه فعل |
| من with time | عَمِلْتُ مِنْ أَوَّلِ النَّهَارِ — I worked from the start of the day | Beginning of a time extent |
| من with cause | هَرَبَ مِنَ الخَوْفِ — He fled because of fear | من للتعليل؛ مفعول له غير صريح |
| من with specification | تَفِيضُ أَعْيُنُهُمْ مِنَ الدَّمْعِ — Their eyes overflow with tears | من للبيان / لبيان الجنس؛ تمييز غير صريح |
| من with exchange | أَرَضِيتُمْ بِالحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا مِنَ الآخِرَةِ — Have you accepted worldly life in exchange for the hereafter? | من للبدل / المقابلة؛ عوض |
| من with an instrument | يَنْظُرُونَ مِنْ طَرْفٍ خَفِيٍّ — They look with a furtive glance | آلة |
| إلى with a mediated object | حُبِّبَ إِلَيْكُمُ الإِيمَانُ — Faith was made beloved to you | The affection is directed towards its recipients |
| إلى with time | سِرْتُ إِلَى اللَّيْلِ — I walked until night | Endpoint in time |
| إلى with accompaniment | لَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَهُمْ إِلَى أَمْوَالِكُمْ | “Do not consume their wealth together with your own”; مفعول معه غير صريح |

Here **طَرْف** concerns looking; **خفيّ** means hidden or furtive. **من** can also mean “in place of,” close to exchange: **مِنْكُمْ** in **وَلَوْ نَشَاءُ لَجَعَلْنَا مِنْكُمْ مَلَائِكَةً** means “in your place.” This **بدل** names a meaning, not the dependent noun-role also called **بدل**.

### Worked tarkīb

**أَنَا تَائِبٌ مِنْ كُلِّ ذَنْبٍ.**

1. **أنا**: ضمير منفصل في محل رفع مبتدأ.
2. **تائبٌ**: خبر مرفوع بالضمة؛ اسم فاعل, here a شبه فعل.
3. **من**: حرف جرّ أصليّ. **كلِّ**: اسم مجرور بمن، وهو مضاف.
4. **ذنبٍ**: مضاف إليه مجرور بالكسرة.
5. **من كل ذنب**: متعلّق بتائب، مفعول به غير صريح. The phrase completes “repenting”; it is not a second independent predicate.

Read: **مِن لابتداء الغاية، وإلى لانتهاء الغاية.** Do not infer merely from **إلى** whether the endpoint itself is included; the expression and context must establish that.

## Lesson 4: ʿAn and ʿalā

### Separation and being over

The central meaning of **عَنْ** is **المُجَاوَزَة**, passing beyond or separation. This may involve an action issuing from something, an agent moving away from it, or an object being removed from it. The relationship need not be physical movement.

**نَهَيْتُهُ عَنِ الشَّرِّ.** — I forbade him from evil. **الهاء** is the direct object; **عن الشر** is a mediated object relationship attached to **نهيت**.

Recognise these further uses:

- **يقبل التوبة عن عباده**: acceptance removes sins from the servants; a place-related interpretation explains the separation.
- **عَمَّا قَلِيلٍ**: “after a little while,” a time relationship. Here **عمّا = عن + ما**; **قليلٍ** remains genitive.
- **وما نحن بتاركي آلهتنا عن قولك**: “We will not abandon our gods because of your statement”; **عن قولك** expresses cause.
- **حُجَّ عَنْ أَبِيكَ**: “Perform the pilgrimage on behalf of your father”; replacement or **عوض**, not a dependent **بدل** noun.

With **بَخِلَ**, the preposition helps specify the relation: **بخل بالمال** identifies what is withheld; **بخل على شخص** typically identifies the person harmed by the withholding; **بخل عن نفسه** presents the withholding as turning benefit away from oneself.

### When ʿan is a noun

In **جِئْتُ مِنْ عَنْ يَمِينِهِ**, “I came from the side of his right,” **عن** follows **من** and is an **اسم بمعنى جانب**, not a second preposition. It is in **محل جرّ بمن**, and **يمينِ** is its genitive إضافة complement. **يمينِه** itself contains **يمين** as مضاف and **الهاء** as مضاف إليه.

### ʿAlā

**عَلَى** expresses **الاسْتِعْلَاء**, being over or upon, literally or figuratively. It can include **إلزام**, an obligation resting on someone, or **تمكّن**, being firmly established in something.

| Example | Phrase relationship |
|---|---|
| أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ — You bestowed favour on them | مفعول به غير صريح |
| جَلَسَ عَلَى السَّرِيرِ — He sat on the bed | مفعول فيه للمكان |
| دَخَلَ عَلَى حِينِ غَفْلَةٍ — He entered at a time of inattention | مفعول فيه للزمان |
| لِتُكَبِّرُوا اللهَ عَلَى مَا هَدَاكُمْ — To magnify Allah for guiding you | مفعول له غير صريح |
| لَهُ عَلَيَّ أَلْفُ دِرْهَمٍ — I owe him a thousand dirhams | An obligation resting on the speaker |

### Worked distinction

In **يُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَى حُبِّهِ**, the pronoun can refer to the food: they give it while loving it, a relationship of accompaniment, **مفعول معه غير صريح**. If the pronoun refers to Allah, the phrase expresses giving because of love for Him, **مفعول له غير صريح**. Resolving the pronoun changes the analysis.

Similarly, **على خوفٍ** with believing can express a cause, accompaniment, or the believers’ state, **حال**. State the intended reading rather than claiming that one preposition has one role in every sentence.

Read: **تكون عن اسمًا بمعنى جانب إذا سُبقت بمِن.** **سُبقت** = was preceded.

## Lesson 5: Lām, fī, and kāf

### Lām and its meanings

**اللام** expresses **تخصيص / استحقاق**, allocation or entitlement; **ملك**, ownership; or **شبه ملك / نسبة**, an associated relationship resembling ownership.

Compare **الحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ** “Praise belongs to Allah,” **الكِتَابُ لِخَالِدٍ** “The book belongs to Khalid,” and **فِي الآيَةِ عِبْرَةٌ لِلْمُؤْمِنِ** “In the sign is a lesson for the believer.” Entitlement connects an abstract thing with its recipient; ownership can connect an entity with its owner; association need not be literal possession.

| Use | Example | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient | وَهَبْتُ لِخَالِدٍ كِتَابًا — I gave Khalid a book | مفعول به غير صريح |
| Position | دَعَا لِجَنْبِهِ — He prayed lying on his side | Place relationship; the posture also explains a حال meaning |
| Time | لِيَوْمِ القِيَامَةِ — for the Day of Resurrection | Time relationship in its governing context |
| Intended purpose | جِئْتُ لِلتَّعَلُّمِ — I came to learn | مفعول له غير صريح؛ لام التعليل / لام كي |

A purpose normally reflects the agent’s intention, **قصد الفاعل**. An outcome can instead be unintended: **لام العاقبة / الصيرورة / المآل**. In **فالتقطه آل فرعون ليكون لهم عدوًّا وحزنًا**, the taking-in resulted in an enemy and sorrow; that was not their intended purpose. Recognise this distinction without yet deriving the rules for **أنْ** and a following verb.

After saying or its equivalent, **لام التبليغ** identifies the addressee: **قُلْتُ لَهُ: اجْلِسْ**. Do not assume every lām gives a reason.

### Fī

**فِي** expresses **الظَّرْفِيَّة**, one thing being contained in another. This can be literal, temporal, or abstract.

| Example | Relationship |
|---|---|
| جَلَسْتُ فِي البَيْتِ | Place: مفعول فيه غير صريح |
| سَافَرْتُ فِي اللَّيْلِ | Time: مفعول فيه غير صريح |
| يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الخَيْرَاتِ | Mediated object relationship: striving within good deeds |
| لُمْتُهُ فِي هَذَا الأَمْرِ | Cause: I blamed him because of this matter |
| فَادْخُلِي فِي عِبَادِي | Accompaniment: enter among My servants |

**في** also expresses **المقايسة**, comparison: in **متاع الحياة الدنيا في الآخرة قليل**, worldly enjoyment is little compared with the hereafter. It stands between what is less and what is superior in the comparison; ordinary physical containment is not intended.

### Kāf

**الكاف** expresses **التَّشْبِيه**, resemblance. Its complement in this prepositional use must be an **اسم ظاهر**, an overt noun, rather than an attached pronoun.

**كَتَبْتُ كَالمُعَلِّمِ.** — I wrote like the teacher. **كالمعلم** attaches to **كتبت** and specifies the manner of writing, **مفعول مطلق غير صريح**.

In **واذكروه كما هداكم**, the kāf can express cause, “remember Him for guiding you,” **مفعول له غير صريح**, or comparison. Here **ما هداكم** supplies a noun-equivalent, “His guiding you”; it is not an attached pronoun after kāf.

Read: **لام التعليل لقصد الفاعل، وقد تكون اللام للعاقبة من غير قصد.**

## Lesson 6: Ḥattā and limits

### Core rule

Prepositional **حَتَّى** expresses **انتهاء الغاية**. Its complement must be an **اسم ظاهر**. With a stretch of time or place, the complement is its final part or something joined to its end.

- **قُمْتُ اللَّيْلَ حَتَّى آخِرِهِ** — I stood in prayer during the night up to its end: the last part of the period.
- **قُمْتُ اللَّيْلَ حَتَّى الصَّبَاحِ** — I stood in prayer during the night until morning: what meets its end.
- For “until half of it,” use **إِلَى نِصْفِهِ**, not **حتى نصفه** in this construction.

Both **إلى** and **حتى** mark endpoints, but **حتى** has this extra restriction. **حتى آخرِه** is allowed: its directly governed noun is **آخر**, and the pronoun belongs to that noun’s إضافة.

### Rank and purpose

When the complement is a single noun denoting neither time nor place, it may be an extreme in rank, **غاية في الرتبة**:

- **مَاتَ النَّاسُ حَتَّى الأَنْبِيَاءِ** — People died, even the prophets: an extreme of exalted rank, **تعظيم**.
- **رَزَقَ اللهُ حَتَّى الظَّالِمِينَ** — Allah provided even for wrongdoers: an extreme of low moral rank, **تحقير**.

These examples use genitive **حتى**. Other uses of the same word need their own analysis.

In **وقاتلوهم حتى لا تكون فتنة**, **حتى** introduces an endpoint with a purpose relationship, **مفعول له**. Recognise the meaning here; detailed government of following verbs belongs to the study of verb-governing particles.

### Worked tarkīb

**سِرْنَا حَتَّى البَيْتِ.** — We walked as far as the house.

**سرنا**: past verb fixed on sukūn before **نا**; **نا** is the subject in **محل رفع**. **حتى** is an original preposition; **البيتِ** is genitive with kasrah. **حتى البيت** attaches to **سرنا**, marking the endpoint in place, **مفعول فيه غير صريح**.

Read: **لا يكون ما بعد حتى إلا آخرًا لما قبلها أو متّصلًا بآخره.** **متّصل** = joined to.

## Lesson 7: Oath prepositions

### Three particles with different permissions

An oath is **قَسَم**. Its underlying verb can be **أُقْسِمُ**, “I swear.” The oath phrase is **مفعول به غير صريح** attached to that expressed or omitted verb.

| Particle | Its complement | Treatment of the oath verb |
|---|---|---|
| باء القسم | Overt noun or pronoun | May be expressed or omitted |
| واو القسم | Overt noun only | Omitted |
| تاء القسم | **الله** in the construction taught here | Omitted |

Thus **أُقْسِمُ بِاللهِ** and **بِاللهِ** are possible oath constructions; **وَاللهِ** and **تَاللهِ** require an understood oath verb. Do not insert an expressed **أقسم** before oath wāw or tāʾ. Prepositional oath tāʾ is not freely attached to other nouns.

**باء القسم** is the original oath particle and can take a pronoun, as in **بِكَ لَأَفْعَلَنَّ**, “By you, I shall certainly do it.” This is a grammatical illustration of its pronoun construction.

### Worked analysis

**وَالعَصْرِ** — By time.

- **الواو**: حرف جرّ وقسم، مبني لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **العصرِ**: اسم مجرور بالواو، وعلامة جرّه الكسرة الظاهرة.
- **والعصر**: جارّ ومجرور متعلّق بفعل قسم محذوف تقديره **أقسم**؛ مفعول به غير صريح.

The **جواب القسم**, oath response, states what the speaker affirms. Recognise it as distinct from the noun sworn by; its detailed patterns are not needed to analyse the preposition.

Read: **يجوز ذكر فعل القسم مع الباء وحذفه، ولا يُذكر مع الواو والتاء.** **ذكر / حذف** = expressing / omitting.

## Lesson 8: Mudh and mundhu

### Time and the preceding action

**مُذْ** and **مُنْذُ** express time. In their prepositional use, the complement is an overt noun denoting past or present time, not a future period or an attached pronoun.

The action must be past and either **منفيّ**, negated, or **مثبت ممتدّ**, affirmative and extended through time:

- **مَا رَأَيْتُهُ مُنْذُ يَوْمِ الجُمُعَةِ** — I have not seen him since Friday.
- **نِمْتُ مُنْذُ نِصْفِ اللَّيْلِ** — I slept from the middle of the night.

An affirmative event without duration, such as a single falling in **سقطت منذ طلوع الشمس**, does not meet this rule. Expressions such as **تجب الصلاة منذ يوم البلوغ** state an obligation applying from that starting point; the condition above governs the action whose elapsed stretch is being described.

### Meaning and preferred endings

| Time intended | Meaning | After منذ | After مذ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present period, definite | **في**: during today/tonight | جرّ obligatory | جرّ obligatory |
| Past starting point, definite | **من**: since Friday | جرّ preferred | رفع preferred |
| Elapsed duration, indefinite | **أوّل**: a span of two days | جرّ preferred | رفع preferred |

Examples: **ما رأيته منذُ اليومِ / مذُ اليومِ** “I have not seen him today”; **منذُ يومِ الجمعةِ** “since Friday”; **منذُ يومينِ** “for two days”; **مذْ يومانِ** “for two days,” with the preferred nominative after **مذ**.

**اليوم، الليلة، الشهر، السنة** and similar definite periods can mean the period containing the present moment even after a past-tense verb. Interpret the period, not just the verb’s tense.

### The small dual prerequisite

The dual, **المثنّى**, uses **ـانِ** in رفع and **ـيْنِ** in نصب and جرّ: **يومانِ / يومينِ**. Here **يومينِ** is genitive by **ياء**, not by the kasrah of its final nūn.

### Worked contrast

**مَا رَأَيْتُهُ مُنْذُ يَوْمَيْنِ.**

**ما** negates; **رأيتُ** contains the verb and subject; **ه** is the object. **منذ** is a preposition; **يومين** is genitive by yāʾ because it is dual. The phrase attaches to **رأيت**, expressing the time span, **مفعول فيه غير صريح**.

In **مَا رَأَيْتُهُ مُذْ يَوْمَانِ**, do not call **يومان** a genitive noun with an unusual sign. On the analysis used here, understand a clause such as **مذ كان يومان / مذ مضى يومان**: “since two days elapsed.” **يومان** is nominative in that understood clause.

When an actual clause follows either word, **مذ / منذ** is an **اسم مضاف**, not a preposition: **مَا اغْتَبْتُ أَحَدًا مُنْذُ عَلِمْتُ الغِيبَةَ مُحَرَّمَةً** — I have not backbitten anyone since I knew backbiting was forbidden. **منذ** is a time noun, and **علمت الغيبة محرمة** is its clause complement in **محل جرّ بالإضافة**. Internally, **الغيبةَ** and **محرّمةً** are the two objects of **علمت**.

Read: **يجب الجرّ في الحاضر، ويترجّح بعد منذ في الماضي.** **يترجّح** = is preferred; it does not mean obligatory.

## Lesson 9: Prepositions resembling additional particles

### Core rule

**الحرف الشبيه بالزائد** supplies a specific meaning but does not require a verb or شبه فعل to govern the preposition and noun as a dependent phrase. The three uses here are **رُبَّ، مِن التبعيضيّة، حاشا**. Do not label them **زائد** simply because there is no such attachment.

### Rubba

**رُبَّ** can express many or few instances, **تكثير / تقليل**; the **قرينة**, contextual indication, decides. It normally stands at the beginning of the utterance, **صدر الكلام**. An attention-giving **يا** can precede it; this use of **يا** may express regret and is not ordinary calling.

Its complement is an overt **نكرة**, indefinite noun, usually described by a word or clause:

**رُبَّ رَجُلٍ كَرِيمٍ لَقِيتُهُ.** — Many a generous man I have met; with a suitable context, “How few generous men I have met.”

**رُبَّ رَجُلٍ يَفْعَلُ الخَيْرَ لَقِيتُهُ.** — Many a man who does good I have met.

A special alternative is an initially unspecified pronoun explained by a **تمييز**: **رُبَّهُ رَجُلًا كَرِيمًا لَقِيتُهُ**. The explaining noun is accusative. In this analysis the pronoun is singular masculine even when its explanation identifies feminine or plural referents; do not freely change it by ordinary agreement. Another grammatical usage permits such variation, so recognition is more important than producing new forms here.

### Worked tarkīb

**رُبَّ رَجُلٍ كَرِيمٍ لَقِيتُهُ.**

- **ربّ**: حرف جرّ شبيه بالزائد، للتكثير أو التقليل بحسب القرينة؛ لا متعلَّق له.
- **رجلٍ**: مجرور لفظًا بربّ، مرفوع محلًّا على الابتداء.
- **كريمٍ**: نعت مجرور, describing **رجل**.
- **لقيتُه**: فعل ماضٍ، والتاء فاعل، والهاء مفعول به يعود إلى الرجل. The clause supplies the predicate, **في محل رفع خبر**.

Thus the visible genitive and the noun’s sentence position are different layers of analysis.

### Forms to recognise

**ما الكافّة** can stop the government of **ربّ**: **رُبَّمَا يَصُومُ زَيْدٌ / رُبَّمَا زَيْدٌ يَصُومُ**. It may then precede a verb or a definite noun. **زيدٌ** is not genitive. The intended frequency concerns the action; reduction is common, but contextual abundance remains possible. The bāʾ of **رُبَّ** can also be lightened: **رُبَمَا**.

**ربّ** may be omitted while its genitive effect remains, often after wāw and less often after fāʾ. In **وَلَيْلٍ كَمَوْجِ البَحْرِ**, “And many a night like the sea’s waves,” **ليلٍ** is governed by understood **ربّ**. An alternative analysis makes the wāw itself the preposition with that meaning. This is an established literary construction, not permission to omit prepositions anywhere.

When **ما** follows **مِنْ**, **مِمَّا** can have a rubba-like force: **كان ممّا يحرّك شفتيه**, describing repeated movement of the lips. Another analysis connects **ما** with a rubba-like meaning. Recognise this established expression; do not equate every **ممّا** with “often.”

### Part and exclusion

**عِنْدِي مِنْ مَاءٍ.** — I have some water. **من** is **تبعيضيّة**, partitive, classified here as **شبيهة بالزائد**. Its meaning is “some of”; it is not the original **من** of a starting point or the additional **من** of emphasis.

**هَلَكَ النَّاسُ حَاشَا العَالِمِ.** — The people perished except the scholar. **حاشا** expresses exclusion and governs **العالمِ** in the genitive in this construction. There is a further analysis of **حاشا** as original; learn the classification used here while recognising that the label is not unanimous.

Read: **ربّ للتكثير أو التقليل، والقرينة تعيّن المراد.** **تعيّن المراد** = determines what is intended.

## Lesson 10: Additional bā and min

### Emphasis without a new dependent relationship

A **حرف جرّ زائد**, also **حرف صلة**, adds emphasis. It governs the visible genitive but does not create a new phrase attached to a verb or شبه فعل. The noun retains the position required by the sentence: **مجرور لفظًا، مرفوع أو منصوب محلًّا**.

Use two distinctions: **قياسيّ**, governed by a productive constructional rule, and **سماعيّ**, an established usage to learn as it stands. Neither term means “optional everywhere.”

### Additional bā

Recognise the regular environments:

| Environment | Example | Underlying position of the noun |
|---|---|---|
| Predicate with negative ليس | لَيْسَ زَيْدٌ بِقَائِمٍ | خبر ليس منصوب محلًّا |
| Predicate with negative ما | وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِظَلَّامٍ لِلْعَبِيدِ | Predicate position in the negative construction |
| Predicate in a question with هل | هَلْ زَيْدٌ بِقَائِمٍ؟ | خبر مرفوع محلًّا |
| Semantic emphasis with نفس | نَظَّفَ الأَمِيرُ المَسْجِدَ بِنَفْسِهِ | نفس reinforces the subject, مرفوع محلًّا |
| Subject in the exclamatory pattern أفعل به | أَسْمِعْ بِهِمْ وَأَبْصِرْ | The pronoun has the subject role of the exclamatory construction |
| مبتدأ after sudden إذا | دَخَلَ الرَّجُلُ فَإِذَا بِابْنِهِ يُصَلِّي | ابن: مبتدأ مرفوع محلًّا |

Here **إذا الفجائية** means “and suddenly.” **التوكيد المعنويّ** reinforces an identified person or thing, as “the prince himself.” These are recognition patterns; do not apply a familiar label such as instrument merely because you see bā.

Established uses also include **بِحَسْبِكَ** “It suffices you,” **حَسْبُكَ بِزَيْدٍ** “Zayd is sufficient for you,” and **كَفَى بِاللهِ شَهِيدًا** “Allah suffices as witness.” In the last, **اللهِ** is **فاعل مجرور لفظًا مرفوع محلًّا**. The rule concerns **كفى** meaning “suffice,” not **كفى** meaning “protect/spare,” as in **وكفى اللهُ المؤمنين القتالَ**.

### Worked tarkīb and prerequisite

**لَيْسَ زَيْدٌ بِقَائِمٍ.** — Zayd is not standing.

**ليس** is a fixed negative verb of the incomplete-verb group: it takes a nominative **اسم** and an accusative **خبر**. **زيدٌ اسم ليس مرفوع**. **الباء حرف جرّ زائد**. **قائمٍ خبر ليس، مجرور لفظًا بالباء، منصوب محلًّا**. There is no separate bā phrase attached to **ليس** as an original-preposition complement. Compare **ليس زيدٌ قائمًا** to see the predicate position.

### Additional min

Additional **من** occurs before the specification of declarative **كم الخبرية**: **كَمْ مِنْ آيَةٍ** = “How many a sign!” Here **كم** expresses abundance rather than asking for a number. The **تمييز** explains what is being counted. Another analysis treats **من** here as original with a mediated specification relationship.

It also occurs in **كلام غير موجب**, non-affirmative speech, before an indefinite noun: with negation or a question using **هل**. **هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ؟** — Is there any more?

**من** reinforces exhaustive scope, **التنصيص على العموم / تأكيد العموم**. A bare **ما جاءني رجلٌ** can leave a possible contrast with two men; **ما جاءني من رجلٍ** excludes any instance of the class. Negation-like interrogation can have this force too. Although another grammatical position allows additional **من** in affirmative speech, do not use that extension as the production rule here. In **يغفر لكم من ذنوبكم**, for example, a partitive analysis remains possible; an affirmative **من** is not automatically additional.

### Worked tarkīb

**هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ؟**

**هل**: حرف استفهام. **من**: حرف جرّ زائد لتأكيد العموم. **مزيدٍ**: مبتدأ مجرور لفظًا بمن، مرفوع محلًّا. Its predicate is omitted, understood as **موجودٌ**. No original-preposition attachment is required.

Read: **الجرّ لفظيّ، والمحلّ بحسب وظيفة الاسم في الجملة.** **بحسب** = according to.

### Practice

1. Complete: **قائمٍ** in **ليس زيد بقائم** is **مجرور …، منصوب …، وهو … ليس**.

## Lesson 11: Additional lām and kāf

### Four lām environments

These uses do not all add the same kind of emphasis. Identify the construction before assigning the label.

| Name | Environment and example | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| لام الجحود | After negative كان: **فما كان الله ليظلمهم** | Reinforces denial: “Allah was not to wrong them” |
| لام التبيين / البيان | Clarifies the doer: **هَيْتَ لَكَ** | Clarifies who is meant by the agent relationship |
| لام التقوية | Before a fronted governed expression: **إن كنتم للرؤيا تعبرون** | Strengthens the governor’s connection to its preceding complement |
| لام التقوية | With a complement of شبه فعل: **فعّالٌ لما يريد** | Strengthens the government of the verb-like noun |

**لام التقوية** reinforces **عمل**, grammatical government, rather than adding an independent new meaning. It is also analysed as an original preposition that strengthens a weak governing connection. Learn to recognise the construction under either label.

**لام الجحود** likewise has two analyses: one treats it as original, expressing an omitted notion such as “intending”; another treats it as additional to strengthen the negation. Use the additional classification here without assuming the alternative is an error. The detailed inflection of the following verb is a later topic.

### Lām al tabyīn needs semantic care

In **هَيْتَ لَكَ**, **هيت** is an **اسم فعل أمر**, a noun functioning as an imperative, “come forward.” Its subject is implicit **أنت**; **لك** clarifies that subject relation. Another established reading makes **هيت** equivalent to past **تهيّأتُ**, with an ordinary object-related lām phrase.

After expressions of love or hatred, lām can identify the **محبوب / مبغوض**, the one loved or hated; **إلى** can identify the **مُحِبّ / مُبْغِض**, the person loving or hating:

- **مَا أَحَبَّنِي لِرَسُولِ اللهِ**: “How great is my love for the Messenger!” The lām identifies the beloved; the speaker is the lover. This is an original lām clarifying an object relationship.
- **مَا أَحَبَّ رَسُولَ اللهِ إِلَيَّ**: “How beloved the Messenger is to me!” The pronoun after **إلى** identifies the lover; the Messenger is the beloved.

Use the stated lover–beloved relationships when analysing these established exclamatory patterns; translating word by word can reverse them.

In **تَعْسًا لَهُمْ**, “Ruin upon them,” **تعسًا** is an accusative verbal noun expressing a prayer, with an omitted verb. The lām clarifies who is the doer of that understood **تَعِسَ**. It can also follow an **اسم فعل**, as in **هَيْهَاتَ لِمَا تُوعَدُونَ**, “Far away is what you are promised.” Lām al tabyīn can enter an object relationship too, in which case it is original; the name alone does not settle its class.

### Additional kāf

In **لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ**, “Nothing is like Him,” **الكاف الزائدة** reinforces comparison, **تأكيد التشبيه**. Do not turn the apparent double comparison into a claim about a separate “likeness” to which something else is compared.

Worked analysis: **ليس** is the negative incomplete verb. **الكاف حرف جرّ زائد**. **مثلِ** is **خبر ليس مقدّم، مجرور لفظًا منصوب محلًّا**, and is مضاف; **الهاء** is in محل جرّ مضاف إليه. **شيءٌ** is **اسم ليس مؤخّر مرفوع بالضمة**.

Read: **لام التقوية لتأكيد العمل دون المعنى.** Explain the difference between strengthening government and adding a new reason or destination.

## Lesson 12: Omission, attachment, and incorporated meaning

### Restricted additional items

Some words are prepositions only in particular usages or grammatical analyses. Learn these for recognition, not unrestricted sentence production:

- **لعلّ** governs جرّ in the usage of **عقيل**: **لَعَلَّ اللهِ فَضَّلَكُمْ عَلَيْنَا**. Do not transfer this ending to ordinary **لعلّ** constructions.
- **خلا، عدا** can be analysed as exception prepositions, although the more prevalent analysis here treats them as verbs.
- **متى** means **مِن** in the usage of **هذيل**.
- **كي** can mean **لام التعليل** before interrogative **ما**: **كَيْمَهْ؟** = **لِمَهْ؟**, “Why?” The interrogative alif is removed after the preposition; pause **هاء السكت** is added when stopping. This is not a rule that every **كي** governs a following ordinary noun.

### Omitted prepositions

Preposition omission is **حذف وإيصال / اتّساع**. Where an ordinary noun becomes directly accusative after omission, it is **منصوب بنزع الخافض**. This is a restricted construction, not permission to remove any inconvenient preposition.

Omission can follow a regular pattern, **قياسًا**, before **أَنْ** or **أَنَّ**:

- **يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَنْ تُؤَدُّوا الأَمَانَاتِ**: the underlying relation is **يأمركم بأن تؤدّوا**.
- **اشْهَدُوا أَنِّي بَرِيءٌ**: compare **اشهدوا بأنّي بريء**.

Here a **مصدر مؤوّل**, noun-equivalent clause, replaces an ordinary noun complement. Do not add an accusative ending to every word inside the clause; its internal grammar remains intact.

Other omissions are established, **سماعًا**: **كَفَرُوا رَبَّهُمْ**, compared with **كَفَرُوا بِرَبِّهِمْ**. In the first, **ربَّ** is accusative through **نزع الخافض**; in the second, **ربِّ** is genitive by bā. Do not infer that every verb accepting bā can freely omit it.

### Attachment must explain different relationships

One governor does not take several independent dependents expressing the same relation except through a dependent construction such as coordination, **التبعيّة**. For “I travelled morning and evening,” use **سَافَرْتُ صَبَاحًا وَمَسَاءً**, not two separate uncoordinated time complements with that intended meaning.

Several phrases can attach to one governor when they express different relations: **يَتَوَارَى مِنَ القَوْمِ مِنْ سُوءِ مَا بُشِّرَ بِهِ**. One **من** phrase identifies those from whom he hides; the other gives the reason. Count relationships, not merely repeated particles.

### Taḍmīn

**التَّضْمِين** incorporates another verb’s meaning into the expressed verb. A **ظرف لغو** can consequently use a preposition suited to that incorporated meaning.

In **وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَهُمْ إِلَى أَمْوَالِكُمْ**, the sense includes **لا تضمّوا أموالهم إلى أموالكم**: “Do not add their wealth to yours and consume it.” **تأكلوا** carries a meaning of **تضمّوا**, which explains **إلى**. On this account, the preposition retains its own relationship while the verb includes another meaning.

An alternative account, **التَّنَاوُب**, allows one preposition to stand in another’s place: here **إلى** has the force of **مع**. Both accounts explain the same intended accompaniment. Do not invent an omitted verb whenever a familiar English translation fails; the meaning must justify the analysis.

Read: **قد يُضمَّن الفعل معنى فعل آخر، فيتعدّى بحرفه.** **يتعدّى بحرفه** = reaches its complement through that other verb’s preposition.
