# Module 2: Verbs as Governing Elements

عَمَلُ الفِعْلِ

## Before you begin

You should be able to distinguish **فعل، فاعل، مفعول به، مبتدأ، خبر**, find an attached or implicit subject, and explain **رفع، نصب، جرّ**. Recall that a noun may have a visible ending, an estimated ending, or a grammatical position: **إعراب لفظي، تقديري، محلي**.

This module develops one question: **What does this verb govern in this particular meaning?** You will distinguish types of objects, recover underlying statements, and analyse complements whose internal words do not take object endings.

Work through one lesson at a time. Attempt its exercises before consulting the separate answers. Early examples are fully vowelled; later tasks require you to supply endings. Use endings for connected speech. Vocabulary help does not replace grammatical analysis.

## Lesson 1: What a verb governs

### The core rule

All verbs are **عَامِلَة**, governing elements. A verb may govern even when its own form is fixed and it has no case or mood position.

The regular pattern is called **قِيَاسِيّ** (*qiyāsī*): it follows a consistent rule, **قَاعِدَة مُطَّرِدَة**. The verb raises its **فاعل**, or its **نائب الفاعل** in a passive construction, and assigns **نصب** to the appropriate **مفاعيل** and **حال**.

**مَفَاعِيل** is broader than “direct objects.” It includes complements such as an adverbial **مفعول فيه**. Not every accusative after a verb is a **مفعول به**.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| فِعْل لَازِم (*lāzim*) | Its action does not pass to a direct object | جَلَسَ الطَّالِبُ — The student sat |
| فِعْل مُتَعَدٍّ (*mutaʿaddin*) | Its action passes to an object | فَتَحَ الطَّالِبُ البَابَ — The student opened the door |
| فِعْل قَاصِر / غَيْر مُتَعَدٍّ | Other names for لازم | جلس |
| فِعْل وَاقِع / مُجَاوِز | Other names for متعدٍّ | فتح |

These classifications concern the **مفعول به**, not whether anything follows the subject.

**جَلَسَ الطَّالِبُ أَمَامَ المُعَلِّمِ مُتَأَدِّبًا.**

The student sat in front of the teacher, behaving respectfully.

- **الطالبُ**: فاعل مرفوع.
- **أمامَ**: ظرف مكان، مفعول فيه منصوب، وهو مضاف. **المعلمِ**: مضاف إليه مجرور.
- **أمام المعلم**: attached to **جلس**; it locates the sitting.
- **متأدبًا**: حال منصوب; it describes the student’s circumstance. The student is **صاحب الحال**.
- **جلس** remains **لازم**: neither accusative is a direct object.

A purpose can also be added: **تَبْجِيلًا لِلْعِلْمِ**, “out of reverence for knowledge.” Here **تبجيلًا** is **مفعول له**, also called **مفعول لأجله**, giving the reason for the sitting. This is another kind of **نصب**, not an extra direct object. Recognising the role is enough for now.

### A small passive prerequisite

Compare **فَتَحَ الطَّالِبُ البَابَ** with **فُتِحَ البَابُ**, “The door was opened.”

The second verb is **مَبْنِيّ لِلْمَفْعُولِ بِهِ**, also called **مَبْنِيّ لِلْمَجْهُول**, passive. The agent is not expressed; **البابُ** occupies **رفع نائب الفاعل**, the nominative role replacing the subject. It no longer has an object ending. The active counterpart is **مَبْنِيّ لِلْفَاعِل** or **معلوم**. For this regular three-consonant past form, compare **فَعَلَ → فُعِلَ**. Do not extend this vowel pattern to every verb form.

Here “**مبني للمجهول**” describes voice. “**مبني على الفتح**” describes a fixed ending. They answer different questions.

### Directly, or through a preposition

**مُتَعَدٍّ بِنَفْسِهِ** reaches its object directly. **مُتَعَدٍّ بِوَاسِطَةِ حَرْفِ جَرٍّ** reaches it through a preposition.

**أَذْهَبَ اللهُ الحُزْنَ.** — Allah removed the sorrow.

**ذَهَبَ اللهُ بِالحُزْنِ.** — Allah took away the sorrow.

In the first, **الحزنَ** is a direct object, **مفعول به صريح**. In the second, **الحزنِ** is genitive after **الباء**, and **بالحزن** attaches to **ذهب**. The object relation is mediated by the preposition: **مفعول به بواسطة حرف الجرّ**, also **مفعول به غير صريح** or **متعلّق بمعنى المفعول به**.

Keep the meaning and ending distinct: this broader use of “object” does not make **الحزنِ** accusative. Likewise, an ordinary location such as **في البيت** is not automatically an indirect object merely because it contains a preposition.

Read: **الفعل اللازم لا يتجاوز أثر فاعله إلى المفعول به.**

**يتجاوز** = passes beyond; **أثر** = effect. Explain why **أمام المعلم** does not contradict this rule.

## Lesson 2: One object, or two separate participants

### Count grammatical roles

A transitive verb may take one, two, or three **مفاعيل به**. Count roles, not just written nouns or accusative marks.

**كَتَبَ خَالِدٌ الدَّرْسَ.** — Khalid wrote the lesson: one object.

**أَعْطَى خَالِدٌ الطَّالِبَ كِتَابًا.** — Khalid gave the student a book: two objects.

In the giving construction, **الطالبَ** is **مفعول به أوّل** and **كتابًا** is **مفعول به ثانٍ**. They do not originate as a subject and predicate: “the student is a book” is not the relationship expressed.

This is **فِعْل جَارِحَة** (*fiʿl jāriḥah*), language associated with outward or sensory action. Here the decisive structural test is **لَيْسَ مَفْعُولَاهُ مُبْتَدَأً وَخَبَرًا فِي الأَصْلِ**: its two objects are not originally a subject–predicate pair.

Common verbs in this group express giving or clothing: **أَعْطَى** (gave), **مَنَحَ** (granted), **وَهَبَ** (bestowed), **كَسَا** (clothed), **أَلْبَسَ** (dressed). **سَمَّى** (named) is also included among the listed verbs. Learn each verb’s actual construction; the English translation alone does not determine it.

### Pronouns still count

**أَعْطَيْتُهُ كِتَابًا.** — I gave him a book.

| Component | Tarkīb |
|---|---|
| أعطيتُ: the verb before تُ | فعل ماضٍ مبني على السكون لاتصاله بتاء الفاعل |
| تُ | ضمير متصل مبني على الضم في محل رفع فاعل |
| هُ | ضمير متصل مبني على الضم في محل نصب مفعول به أوّل |
| كتابًا | مفعول به ثانٍ منصوب بالفتحة الظاهرة |

The past verb itself has no mood position here. Its subject is **تُ**, not **هو**. The object **هُ** is fixed, so its object status is positional, **محلي**. The whole independent utterance is a verbal sentence.

Useful object pronouns are **ـهُ** (him/it), **ـهَا** (her/it), **ـنَا** (us), and **ـكَ** (you, male singular). An attached pronoun’s role depends on its construction: **ـنا** in **هَدَانَا** is an object, while **ـنا** in **كَتَبْنَا** is the subject.

### A verb can use both routes

**هَدَى المُعَلِّمُ الطَّالِبَ الطَّرِيقَ.** — The teacher guided the student along the way.

**هَدَى المُعَلِّمُ الطَّالِبَ إِلَى الطَّرِيقِ.** — The teacher guided the student to the way.

The first has two direct objects. In the second, **الطالبَ** remains the direct object; the goal is expressed by **إلى الطريقِ**, attached to **هدى**. This is the same verb using a direct construction or a prepositional one.

In a plea for guidance, the direct wording can stress the desire to reach the goal fully; **إلى** presents it as a limit or destination and does not, by itself, settle inclusion within that limit. This is a possible meaning distinction, not a rule that every **إلى** phrase excludes its endpoint.

### Omitted objects in this group

One or both objects can be unexpressed:

- **أَعْطَيْتُ كِتَابًا.** — I gave a book: the recipient is unexpressed.
- **أَعْطَيْتُ الطَّالِبَ.** — I gave to the student: what was given is unexpressed.
- **أَعْطَيْتُ.** — I gave: both are unexpressed.

The context decides whether particular objects are understood or the speaker simply leaves them unspecified. The verb does not become intransitive merely because its objects are not written.

Read: **يجوز حذف المفعول الأوّل أو الثاني أو كليهما.**

**حذف** = omission; **كليهما** = both of them. This rule concerns the two-object group just studied; the next group needs a different restriction.

## Lesson 3: Two objects that express a statement

### The underlying subject–predicate relationship

**خَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ.** — Khalid is truthful.

**ظَنَنْتُ خَالِدًا صَادِقًا.** — I thought Khalid was truthful.

With **ظننتُ**, both members of the underlying assertion become objects:

- **تُ**: the subject who thinks.
- **خالدًا**: مفعول به أوّل منصوب.
- **صادقًا**: مفعول به ثانٍ منصوب; it says something about the first object.

The sentence attributes the judgement to the speaker; it does not independently establish that Khalid is truthful. Here **في الأصل**, “originally,” identifies the underlying grammatical relationship, not an earlier event in time.

These are **أَفْعَال القُلُوب** (*afʿāl al-qulūb*), verbs of mental judgement or cognition. **القلب** here concerns knowing, finding, or supposing.

### Learn the vocabulary by meaning

| Group | Verbs in the relevant senses |
|---|---|
| أَفْعَال اليَقِين — certainty | وَجَدَ (found to be), أَلْفَى (found), رَأَى (knew/perceived mentally), عَلِمَ (knew), عَرَفَ (recognised/knew), دَرَى (came to know), تَعَلَّمْ (know!) |
| أَفْعَال الرُّجْحَان — supposition or judgement | ظَنَّ (thought), حَسِبَ (supposed), زَعَمَ (claimed), جَعَلَ (regarded as), حَجَا (supposed), عَدَّ (reckoned), وَهَبَ (supposed), خَالَ (imagined/supposed) |

**رُجْحَان** is the leaning of a judgement towards one possibility. The last few verbs are mainly recognition vocabulary at this stage. **تَعَلَّمْ** here is the established imperative “know!”; do not automatically assign this two-object use to every form meaning “learn.”

Keep these reading distinctions:

- **علم** commonly concerns a judgement about qualities, hence its frequent two-object use. **عرف** commonly identifies an entity; its two-object cognitive use is less frequent.
- **درى** often suggests knowledge reached after ignorance through investigation or a means of discovery.
- Cognitive **وجد** and **رأى** use the language of finding and seeing for knowledge, as though the matter were directly perceived. Cognitive **حسب** connects judgement with reckoning or consideration.
- **ألفى** also has a sensory use. Its treatment as a cognitive verb is not universal; do not assume that every occurrence requires two objects.
- **زعم** often introduces a clause with **أنّ** or **أنْ**, and frequently presents an unfounded claim. Context must still establish its force.

The group labels describe characteristic uses. **ظنّ** can express conviction reached through reflection, **عِلْم نَظَرِيّ**, rather than mere uncertainty. Distinguish that from **عِلْم بَدِيهِيّ**, knowledge evident without such reasoning.

### Meaning decides the construction

| Expression | Intended meaning | Objects |
|---|---|---|
| رَأَيْتُ خَالِدًا | I saw Khalid with my eyes | One |
| رَأَيْتُ الصَّبْرَ نَافِعًا | I recognised patience as beneficial | Two; الصبرُ نافعٌ |
| وَجَدْتُ القَلَمَ | I found the pen | One |
| وَجَدْتُ العِلْمَ نَافِعًا | I found knowledge beneficial | Two; العلمُ نافعٌ |
| ظَنَنْتُ خَالِدًا | I suspected/accused Khalid | One, in this specified sense |

Likewise, **عَلِمَ** meaning “recognised/knew of” and **أَلْفَى** meaning “encountered” do not automatically take the two-object construction. A familiar spelling is not enough: identify the sense being used.

### Worked tarkīb

**عَلِمْتُ خَالِدًا فِي البَيْتِ.** — I knew Khalid was in the house.

1. **علمتُ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على السكون; **تُ** is its subject in **محل رفع**.
2. **خالدًا**: مفعول به أوّل منصوب بالفتحة.
3. **في**: حرف جرّ; **البيتِ**: اسم مجرور بفي بالكسرة.
4. **في البيت**: attached to an omitted general governor, such as **كائنًا**; **ظرف مستقرّ في محل نصب مفعول به ثانٍ**.
5. The underlying assertion is **خالدٌ في البيت**. The inner noun **البيتِ** stays genitive; the phrase as a whole supplies the second-object position.

Read: **مفعولا فعل القلب مبتدأ وخبر في الأصل.** Restate this using the worked example.

## Lesson 4: Transformation and three-object constructions

### Making something be something

**أَفْعَال التَّحْوِيل** (*afʿāl al-taḥwīl*) express making or rendering: **بِمَعْنَى صَيَّرَ**. Their two objects also have an underlying subject–predicate relationship.

**جَعَلَ النَّجَّارُ الخَشَبَ بَابًا.** — The carpenter made the wood into a door.

**النجارُ** is the subject; **الخشبَ** is the first object; **بابًا** is the second. The relationship is “the wood is a door,” presented as the result. It need not have been true before the action.

| Transformation verbs | Sense to recognise |
|---|---|
| صَيَّرَ، جَعَلَ | Made/rendered |
| رَدَّ | Turned/returned something into a state |
| تَرَكَ | Left something in a state |
| تَخِذَ، اِتَّخَذَ | Took/adopted as |
| وَهَبَ | Made/rendered, in this less common use |

These constructions belong to particular meanings. Compare:

- **تَرَكْتُ البَابَ.** — I left the door: one object.
- **تَرَكْتُ البَابَ مَفْتُوحًا.** — I left the door open: two objects in the intended transformation reading.
- **جعل** meaning “created/brought into existence” does not, merely by being **جعل**, require a second predicative object.
- **ردّ** meaning “returned to a destination,” or **اتّخذ** meaning “took/obtained,” likewise needs analysis from its actual sense.

**وهب** can express giving, supposing, or making. Ask both “How many objects?” and “What relationship holds between them?”

### Adding a recipient of knowledge

A three-object construction belongs to the cognitive type. Its **second and third** objects form the underlying subject–predicate pair.

Compare **رَأَيْتُ الأَمْرَ سَهْلًا**, “I recognised the matter as easy,” with:

**أَرَيْتُ خَالِدًا الأَمْرَ سَهْلًا.** — I showed Khalid that the matter was easy.

Here **أَرَى** has a causative cognitive sense: causing someone to perceive or recognise a judgement. Do not assume that every use of **أرى**, including physical showing, takes three objects.

| Component | Role | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| أريتُ: verb + تُ | Past verb + subject-pronoun | I caused the recognition |
| خالدًا | مفعول به أوّل | The person shown |
| الأمرَ | مفعول به ثانٍ | What the judgement concerns |
| سهلًا | مفعول به ثالث | What is predicated of الأمر |

All three objects here have visible fatḥah. The underlying statement is **الأَمْرُ سَهْلٌ**, not **خالدٌ الأمرُ**. **أريتُ** has fixed sukūn before subject **تُ**; its initial **أَ** is part of this past verb’s form, not an interrogative particle.

Read: **المفعول الثاني والثالث مبتدأ وخبر في الأصل.** Identify the verb type described.

## Lesson 5: Omission and special uses of رأى

### Omission needs evidence in a cognitive judgement

| Term | Meaning | Treatment here |
|---|---|---|
| اِخْتِصَار (*ikhtiṣār*) | Omission with evidence for what is intended | Permitted with the objects of أفعال القلوب |
| اِقْتِصَار (*iqtiṣār*) | Omission without evidence for the intended missing element | Not permitted when dropping just one member of the cognitive pair |
| دَلِيل | Evidence or a contextual clue | Enables recovery of the omitted content |
| مَحْذُوف مَنْوِيّ | An omitted element still intended | Part of the intended analysis despite being unspoken |

After discussion of whether Khalid is truthful, **ظَنَنْتُ خَالِدًا**, “I thought Khalid [truthful],” can leave **صادقًا** understood. Without evidence, it does not complete that intended judgement.

Both objects can also be omitted when the context recovers them: after **أَظَنَنْتَ خَالِدًا صَادِقًا؟**, “Did you think Khalid truthful?”, an answer **ظَنَنْتُ**, “I did think [him truthful],” recovers both objects.

Do not confuse omission with a change of meaning. **ظننتُ خالدًا** meaning “I accused/suspected Khalid” is a one-object use; it is not an incomplete two-object judgement.

### Worked analysis of a short exchange

**أَظَنَنْتَ الضَّيْفَ صَادِقًا؟ — ظَنَنْتُهُ.**

Did you think the guest truthful? — I thought him [truthful].

- In the question, **أَ** is **حرف استفهام**; **تَ** is the subject-pronoun; **الضيفَ، صادقًا** are the two objects.
- In the answer, **ظننتُ** contains the past verb and subject **تُ**; **هُ** is **في محل نصب مفعول به أوّل**, referring to the guest.
- The second object **صادقًا** is omitted. The question supplies the evidence: **حذف اختصارًا**.

The fixed ending of subject **تَ** is fatḥah; that of subject **تُ** is ḍammah. Both subjects occupy **محل رفع**.

### Reading رأى beyond physical sight

**أَلَمْ تَرَ** can mean **أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ**, “Have you not known/considered?”, carrying a sense of wonder. The language of seeing gives force to knowing. In this expression, **أَ** asks, **لم** negates and governs jussive mood, and **تَرَ** is jussive by deletion of the final weak letter of **تَرَى**. The remaining fatḥah is not naṣb.

**أَرَأَيْتَ** can mean **أَخْبِرْنِي**, “Tell me,” especially in a striking or surprising situation. A noun object may be followed by a question occupying the second-object place:

**أَرَأَيْتَ خَالِدًا أَيَصْدُقُ؟** — Tell me about Khalid: does he speak truthfully?

Here **خالدًا** is the first object. **أَيَصْدُقُ؟**, with its implicit subject **هو** referring to Khalid, is an interrogative sentence **سَادَّة مَسَدَّ المَفْعُولِ بِهِ الثَّانِي**: filling the second-object place. Context can also recover an omitted second object or both objects in this idiom.

Sometimes this idiom approaches **أمّا**, “as for…,” and its continuation uses **فـ**. Recognise that discourse use without forcing a literal visual-seeing interpretation.

### A kāf that is not an object-pronoun

In ordinary **أَرَأَيْتَ**, the subject ending changes with the addressee: **أَرَأَيْتِ** to a female, **أَرَأَيْتُمْ** to a male or mixed group.

The special emphatic-address forms **أَرَأَيْتَكَ / أَرَأَيْتَكُمْ** keep **تَ** unchanged. It is the **كاف الخطاب** ending that varies with the addressee. In this idiom the kāf is a particle reinforcing address, **حرف خطاب لا محل له من الإعراب**, not an extra object and not the subject.

Do not apply this exception to ordinary object **كَ** in **أَكْرَمْتُكَ**, “I honoured you.” There **كَ** is an object-pronoun.

### Dream-seeing: recognise two analyses

**رَأَيْتُ فِي المَنَامِ طِفْلًا يَكْتُبُ.** — I saw in a dream a child writing.

Dream-seeing admits treatment as sensory seeing or as a cognitive construction:

- On the sensory reading, **طفلًا** is the object and **يكتب** may describe that indefinite noun: **جملة في محل نصب نعت**.
- On the cognitive reading, **طفلًا** is the first object and **يكتب** fills the second-object place.

Other sensory contexts can have a **حال** rather than a **نعت**. Keep the chosen analysis and its noun relationships consistent; the word “dream” alone does not settle the analysis.

Read: **يجوز الحذف بدليل، ولا يجوز حذف أحد المفعولين بغير دليل في هذا الاستعمال.** Identify the use being restricted.

## Lesson 6: A noun-equivalent can fill both object places

### The core rule

A cognitive verb need not be followed by two separate object words. An **اِسْم مُؤَوَّل**, a noun-equivalent formed from a particle and its clause, can fill both positions together:

**عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ.** — I knew that Khalid was truthful.

**أَنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ** is **في محل نصب، سادّ مسدّ مفعولي علم**: in an accusative position, filling the place of the two objects of **علم**.

**سَدَّ مَسَدَّ** means “filled the place of.” **سَادّ** describes a masculine unit such as **المصدر**; **سَادَّة** describes a feminine unit such as **الجملة**.

### The necessary particle distinction

| Particle | Pattern | Internal government |
|---|---|---|
| أَنَّ, with shaddah | أَنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ | Its اسم is accusative; its خبر is nominative |
| أَنْ المصدرية, with sukūn | أَنْ يَكْتُبَ | Governs naṣb of the مضارع; forms a noun-equivalent with its clause |
| إِنَّ, with kasrah | إِنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ | Its اسم is accusative; its خبر is nominative; introduces an emphatic assertion |

Do not swap these merely because the letters look alike. In **علمتُ أنّ خالدًا صادقٌ**, the outer verb governs the **whole noun-equivalent**. **أنّ** governs its own اسم and خبر internally.

### Worked comparison

| Construction | Inner roles | Outer role |
|---|---|---|
| عَلِمْتُ خَالِدًا صَادِقًا | خالدًا: first object; صادقًا: second object | Two separately governed objects |
| عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ خَالِدًا صَادِقٌ | خالدًا: اسم أنّ; صادقٌ: خبر أنّ | The complete أنّ-unit fills both object places |

In the second sentence:

- **علمتُ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على السكون؛ التاء في محل رفع فاعل.
- **أنّ**: حرف مصدريّ ونصب، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **خالدًا**: اسم أنّ منصوب بالفتحة.
- **صادقٌ**: خبر أنّ مرفوع بالضمة.
- **أنّ مع اسمها وخبرها**: مصدر مؤوّل في محل نصب، سادّ مسدّ مفعولي علم.

Do not label **خالدًا** “first object of علم” here. Its ending belongs to the internal construction governed by **أنّ**.

### Recognising degrees of assertion

The following arrangements can give a typical progression from stronger to less strongly expressed certainty. It is a reading tendency, not a numerical scale or a substitute for context:

1. **علم + أنّ** and its clause.
2. **علم + أنْ المخفّفة من الثقيلة** and its clause.
3. **علم** with two separate objects.
4. **ظنّ + أنّ** and its clause.
5. **ظنّ + أنْ المخفّفة من الثقيلة** and its clause.
6. **ظنّ** with two separate objects.

**أَنْ المُخَفَّفَة مِنَ الثَّقِيلَة** is a lightened form of **أنّ**. In **عَلِمْتُ أَنْ سَيَحْضُرُ خَالِدٌ**, “I knew that Khalid would attend,” the **أنْ** is lightened; it does not make **يحضرُ** accusative. Its understood اسم is a **ضمير الشأن**, a pronoun introducing the matter to be stated, and the following sentence is its خبر. Recognise this distinction; producing the full range of lightened constructions comes later.

Read: **المصدر المؤوّل سادّ مسدّ المفعولين، ولكلماته إعرابها داخل التركيب.** Explain both levels of analysis.

## Lesson 7: تعليق — suspending direct government

### Internal grammar, external object position

**تَعْلِيق** (*taʿlīq*) suspends the cognitive verb’s **عَمَل لَفْظِيّ**, its direct effect on the following words’ endings. Its **عَمَل مَحَلِّيّ**, government of the whole complement’s position, remains.

**عَلِمْتُ أَخَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ.** — I knew whether Khalid was truthful.

The interrogative **أَ** begins the embedded question. **خالدٌ** and **صادقٌ** retain their own subject–predicate endings. The entire question fills both object places of **علمتُ**.

Say **جملة معلّق عنها العمل اللفظيّ**: a sentence from which direct government is suspended. Saying only “government is suspended” can hide the important fact that the clause still has a governed position.

### What triggers suspension?

Certain elements have **صَدْر الكَلَام**, priority at the beginning of their own construction. A preceding cognitive verb does not reach through them to change the internal words into ordinary objects.

| مُعَلِّق: suspending element | Recognition example | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| إِنْ النافية | ظَنَنْتُ إِنْ خَالِدٌ إِلَّا صَادِقٌ | “I thought Khalid was only truthful.” Here إنْ means ما, not “if”; إلا marks the restriction |
| مَا النافية | عَلِمْتُ مَا حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ | “I knew Khalid had not arrived.” ما negates the inner sentence |
| لَام الابتداء | عَلِمْتُ لَخَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ | “I knew that Khalid was certainly truthful.” The lām emphasises the assertion |
| اللَّام المُزَحْلَقَة | عَلِمْتُ إِنَّ خَالِدًا لَصَادِقٌ | “I knew that Khalid was certainly truthful.” This emphatic lām occurs later in an إنّ construction |
| لَعَلَّ | لَا أَدْرِي لَعَلَّهُ قَرِيبٌ | “I do not know; perhaps it is near.” لعلّ governs an اسم and خبر internally |
| حرف الاستفهام | عَلِمْتُ أَحَضَرَ خَالِدٌ | “I knew whether Khalid had arrived.” أَ introduces the embedded question |
| اسم الاستفهام | عَلِمْتُ مَنْ حَضَرَ | “I knew who had arrived.” من remains an interrogative noun |
| لَام جواب القسم | عَلِمْتُ لَيَحْضُرَنَّ خَالِدٌ | “I knew Khalid would certainly attend.” The lām introduces an oath-response; نّ reinforces the verb |

These are recognition patterns, not a requirement to master every particle now. In **لعلّه قريبٌ**, **هُ** occupies **نصب اسم لعلّ**, and **قريبٌ** is **خبر لعلّ مرفوع**. In the **إنّ…لـ** example, **خالدًا** is **اسم إنّ**, not a directly governed object of **علم**.

### Worked tarkīb

**عَلِمْتُ أَحَضَرَ الضَّيْفُ.** — I knew whether the guest had arrived.

1. **علمتُ**: past verb fixed on sukūn; **تُ** is its subject in **محل رفع**.
2. **أَ**: حرف استفهام، لا محل له من الإعراب; the suspending element.
3. **حضرَ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح، لا محل له من الإعراب.
4. **الضيفُ**: فاعل حضر مرفوع بالضمة; not an object of **علم**.
5. **أحضر الضيف**: جملة استفهامية في محل نصب، سادّة مسدّ مفعولي علم، معلّق عنها العمل اللفظيّ.

The whole utterance reports knowledge. The embedded interrogative supplies the matter known; the speaker is not asking the listener to provide that information. Do not classify the outer utterance solely from the embedded question.

### An interrogative noun keeps its internal role

**عَلِمْتُ أَيَّ كِتَابٍ قَرَأَ خَالِدٌ.** — I knew which book Khalid read.

**أيّ** means “which” and is **مضاف**; **كتابٍ** is **مضاف إليه**. **أيَّ** is accusative as the fronted object of **قرأ**, not as an object directly governed by **علم**. The internal verb governs that internal role despite following it. The entire question fills the two object places of **علم**.

Thus تعليق does not mean “every word in the embedded clause must be nominative.” It means that the clause’s own grammar determines those words’ roles and endings.

### The extension to similar verbs

Suspension also occurs with **فعل شبيه بفعل القلب**: a verb related to knowing, reporting, asking, or testing. An interrogative complement after a verb requires this cognitive or related sense; do not attach the pattern to any verb at random.

**سَأَلْتُ خَالِدًا أَحَضَرَ الضَّيْفُ.** — I asked Khalid whether the guest had arrived.

**خالدًا** is the person asked, an expressed object. The embedded question supplies the content asked about, filling the second-object place. The first object is not part of that question.

A verb taking one object may also be suspended before an interrogative complement, as with **نَظَرَ** in the sense “examined/considered.” In that construction the whole question supplies **one** object position. Do not automatically say **سدّت مسدّ المفعولين** after every suspended verb; determine what that particular verb requires.

Read: **في التعليق يُبطَل العمل اللفظيّ ويبقى العمل المحليّ.** Explain using one example.

## Lesson 8: إلغاء — cancelling the object government

### The core rule

**إِلْغَاء** (*ilghāʾ*) cancels a cognitive verb’s object government both **لَفْظًا وَمَحَلًّا**: in the individual endings and in the grammatical position of the statement.

**خَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ ظَنَنْتُ.** — Khalid is truthful, I thought.

Here **خالدٌ صادقٌ** remains an ordinary nominal statement. With this **إلغاء** analysis, it is not a complement in an accusative position governed by **ظننتُ**. The verb still has its subject **تُ**; “cancelled” concerns its government of the object pair.

### Position and preference

| Position of the cognitive verb | Pattern | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Before the pair | ظَنَنْتُ خَالِدًا صَادِقًا | Ordinary إعمال: both objects accusative |
| Between the pair | خَالِدًا ظَنَنْتُ صَادِقًا | إعمال is preferred; the fronted first object stays accusative |
| Between the pair | خَالِدٌ ظَنَنْتُ صَادِقٌ | إلغاء is also possible: مبتدأ and خبر |
| After the pair | خَالِدٌ صَادِقٌ ظَنَنْتُ | إلغاء is preferred |
| After the pair | خَالِدًا صَادِقًا ظَنَنْتُ | إعمال is also possible: two fronted objects |

**إِعْمَال** means allowing the verb to govern. **يَتَوَسَّط** = comes between; **يَتَأَخَّر** = comes after; **يَتَرَجَّح** = is preferred. A preference is not an obligation. Do not use these alternatives to cancel an ordinary initial **ظننتُ** arbitrarily.

### Worked comparison

**خَالِدًا ظَنَنْتُ صَادِقًا.**

- **خالدًا**: مفعول به أوّل مقدّم، منصوب بالفتحة؛ عامله ظننتُ.
- **ظننتُ**: past verb, with **تُ** in **محل رفع فاعل**.
- **صادقًا**: مفعول به ثانٍ منصوب بالفتحة.
- This is **إعمال**. The fronted noun does not make the construction nominal.

**خَالِدٌ ظَنَنْتُ صَادِقٌ.**

- **خالدٌ**: مبتدأ مرفوع بالضمة.
- **ظننتُ**: verb and subject, parenthetically inserted between the two pillars; its sentence has no case position here.
- **صادقٌ**: خبر مرفوع بالضمة.
- This is **إلغاء**. The containing statement is nominal; it has no object-position imposed by **ظننتُ**.

### Keep the three treatments distinct

| Treatment | Government of separate object words | Government of the clause’s position |
|---|---|---|
| إعمال in ظننتُ خالدًا صادقًا | Present: two accusatives | No replacement clause in this example |
| تعليق in علمتُ أخالدٌ صادقٌ | Suspended; inner grammar remains | Present: the question occupies نصب and fills both places |
| إلغاء in خالدٌ صادقٌ ظننتُ | Cancelled | Cancelled: the statement is not an accusative complement of ظننتُ |

Read without a gloss: **التعليق إبطال العمل لفظًا لا محلًّا، والإلغاء إبطاله لفظًا ومحلًّا.** Illustrate the difference with two short analyses.

Choices: **علمت العلم نافعا**; **علمت أن العلم نافع**; **العلم نافع علمت**.

### E. Final transfer task

**E1.** Analyse this unfamiliar sentence fully:

**أريت المسافر الطريق آمنا.**

Intended meaning: “I showed the traveller that the route was safe.” **مُسَافِر** = traveller; **آمِن** = safe. Read the verb as the cognitive causative **أَرَيْتُ**.

Add full vowels. Identify all grammatical components, state which pair forms the underlying assertion, and explain every object ending. Does **آمنًا** function as a حال or as a third object in this intended construction?

**E2.** Express the following meanings in Arabic, then give the critical tarkīb distinction:

1. I knew that the route was safe — use **أنّ**.
2. I knew whether the route was safe — use interrogative **هل**.
3. The route is safe, I thought — place **ظننتُ** last and use **إلغاء**.

### Ready to continue?

You are ready when you can justify object counts from meaning and relationships, distinguish direct objects from other accusatives, recover the correct underlying statement, and keep a complement’s external position separate from its internal grammar. You should also be able to explain **إعمال، تعليق، إلغاء** in a few words of Arabic.
