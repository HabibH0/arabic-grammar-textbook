# Module 7: Particles without Government

الحُرُوفُ غَيْرُ العَامِلَةِ

## Before you begin

A **حرف غير عامل** adds meaning or connects expressions without itself assigning رفع، نصب، جرّ، or جزم. It may still change how a sentence must be understood. Its function depends on its use: **لا** can negate a statement, prohibit an action, coordinate alternatives, or reinforce negation.

Recall these relationships:

- **فاعل مرفوع**: the verb's subject is nominative.
- **مفعول به منصوب**: its direct object is accusative.
- **مبتدأ وخبر مرفوعان**: the basic nominal subject and predicate are nominative.
- **إنَّ** assigns نصب to its noun and رفع to its predicate; a preposition assigns جرّ.
- An ordinary مضارع is مرفوع unless a governing construction requires نصب or جزم. A past verb has a fixed ending, **بناء**.
- A pronoun has a grammatical position, **محلّ**, rather than a changeable case ending: **كتبتُهُ** contains subject **تُ** and object **هُ**.

In tarkīb, identify the particle's **meaning**, its **relationship to the sentence**, and the **actual reason for each ending**. “It comes after a particle” is not a reason for نصب or جزم. Particles themselves are **مبنية، لا محل لها من الإعراب**, whether they govern or not.

Connected pronunciation can introduce an incidental vowel: **لم يكتبِ الطالبُ** still has a jussive verb. Its sukūn is estimated, **مقدّر**, because a kasrah is pronounced to avoid two successive vowelless consonants before الـ. Distinguish this from a vowel that marks grammatical inflection.

Work through one lesson at a time. **G** gives guided practice; **I** asks for independent application; **R** revisits earlier learning. Use the separate answers after attempting the work. Supply endings for connected reading unless a task explicitly asks about pausing.

## Lesson 1: Joining expressions — و، ف، ثم، حتى

### Core rule and terminology

In **جَاءَ خَالِدٌ وَسَعِيدٌ**, “Khalid and Saʿid came,” **خالد** is **المعطوف عليه**, the expression being joined to; **سعيد** is **المعطوف**, the joined expression; **و** is **حرف عطف**. A coordinated noun follows the case of the noun to which it is joined. Conjunctions can also join clauses.

| Particle | Meaning | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| و | **مطلق الجمع**: simple joining | Neither simultaneity nor order |
| ف | **الترتيب والتعقيب**: order and close succession | Not necessarily an instantaneous interval |
| ثم | **الترتيب والتراخي**: order with an interval | Not always a chronological sequence when joining clauses |
| حتى | **الغاية والتدريج**: reaching an endpoint or extreme | Not chronological order by itself |

**قَرَأْتُ الدَّرْسَ وَالمِثَالَ.** — I read the lesson and the example. Either could have come first.

**دَخَلَ خَالِدٌ فَسَعِيدٌ.** — Khalid entered, then Saʿid closely after him.

**دَخَلَ خَالِدٌ ثُمَّ سَعِيدٌ.** — Khalid entered, then Saʿid after an interval.

**التعقيب** is relative to the action. The interval between two stages of a journey can be longer than the interval between two people entering a room. Context, **قرينة**, can establish simultaneous action or order with **و**; that information does not come from **و** alone. In some contexts, joined alternatives are distributed rather than added together: **اثنين وثلاثة وأربعة** need not mean a total of nine.

### When ف and ثم join clauses

Besides time order, **ف** can introduce a detailed explanation after a general statement: **طلب أمرًا عظيمًا، فقال...**, “He asked for something momentous, namely, he said…”. This is **ترتيب ذكري**, also called **رتبي**.

It can also show a reason or consequence. Read the direction of the reasoning:

- **تُبْ، فَإِنَّ اللهَ يَغْفِرُ.** — Repent, for Allah forgives. What follows gives a reason.
- **أَخْطَأْتَ، فَتُبْ.** — You have erred, so repent. What follows is a consequence.

**الفاء الفصيحة** reveals an omitted preceding event or condition needed to connect the sense. Recognise that an omission must be recoverable; do not invent one for every **ف**.

**ثم** can likewise mark a further, weightier point, **ترتيب ذكري**, with a sense like “furthermore.” Do not force a time sequence onto every pair of clauses.

### The conditions of حتى العاطفة

The coordinated expression must be included within the earlier whole and represent its endpoint, literally or in rank: **جزء مما قبلها، وغاية له**.

**أَكَلْتُ السَّمَكَةَ حَتَّى رَأْسَهَا.** — I ate the fish, even its head.

The head is part of the fish and is presented as the extreme reached. An unrelated fruit would not work as the coordinated endpoint of the fish. The endpoint can be the greatest or the least member: “even the strongest” or “even those on foot.” It need not be the last event in time.

Distinguish **حتى الجارّة**. In **مَشَيْتُ حَتَّى بَابِ المَسْجِدِ**, “I walked as far as the mosque door,” **بابِ** is genitive. The preposition does not require its endpoint to be a part of a previously named whole. If it is a part, it is normally included unless evidence excludes it; if it is merely an adjoining boundary outside the whole, it is not included. With coordinating **حتى**, participation in the preceding judgement is certain.

### Worked tarkīb

**أَكَلْتُ السَّمَكَةَ حَتَّى رَأْسَهَا.**

- **أكلتُ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على السكون لاتصاله بتاء الفاعل؛ التاء ضمير في محل رفع فاعل.
- **السمكةَ**: مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة، وهو المعطوف عليه.
- **حتى**: حرف عطف للغاية والتدريج، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **رأسَ**: معطوف على السمكة منصوب بالفتحة، وهو مضاف.
- **ها**: ضمير متصل في محل جر مضاف إليه.

Read: **المعطوف يتبع المعطوف عليه في الإعراب.** **يتبع** = follows. Explain which relationship gives **رأس** its fatḥah.

## Lesson 2: Alternatives, exclusion, and correction

### Five more conjunctions

| Particle | Essential function | Example and intended meaning |
|---|---|---|
| أو | **شكّ** after a statement; **تخيير** after a command | **جاء خالدٌ أو سعيدٌ**: the speaker is unsure which; **خذ قلمًا أو كتابًا**: choose one |
| أم | **طلب التعيين** or **التسوية** | **أخالدٌ جاء أم سعيدٌ؟**: identify which; equality will be studied in Lesson 7 |
| لا | **نفي الحكم عن المعطوف** | **جاء خالدٌ لا سعيدٌ**: Khalid came, Saʿid did not |
| بل | **إضراب**: turning away from a preceding expression | **جاء خالدٌ بل سعيدٌ**: the correction assigns the arrival to Saʿid |
| لكنْ | **استدراك**: a contrasting correction | **ما جاء خالدٌ لكنْ سعيدٌ**: Khalid did not come, but Saʿid did |

After negation or prohibition, **أو** can bring both alternatives under the negative scope: **لا تُطِعْ آثِمًا أَوْ كَفُورًا**, “Do not obey a sinner or an ingrate.” It does not grant permission to obey one of them.

With **أم** seeking identification, the first alternative follows **همزة الاستفهام**, and its counterpart, **المعادل**, follows **أم**. The alternatives can be subjects, objects, verbs, or other corresponding expressions. The answer supplies an identity, not merely “yes.”

### Conditions that change the analysis

**لا العاطفة** follows an affirmative statement or a command. After negation or prohibition, use **وَلا** in this pattern: **ما جاء خالدٌ ولا سعيدٌ**. Here **و** coordinates, and **لا** reinforces the negation: **زائدة في الإعراب**. This description does not remove its meaning.

Repeat **لا ... ولا** when negating paired predicates, adjectives, or circumstantial descriptions:

- **خالدٌ لا غافلٌ ولا لاهٍ.** — Khalid is neither inattentive nor distracted. Both are predicates.
- **صَلَّى لا غَافِلًا وَلا لَاهِيًا.** — He prayed neither inattentively nor distractedly. Both are **حال**, descriptions of his state, so they are accusative.

Usually both descriptions are denied. Sometimes context identifies a middle state between two extremes. In longer lists, repetition may be selective for emphasis; do not assume every **و** must always be followed by another **لا**.

**بل** has two effects in the noun-coordination patterns here:

- After affirmation or a command, **إبطال** replaces the earlier assignment: **اِقْرَأِ الدَّرْسَ بَلِ المِثَالَ** — Read the example instead of the lesson.
- After negation or prohibition, the earlier negative judgement stays and the second receives its opposite: **لا تَقْرَأِ الدَّرْسَ بَلِ المِثَالَ** — Do not read the lesson; read the example.

The kasrah in **اقرأِ، تقرأِ، بلِ** here permits connected pronunciation before **الـ**. It is not a case ending or a change from prohibition to another mood.

Coordinating **لكنْ** requires preceding negation or prohibition and no accompanying **و**. In **ولكنْ**, the **و** does the joining, or begins a new clause; **لكنْ** supplies the contrast and is extra to the government structure. Distinguish light **لكنْ** from governing **لكنَّ**.

### إِمَّا is not a tenth conjunction here

**الاسمُ إِمَّا مذكّرٌ وإِمَّا مؤنّثٌ.** — A noun is either masculine or feminine.

Use **إما ... وإما** or **إما ... أو**. Analyse **إما** as **حرف تفصيل** and the **و** as the conjunction. Another analysis treats the second **إما** as the conjunction and **و** as additional; recognise it, but use the first analysis consistently in these exercises. Distinguish **إِمَّا** from **أَمَّا** in Lesson 5.

### Worked tarkīb

**مَا رَأَيْتُ خَالِدًا لَكِنْ سَعِيدًا.** — I did not see Khalid, but I saw Saʿid.

**ما**: حرف نفي غير عامل. **رأيتُ**: past verb with subject تاء الفاعل. **خالدًا**: مفعول به منصوب. **لكنْ**: حرف عطف واستدراك. **سعيدًا**: معطوف منصوب. The second object has the same case, but a different affirmative/negative judgement.

Read: **يشترط في لكنْ العاطفة أن يسبقها نفي أو نهي.** **يشترط** = it is required; **يسبقها** = precedes it.

## Lesson 3: Questions and their answers

### Ask for truth or ask for identification

**التصديق** asks whether a whole judgement is true. **التصوّر** asks for identification of a particular element.

| Feature | أَ — همزة الاستفهام | هل |
|---|---|---|
| تصديق | **أحضر خالدٌ؟** — Did Khalid attend? | **هل حضر خالدٌ؟** |
| تصوّر | **أخالدٌ حضر أم سعيدٌ؟** — Which one attended? | Does not supply this أ...أم identification pattern |
| Affirmative or negative construction | Both: **ألم يحضرْ؟** | Enters affirmative constructions in the pattern taught here |
| With a conjunction | Hamzah precedes it: **أَوَلَمْ...؟** | هل follows it: **وَهَلْ...؟** |

Both particles can introduce nominal or verbal sentences: **هل خالدٌ حاضرٌ؟** and **هل حضر خالدٌ؟**. They do not themselves change noun or verb endings.

### نعم and بلى are not interchangeable

**نعم** confirms the judgement presented. **بلى** overturns a negative judgement and affirms the action.

| Question | Response | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **أَسَافَرَ خَالِدٌ؟** | **نعم** | He travelled |
| **أَمَا سَافَرَ خَالِدٌ؟** | **نعم** | Correct: he did not travel |
| **أَمَا سَافَرَ خَالِدٌ؟** | **بلى** | He did travel |

**بلى** also answers a negative statement: **لم يسافر خالدٌ. — بلى.** The reply asserts that he did travel. **لا** answers an affirmative question negatively: **هل سافر؟ — لا.**

Other responses to recognise:

- **إي**: affirmation before an oath, **إِي وَاللهِ**.
- **أجلْ**: assent to a statement.
- **إنَّ**: an affirmative response, appearing as **إِنَّهْ** at pause; the final **هْ** is هاء السكت, not “him.” It is not governing إنَّ here.
- **جَيْرِ**: an affirmative response.
- **كَلَّا**: normally **ردع وزجر**, forceful rejection or rebuke, commonly followed by further speech. It can also express assent, like أجل/إي, or introduce attention, like ألا. Context decides; do not translate every occurrence as “no.”

With rejecting **كلا**, a pause can follow it; with affirming **كلا**, begin with it after pausing before it. Where rejection and assent are both plausible, rejection is the primary reading. A proposed “truly” sense must not be used to change a following **إنَّ** into **أنَّ**; some analyses instead recover an unspoken claim being rejected.

### Worked tarkīb

**أَلَمْ يَحْضُرْ خَالِدٌ؟ — بَلَى.** — Did Khalid not attend? — He did.

**أَ**: حرف استفهام. **لم**: حرف نفي وجزم وقلب. **يحضرْ**: مضارع مجزوم بلم بالسكون. **خالدٌ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة. **بلى**: حرف جواب لإيجاب المنفي. The question particle does not cause the sukūn; **لم** does.

Read: **نعم لتصديق الكلام، وبلى لإيجاب المنفي.** Explain the two responses to a negative question without using the ambiguous English word “yes.”

## Lesson 4: Emphasis without case government

### The emphasis particles

**تأكيد** and **توكيد** both mean emphasis.

| Form | Function and example |
|---|---|
| **لام الابتداء** | Begins a nominal assertion emphatically: **لَلْعِلْمُ نَافِعٌ** — Knowledge certainly is beneficial |
| **اللام المزحلقة** | Emphatic lām shifted away from the beginning after إنَّ: **إِنَّ العِلْمَ لَنَافِعٌ** |
| **لام جواب القسم** | Marks an emphatic oath response: **وَاللهِ لَأَكْتُبَنَّ** — By Allah, I shall certainly write |
| **نونا التأكيد** | Heavy **نَّ** and light **نْ**, attached to a verb |
| **قد** | Confirmation or a contextual qualification of a verb |

**لام الابتداء** and **اللام المزحلقة** are also called **لام التوكيد**. The shifted lām avoids beginning with both emphasis markers together. It can occur with a noun predicate, an imperfect verb predicate, or a phrase predicate:

**إِنَّ خَالِدًا لَصَادِقٌ. إِنَّ خَالِدًا لَيَصْدُقُ. إِنَّ خَالِدًا لَفِي البَيْتِ.**

It can also precede a delayed اسم إنَّ after a phrase predicate: **إِنَّ فِي البَيْتِ لَكِتَابًا**. Here **كتابًا** is still اسم إنّ مؤخر منصوب; lām does not make it a predicate.

### A necessary verb-form distinction

In **لَأَكْتُبَنَّ**, **أكتبنَّ** has an implicit subject **أنا**. With نون التوكيد directly attached in this simple form, the imperfect is **مبني على الفتح في محل رفع**: fixed on fatḥah but in an indicative position, since no subjunctive or jussive governor precedes it. This is **بناء**, not نصب governed by the nūn. The light counterpart is **لَأَكْتُبَنْ**. Recognise both; do not extend this simple attachment pattern to every plural or dual verb without studying its conjugation.

### قد depends on tense and meaning

With a past verb, **قد** gives **تحقيق**, confirmation: **قَدْ وَصَلَ الضَّيْفُ**, “The guest has indeed arrived.”

With a مضارع, distinguish:

- **تحقيق**: certainty, as in **قَدْ يَعْلَمُ اللهُ**. It does not express doubt about divine knowledge.
- **توقّع**: expected or hoped-for occurrence, **قَدْ يَبْرَأُ المَرِيضُ**, “The patient may recover.”
- **تكثير**: frequent occurrence; **قَدْ نَرَى تَقَلُّبَ وَجْهِكَ**, frequent turning of the face being observed.
- **تقليل**: infrequency, the common use with مضارع: **قَدْ يَصْدُقُ الكَذُوبُ**, “The habitual liar may occasionally tell the truth.”

Vocabulary: **يبرأ** = recovers; **كذوب** = habitual liar; **وقوع** = occurrence. Do not determine the meaning of **قد** from the verb form alone.

### Worked tarkīb

**إِنَّ خَالِدًا لَيَقْرَأُ الدَّرْسَ.**

**إنّ**: حرف توكيد ونصب. **خالدًا**: اسم إنّ منصوب. **لَـ**: اللام المزحلقة، غير عاملة. **يقرأُ**: مضارع مرفوع بالضمة؛ فاعله ضمير مستتر تقديره هو، يعود إلى خالد. **الدرسَ**: مفعول به منصوب. **يقرأ الدرس**: جملة فعلية في محل رفع خبر إنّ. The lām adds emphasis; it neither causes the verb's رفع nor cancels إنّ's government.

Read: **قد تدخل على الماضي والمضارع، ويتعيّن معناها بالسياق.** **يتعيّن** = is determined; **السياق** = context.

## Lesson 5: Conditions without jussive government

### لو: an unrealised condition and what follows from it

**لَوْ حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ لَأَكْرَمْتُهُ.** — Had Khalid attended, I would have honoured him.

**لو** is **حرف شرط غير جازم**. In this conditional use, it presents an unrealised past condition; an imperfect form after it can also have past reference. The affirmative response, **جواب**, commonly has **لَـ**, but this lām is not compulsory.

Learn the important qualification to **امتناع الجواب لامتناع الشرط**: an unrealised condition would have entailed its response, but the response could still have occurred for a different reason. From “Had he attended, I would have honoured him,” we cannot automatically conclude that he received no honour in any other circumstances.

The precise relationship is **امتناع الشرط واستلزامه للجواب**: non-occurrence of the condition, and its entailing the response. The response lām gives emphasis, especially appropriate to a consequence presented as remote or remarkable; do not make it a condition of grammatical correctness.

### A noun immediately after لو

Conditional **لو** calls for a verbal construction. If a noun appears first, recover the governor:

- **لَوْ خَالِدًا رَأَيْتُهُ لَأَكْرَمْتُهُ**: **خالدًا** is the object of an omitted **رأيتُ**, explained by the following **رأيتُهُ**. Do not call it the subject of لو.
- **لَوْ أَنَّ خَالِدًا حَضَرَ لَأَكْرَمْتُهُ**: understand **لو ثبتَ أنَّ خالدًا حضرَ**. **أنّ ومعمولاها** form a **مصدر مؤوّل** in the nominative subject position of omitted **ثبت**. Internally, خالد remains اسم أنّ منصوب.

**محذوف** = omitted; **يفسّره** = explains it; **ثبت** = was established. A مصدر مؤوّل is a clause understood as a noun-like unit; Lesson 7 develops this.

### لولا and لوما: an existing condition prevents a result

**لَوْلَا العِلْمُ لَضَلَّ النَّاسُ.** — Were it not for knowledge, people would have gone astray.

**لولا** expresses **امتناع الجواب لوجود الشرط**: the condition exists, preventing the result stated in the response. The first construction is nominal, normally with a general-existence predicate omitted: **العلمُ موجودٌ**. The response is verbal. Conditional **لوما** has the same relationship: **لَوْمَا العِلْمُ لَضَلَّ النَّاسُ**.

### أما: a condition-like introduction to detail

**أَمَّا خَالِدٌ فَحَاضِرٌ، وَأَمَّا سَعِيدٌ فَغَائِبٌ.** — As for Khalid, he is present; as for Saʿid, he is absent.

**أما** is **حرف شرط وتفصيل وتوكيد**. Its sense can be expanded as **مهما يكن من شيء**, “whatever the circumstances may be.” A separating expression comes between **أما** and its linking **ف**. Do not place the two directly together in the ordinary pattern.

What intervenes can be:

| Expression | Example | Its role |
|---|---|---|
| A subject | **أما خالدٌ فحاضرٌ** | مبتدأ |
| A predicate phrase | **أما في البيتِ فخالدٌ** | خبر مقدم؛ خالد مبتدأ مؤخر |
| A conditional clause | **أما إنْ حضر خالدٌ فأكرمْهُ** | شرط within the تفصيل construction |
| A noun governed from the response | **أما اليتيمَ فلا تقهرْ** | مفعول به مقدم لتقهر |
| A noun governed by an omitted verb | **أما خالدًا فأكرمتُهُ** | منصوب بفعل محذوف يفسّره أكرمته |

Vocabulary: **يتيم** = orphan; **تقهر** = oppress. **أما** does not assign the different cases in this table. Repetition normally develops **تفصيل**, dividing a general topic into parts. One part may remain implicit. When no repeated division is expressed or intended, the emphatic force is more prominent. Retain the name **فاء رابطة لجواب أما** without inventing a second complete sentence after the ف in **أما خالد فحاضر**.

### Worked tarkīb

**لَوْلَا العِلْمُ لَضَلَّ النَّاسُ.**

**لولا**: حرف شرط غير جازم، لامتناع الجواب لوجود الشرط. **العلمُ**: مبتدأ مرفوع؛ خبره محذوف تقديره موجود. **لَـ**: رابطة للجواب. **ضلَّ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح. **الناسُ**: فاعل مرفوع. **ضلّ الناس**: جواب لولا، لا محل له من الإعراب. No noun or verb here is made jussive by لولا.

Read: **لو غير جازمة، ولولا تدلّ على وجود الشرط.** State which condition exists in the worked example and which result is prevented.

## Lesson 6: Urging, offering, and drawing attention

### Same form, different communicative purpose

**توبيخ** is reproach for a missed action or fault; **تحضيض** is forceful urging; **عرض** is a gentle offer or suggestion.

| Use | Particles | Typical construction |
|---|---|---|
| توبيخ | **هلّا، ألّا، لولا، لوما** | Past action: **هَلَّا صَلَّيْتَ!** — Why did you not pray? |
| تحضيض | **هلّا، ألّا، لولا، لوما، ألا** | Imperfect: **هَلَّا تُصَلِّي!** — Do pray! |
| عرض | **ألا، أما، لو** | Gentle invitation: **أَلَا تَجْلِسُ؟** — Will you sit down? |

Distinguish strong **أَلَّا** from ungeminated **أَلَا**. The latter, in its urging use here, enters the imperfect, not the past. Context and tone distinguish its urging and offering uses. These particles do not by themselves make the following imperfect منصوب or مجزوم.

**لَوْلَا تَصْدُقُ!** urges truthfulness; **لولا العلمُ لضلَّ الناسُ** is conditional. Look for the construction and the response, not just the written particle. Reproach about a missed act has no conditional response.

The speaker's position and context can refine the force of **لولا**: reproach can be called **تغليط** or **تنديم**; a less authoritative speaker may express a wish or regret; address to someone other than the actor can express wonder; a demand can challenge another's ability, **تعجيز**. Do not label every past-looking request an angry rebuke. Urging seeks an action still unrealised; conditional لولا presents an existing condition.

### تنبيه: directing attention to a statement

| Particle | Where to recognise it |
|---|---|
| **ألا** | Before nominal or verbal statements; often followed by emphasis: **أَلَا إِنَّ العِلْمَ نَافِعٌ** |
| **أما** | Commonly before an oath: **أَمَا وَاللهِ...**; its hamzah may be omitted in attested usage |
| **ها** | With a near demonstrative (**هذا، هؤلاء**), a nominative pronoun (**ها أنتم**), a past verb with قد (**ها قد وصلَ**), or **أيّ/أيّة** before an أل noun in address (**يا أيها الطالب**) |

Removing attention **ألا** leaves the underlying assertion intact. Removing offering **ألا** from **ألا تجلس؟** loses the offer, even though the remaining words could form a different statement. This is a meaning test, not just a test of word order.

### Worked tarkīb

**هَا قَدْ حَضَرَ المُعَلِّمُ.** — Look, the teacher has arrived.

**ها**: حرف تنبيه. **قد**: حرف تحقيق. **حضرَ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح. **المعلمُ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة. Neither particle governs the subject's case.

Read: **العرض طلب بلين، والتحضيض طلب بشدّة.** **لين** = gentleness; **شدّة** = forcefulness.

## Lesson 7: Turning a clause into a noun-like unit

### المصدر المؤوّل

A **مصدر صريح** is an ordinary verbal noun, such as **حضور**, attendance. A **مصدر مؤوّل** is a particle and its following construction understood as a noun-like unit. Its overall role is separate from the internal endings of its words.

These particles are **أحرف المصدر** or **موصولات حرفية / حروف موصولة**. Non-governing forms here are **ما، لو بعد ودّ، همزة التسوية**. Governing **أنْ، أنَّ، كي** also form such constructions, but retain their own government.

### ما: with and without a time meaning

**فَرِحْتُ بِمَا نَجَحْتَ.** — I rejoiced at your succeeding; that is, **بنجاحِكَ**.

Here **ما مصدرية غير ظرفية**. It does not negate **نجحتَ**, and it is not a relative noun meaning “the thing which.” The combined unit **ما نجحتَ** supplies the genitive complement of **بـ**.

**أَذْكُرُ اللهَ مَا دُمْتُ حَيًّا.** — I remember Allah as long as I remain alive; that is, **مدّةَ دوامي حيًّا**.

Here **ما مصدرية ظرفية**: the construction supplies a duration. **دام** acts as a ناقص verb after this ما: **تُ** is its noun in رفع, and **حيًّا** its predicate in نصب. The absence of government by **ما** does not prevent **دام** from governing internally.

### لو after ودّ

**وَدِدْتُ لَوْ تَحْضُرُ.** — I wished for your attendance; approximately **وددتُ حضورَكَ**.

**لو** is مصدرية here. **تحضرُ** remains indicative; the noun-like construction is an object of **وددتُ**. Do not search for a conditional response when this is the intended construction.

### همزة التسوية with أم

**سَوَاءٌ عَلَيَّ أَحَضَرْتَ أَمْ غِبْتَ.** — Your attending and your being absent are equal to me.

**التسوية** means treating the alternatives equally. The hamzah is not asking which happened. Understand the alternatives as **حضورُكَ وغيابُكَ**. In this pattern, **سواءٌ** is a fronted predicate and the noun-like alternatives supply the delayed subject. Similar hamzah constructions occur after expressions such as **ما أبالي** (“I do not care”), **ما أدري** (“I do not know”), and **ليت شعري** (“would that I knew”); determine their force from the whole expression.

### Worked tarkīb

**وَدِدْتُ لَوْ تَحْضُرُ.**

**وددتُ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على السكون؛ التاء في محل رفع فاعل. **لو**: حرف مصدري غير عامل. **تحضرُ**: مضارع مرفوع بالضمة؛ فاعله مستتر تقديره أنت. **لو تحضر**: مصدر مؤوّل في محل نصب مفعول به. The object's position does not make **تحضرُ** منصوبًا.

Read: **المصدر المؤوّل له محلّ، ولا يلزم أن يظهر إعرابه على الفعل الذي فيه.** Explain this using **تحضرُ**.

## Lesson 8: Negation and additional particles

### Negating without government

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ما** | Before a past verb or an imperfect; with the imperfect, negation of the present situation | **ما حضرَ خالدٌ**; **ما يكتبُ خالدٌ** |
| **لا** | Normally before an imperfect | **لا يكذبُ خالدٌ** — Khalid does not lie |
| **إنْ** | Negative; often followed later by إلا | **إنْ خالدٌ إلا طالبٌ** — Khalid is only a student |

Non-governing **لا** can precede past verbs when repeated, **لا قرأَ ولا كتبَ**, or when the past form has future-oriented supplicatory meaning, **لا قَدَّرَ اللهُ**, “May Allah not decree it.” Do not make unrepeated لا the ordinary substitute for ما before a past statement.

Distinguish **لا يكذبُ** (a statement) from **لا تكذبْ** (a prohibition). Governing negatives include **لا لنفي الجنس**, particles working like **ليس**, **لن، لم، لمّا**, and prohibitive **لا**. The spelling alone does not settle the analysis.

### زائد does not mean useless

**أحرف الزيادة**, also **أحرف الصلة**, are additional to the basic grammatical structure. Removing one can leave a grammatical sentence while losing emphasis or a meaning distinction. Their occurrence is tied to particular constructions; do not insert them arbitrarily.

| Additional particle | Recognition pattern |
|---|---|
| **إنْ** | Usually with negative ما: **ما إنْ كذبَ خالدٌ** |
| **أنْ** | Commonly after **لمّا الحينيّة**: **لمّا أنْ جاء البشيرُ...** — When the bearer of good news came…; also between an earlier oath and لو: **واللهِ أنْ لو قمتَ قمتُ** |
| **ما** | After conditional expressions such as **إذا، متى، أيّ، أين، إنْ**; after **من، عن، باء الجرّ** |
| **لا** | With و after negation; in certain أنْ constructions; before an oath expression |

Time **لمّا** in **لمّا أن جاء** means “when”; it is not the جازمة meaning “not yet.” Additional **أنْ** there can suggest an interval or delay; do not read it as a subjunctive governor. Additional **ما** with conditionals can strengthen the connection or generalise its scope. In **إمّا** meaning “if,” the structure is **إنْ + ما**; distinguish it from the “either” إما of Lesson 2 by its conditional construction.

Additional **ما** does not stop government in these patterns: **عَمَّا قَلِيلٍ** retains جرّ after عن; **أَيْنَمَا تَجْلِسْ أَجْلِسْ** retains the conditional government of أين. Do not confuse it with **ما الكافّة**, which does stop a governor in constructions already studied.

Two restricted uses of additional **لا** need care:

- **ما منعكَ ألّا تسجدَ؟**: with “prevent” already expressing non-performance, لا reinforces that negative sense; the question concerns what prevented prostration. Do not apply a mechanical “two negatives always cancel” rule.
- **لا أُقْسِمُ...** can be analysed with additional لا before the oath, giving rhetorical emphasis rather than simply denying the oath. Other readings exist; this is recognition of an attested construction, not permission to add لا before any affirmative verb.

Additional **ب، من، لام الجرّ** still govern جرّ; “additional” and “non-governing” are separate classifications.

One prerequisite completes the four-way distinction for **إنْ**: **إنْ المخففة من الثقيلة** is a lightened form of إنَّ. In **إِنْ كَانَ خَالِدٌ لَصَادِقًا**, it is non-governing; **كان** raises its noun خالدٌ and assigns نصب to its predicate صادقًا. The **لام فارقة** distinguishes this affirmative reading from negative إنْ. Thus **إنْ** may be conditional, lightened, negative, or additional; use the complete construction to decide.

### Worked tarkīb and a meaning distinction

**مَا جَاءَ خَالِدٌ وَلَا سَعِيدٌ.**

**ما**: حرف نفي غير عامل. **جاءَ**: فعل ماضٍ. **خالدٌ**: فاعل مرفوع. **و**: حرف عطف. **لا**: زائدة لتأكيد النفي. **سعيدٌ**: معطوف مرفوع. Each person's arrival is denied. Without the second لا, **ما جاء خالد وسعيد** can deny their joint arrival and leave separate arrivals possible. Thus “additional” does not mean semantically dispensable.

Read: **ما الزائدة هنا لا تكفّ العامل عن العمل.** **تكفّ** = stops. Explain using **عمّا قليلٍ**.

## Lesson 9: Wishes and future reference

### Wishes: لو and هل

**التمنّي** expresses a wish. **لو** and **هل** can carry this meaning without governing endings; **ليت** expresses wishing while governing a noun and predicate.

**لَوْ أَنَّ لَنَا كَرَّةً!** — If only we had a return!

**هَلْ لَنَا مِنْ شُفَعَاءَ؟** — If only we had intercessors! In a context of longing, the question form expresses a wish. Vocabulary: **كرّة** = a return; **شفعاء** = intercessors. Recognise the wish without treating every هل question as a wish.

Four uses of **لو** now fit together:

| Context | Function |
|---|---|
| An unrealised condition with its consequence | شرط: **لو حضر لأكرمتُه** |
| Longing for what has been missed or is unavailable | تمنٍّ: **لو أن لنا كرّةً** |
| A gentle proposal for something still possible | عرض: **لو تتصدّقُ على الفقراء** |
| After ودّ, forming the desired object | مصدر: **أودّ لو تحضرُ** |

Wishing لو has two recognised analyses: a wish particle needing no conditional response, or a conditional particle coloured by wishing with an omitted response. Do not require a learner to invent an expressed response that is not there.

### س and سوف

**سـ** is **حرف استقبال / تنفيس**; **سوف** is **حرف استقبال / تسويف**. Both place an imperfect action in the future and leave its mood unaffected.

**سَيَشِبُّ الغُلَامُ، وَسَوْفَ يَشِيخُ الفَتَى.** — The boy will grow into youth, and the young man will grow old.

The distinction taught here is nearer future with **سـ**, a longer or more remote future with **سوف**. It is relative, not a fixed number of days. In this comparison of affirmative and negative future meanings, **سـ** is paired with **لا**, and **سوف** with **لن**; the particles do not share the same government: **لن** assigns نصب, whereas سـ and سوف do not.

### Worked tarkīb

**سَوْفَ يَقْرَأُ خَالِدٌ الكِتَابَ.**

**سوف**: حرف استقبال غير عامل. **يقرأُ**: مضارع مرفوع بالضمة. **خالدٌ**: فاعل مرفوع. **الكتابَ**: مفعول به منصوب. The future meaning is not a reason for نصب.

Read: **السين وسوف للاستقبال، ولا تنصبان المضارع.** Explain the last verb, **تنصبان**, as “the two do not make [it] accusative/subjunctive.”

## Lesson 10: Explaining an expression — أيْ and أنْ

### Core rule

**التفسير** explains an earlier expression. **أيْ** can explain a single expression or a clause:

**أَسْأَلُ اللهَ الفِرْدَوْسَ أَيِ الجَنَّةَ.** — I ask Allah for al-Firdaws, that is, Paradise.

**اِسْتَغْفَرْتُ اللهَ، أَيْ سَأَلْتُ اللهَ أَنْ يَغْفِرَ لِي.** — I sought Allah's forgiveness; that is, I asked Allah to forgive me.

**أيْ** is an explanatory particle, not interrogative **أيّ**. It does not govern the following noun's case. In the first example, **الجنةَ** explains **الفردوسَ** and follows its accusative status; it can be analysed as **عطف بيان**. This is an explanatory noun-dependency, not a tenth حرف عطف.

### Conditions of أنْ التفسيرية

It explains a clause only. For this analysis:

1. A clause must precede it.
2. That clause conveys the meaning of saying, **معنى القول**, without the actual word “say”: calling, revelation, a charge or instruction, or writing can supply this meaning.
3. No preposition governs أنْ, whether expressed or understood.

**كَتَبْتُ إِلَيْهِ أَنْ اُكْتُبْ.** — I wrote to him: “Write.”

Here أنْ introduces the content of the written communication; it does not turn **اكتبْ** into a subjunctive. The preposition **إلى** governs the recipient pronoun, not أنْ. The following explanatory sentence has **لا محل لها من الإعراب**.

Contrast **أَمَرْتُهُ أَنْ يَكْتُبَ**, “I ordered him to write,” understood with **بأن يكتب**. Here أنْ is مصدرية ناصبة because a prepositional relationship is understood. A verb of communication alone does not establish تفسيرية.

The conditions permit explanatory أنْ; they do not make its insertion obligatory. A call or instruction can be followed by its content without أنْ.

### Four readings of أنْ

| Reading | Diagnostic example |
|---|---|
| مصدرية ناصبة | **أريد أنْ أكتبَ** |
| مخففة من الثقيلة | **علمتُ أنْ قد حضر خالدٌ**: underlying أنَّ with an omitted **ضمير الشأن**, a pronoun introducing the matter explained by the following clause |
| زائدة | **لمّا أنْ حضر خالدٌ...** |
| تفسيرية | **كتبتُ إليه أنْ اكتبْ** |

A cognitive verb is a clue to lightened أنَّ; a communicative clause is a clue to تفسيرية. These clues do not replace the full construction, especially the preposition condition. Where the preceding expression is not a clause, do not label أنْ تفسيرية merely because English “namely” sounds possible.

### Worked tarkīb

**كَتَبْتُ إِلَيْهِ أَنْ اُكْتُبْ.**

**كتبتُ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على السكون؛ التاء فاعل. **إلى**: حرف جر؛ **الهاء**: ضمير في محل جر؛ الجار والمجرور متعلق بكتبت. **أنْ**: حرف تفسير غير عامل. **اكتبْ**: فعل أمر مبني على السكون؛ فاعله مستتر وجوبًا تقديره أنت. **اكتب** مع فاعله: جملة تفسيرية لا محل لها من الإعراب.

Read: **يشترط في أنْ التفسيرية ألّا يسبقها حرف جرّ لفظًا أو تقديرًا.** Here **ألّا** itself is **أنْ + لا**, not the urging particle of Lesson 6.

1. أعرب **كتبت إليه أن اجلس**. Include the attachment of إلى and the status of the explanatory clause.
2. Correct: “Every أنْ following a word associated with speech is تفسيرية.” State the three necessary checks.

**10R:** Classify **أنْ** in **لمّا أن حضر خالدٌ كتبتُ إليه أن اجلسْ**. Why does neither occurrence assign نصب to its following verb?

## Lesson 11: Response links and attached particles

### ف and ل linking responses

**الفاء الرابطة للجواب** connects an appropriate response to its condition; it is not necessarily a conjunction between equal sentence members.

**إِنْ يَحْضُرْ خَالِدٌ فَأَكْرِمْهُ.** — If Khalid attends, honour him.

**إنْ** governs the condition. **ف** links the imperative response. The response clause is **في محل جزم جواب الشرط** because the condition is jussive; **أكرمْ** itself remains an imperative, مبني على السكون. Other familiar response forms requiring ف include nominal responses and responses beginning with **قد، لن، سـ، سوف**. The link does not itself govern جزم.

**اللام الرابطة للجواب** appears with لو and لولا: **لولا العلمُ لضلَّ الناسُ**. Distinguish it from prepositional لام in **لِخالدٍ**, emphatic لام in **إنه لصادقٌ**, and لام الأمر in **لِيكتبْ**.

### تاء التأنيث الساكنة is not the subject

**كَتَبَتْ هِنْدٌ.** — Hind wrote.

The **تْ** marks a feminine subject; **هندٌ** is the subject. Contrast **كَتَبْتُ**, “I wrote,” where **تُ** is a subject pronoun.

The feminine marker can carry an incidental vowel: **كَتَبَتِ الطَّالِبَةُ**, with kasrah to avoid two successive vowelless consonants in connected reading, **دفع التقاء الساكنين**. Analyse the marker as **مبني على السكون المقدّر**, not as a genitive word. In **كَتَبَتَا**, “the two females wrote,” **ت** is still the gender marker; **ألف الاثنين** is the subject pronoun.

### هاء السكت

**هاء السكت** is a vowelless hāʾ attached in pausing. In **مَالِيَهْ**, “my wealth,” distinguish **مال** (wealth), **ياء المتكلم** (“my”), and the final pause **هْ**. The hāʾ is not an object or possessive pronoun. Its occurrence belongs to established pause forms; do not append it freely to every word.

Compare affirmative **إِنَّهْ** at pause in Lesson 3 with **إِنَّهُ صَادِقٌ**: in the latter, **هُ** is اسم إنّ in نصب and **صادقٌ** is its predicate.

### Worked tarkīb

**كَتَبَتِ الطَّالِبَةُ الدَّرْسَ.**

**كتبَ**: فعل ماضٍ مبني على الفتح. **تِ**: تاء التأنيث الساكنة، حرف مبني على السكون المقدّر، حُرّك بالكسر للتخلّص من التقاء الساكنين، لا محل له. **الطالبةُ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة. **الدرسَ**: مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة.

Read: **تاء التأنيث حرف، وتاء الفاعل ضمير.** Use the different sentence structures to prove the distinction.

2. Explain why the kasrah in **كتبتِ الطالبةُ** is not جرّ.
3. Classify ف in **حضر خالدٌ فسعيدٌ** and **إنْ يحضرْ خالدٌ فأكرمْهُ**.

## Lesson 12: Tanwīn and reading a whole construction

### Tanwīn has more than one function

**التنوين** supplies a final nūn sound without an ordinary written ن: **رجلٌ** is pronounced with an ending like **ـُنْ** in connected speech. The vowel and the n sound must not be confused: one can signal case while the tanwīn has a separate classification.

| Kind | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **تنوين التمكين**; also **الصرف، الأمكنية** | Attached to fully declinable, triptote nouns | **خالدٌ، رجلٌ** |
| **تنوين التنكير** | Makes certain fixed nouns and verb-nouns indefinite | **صَهٍ**: “Be silent about any speech,” versus **صَهْ** about a specified conversation |
| **تنوين المقابلة** | The tanwīn of the sound feminine plural, corresponding to the ن of the sound masculine plural | **مسلماتٌ** beside **مسلمونَ** |
| **تنوين التعويض** | Replaces an omitted letter, word, or clause | See below |

A **اسم فعل** is a noun-form expressing a verb's force: **صه** means **اسكتْ**, “be silent.” It remains fixed, **مبني**, when tanwīn التنكير is added. Certain fixed names ending in **ـويه** likewise distinguish a particular named person from an unspecified bearer of that name. Learn this as a restricted class, not a way of making every proper name indefinite.

Three compensations, **أعواض**, to recognise:

- **عن حرف**: **غَوَاشٍ**, “coverings,” has an underlying final ي omitted in this form. The tanwīn compensates for a letter; do not infer that every noun ending **ـٍ** has lost a yāʾ.
- **عن كلمة**: **كُلٌّ آمَنَ**, understood as **كلُّ أحدٍ آمنَ**. The tanwīn replaces the omitted complement of كلّ.
- **عن جملة**: **يَوْمَئِذٍ**. The tanwīn attached to إذ replaces a clause specifying the event meant by “then.”

An additional poetic category, **تنوين الترنّم**, replaces a rhyme's final prolongation with a n sound. It may occur at a verse ending on a noun, verb, or particle. Recognition forms include **العتابَنْ** and **أصابَنْ**. Do not treat this poetic n as ordinary case tanwīn or as proof that a word is a noun.

**خالدٌ** is a definite proper name despite its tanwīn. **صهٍ** is fixed despite its tanwīn. Therefore tanwīn does not always mean “indefinite,” nor does it always prove a word is declinable.

### Worked tarkīb

**حَضَرَتْ مُسْلِمَاتٌ.** — Muslim women attended.

**حضرَ**: past verb, fixed on fatḥah. **تْ**: تاء التأنيث الساكنة، لا محل لها. **مسلماتٌ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة؛ جمع مؤنث سالم؛ تنوينه تنوين مقابلة. The ḍammah marks رفع; the tanwīn belongs to the مقابلة category.

### A final word about صدر الكلام

Negation, attention, questioning, conditional and urging particles, and the particles resembling verbs except **أنَّ**, have **صدر الكلام** in the relevant constructions. Identify the beginning of the clause they introduce, not merely the first ink on the line. A conjunction can connect that clause to earlier speech; emphasis can occur inside a larger sentence. The placement distinction **أَوَ... / وَهَلْ...** still matters.

Read without added vowels: **التنوين ليس نوعا واحدا. والحرف يعرف معناه ووظيفته بالسياق.** Explain both statements using two different examples.

## Cumulative application

### A. A connected account

Read without adding full internal vowels. Add grammatical endings where needed.

**حضر خالد وسعيد. أما خالد فقرأ الدرس، وأما سعيد فكتب المثال. ما قرأ خالد المثال بل الدرس. ثم حضرت هند. ألا إن هندًا لفي البيت. سوف تقرأ الدرس ثم تكتب المثال.**

1. Identify every particle and its function. Which occurrences of ف link an أما construction?
2. Does the first sentence establish whose arrival came first? Does ثم establish a later stage here?
3. Explain the correction in **ما قرأ خالد المثال بل الدرس**. Which reading is denied, and which affirmed?
4. Give full tarkīb of **أما خالد فقرأ الدرس** and **ألا إن هندًا لفي البيت**.
5. Analyse the last sentence. Recover both hidden subjects; give the mood of both verbs and the case of both objects.

### B. A short exchange

**ألم يحضر الضيف؟ — بلى، وقد حضر المعلم أيضًا. كتبت إلى الضيف أن اجلس. لولا المعلم لخرج الضيف. لو خرج الضيف لحزنت.**

Vocabulary: **أيضًا** = also; treat it here as an accusative verbal-noun expression used adverbially. **حزنتُ** = I would have been saddened.

1. What does بلى assert? What would نعم assert in its place?
2. أعرب **كتبت إلى الضيف أن اجلس** إعرابًا تامًّا.
3. Explain the existing condition and prevented response in the لولا sentence. Supply its omitted predicate.
4. What does the لو sentence establish about the guest's departure? Does it prove that the speaker felt no sadness from any other cause?

### C. Discriminate before analysing

For each pair, name the particles' different functions and explain the endings.

1. **لا تكتبُ هندٌ / لا تكتبْ** (“Hind does not write” / “Do not write”).
2. **أريد أنْ أكتبَ / كتبتُ إليه أنْ اكتبْ**.
3. **لولا العلمُ لضلَّ الناسُ / لولا تتعلّمُ!** (**تتعلّم** = learn; forceful urging).
4. **أودُّ لو تحضرُ / لو حضرتَ لأكرمتُكَ**.
5. **إنْ يحضرْ خالدٌ أكرمْهُ / إنْ خالدٌ إلا طالبٌ**. Read the first response **أُكْرِمْهُ**, “I honour him,” not imperative **أَكْرِمْهُ**.
6. **ما إنْ كذبَ / إنْ كانَ لَصادقًا**.

### D. Independent tarkīb

أضف الحركات اللازمة، واذكر معنى كل حرف، والعامل، والمعمول، والعلامة، ومحل الجملة حيث يلزم.

1. **إن يحضر المعلم فسوف أقرأ الدرس**.
2. **ما رأيت المعلم لكن الطالب**.
3. **أما في البيت فخالد**.
4. **كتبت الطالبة الدرس والمثال** — The female student wrote the lesson and the example.

For each sentence, explain one plausible mistaken analysis and why the structure rules it out.
