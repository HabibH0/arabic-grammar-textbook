# Module 5: Particles Governing Nouns

الحُرُوفُ العَامِلَةُ فِي الاسْمِ

## Before you begin

This module teaches how particles change a nominal sentence, how their meanings differ, and how to analyse both the words inside a clause and the clause’s place in a larger sentence.

Recall **مبتدأ، خبر، فاعل، مفعول به، مضاف إليه** and **رفع، نصب، جرّ**. An ordinary singular noun usually shows these cases with **ـُ، ـَ، ـِ**. A fixed pronoun instead occupies a position: **في محل رفع / نصب / جرّ**. Do not call a particle’s fixed vowel a case ending.

Two useful prerequisites:

- **خبر مفرد**: a predicate that is neither a sentence nor a شبه جملة. Here **مفرد** does not mean “singular in number.”
- **شبه جملة**: a prepositional or adverbial phrase. In **خالدٌ في البيتِ**, the phrase attaches to an omitted general predicate, approximately **كائنٌ**, “present.” We will name both that attachment and its predicate function.

In **إنَّهُ صادقٌ**, **هُ** is an attached pronoun occupying **نصب اسم إنّ**. In **إنَّ خالدًا يكتبُ**, the verb has an implicit subject **هو** referring to Khalid; the whole **يكتبُ** clause occupies **رفع خبر إنّ**.

Work through one lesson at a time, then check the separate answers. Early examples supply vowels; later tasks ask you to recover endings. Use connected-speech endings. Every tarkīb should explain the governor, role, ending or position, and relevant relationship.

## Lesson 1: Three patterns of government

### The core rule

After prepositions, distinguish three further patterns:

| Pattern | Particles introduced here | Result |
|---|---|---|
| One noun receives نصب | إِلَّا in an accusative exception construction | مستثنى منصوب |
| The noun receives نصب and the predicate رفع | إنّ، أنّ، كأنّ، لكنّ، لعلّ، ليت؛ لا لنفي الجنس | اسم منصوب، خبر مرفوع |
| The noun receives رفع and the predicate نصب | ما، لا، لات when governing like ليس | اسم مرفوع، خبر منصوب |

**إِلَّا** marks an exception, **استثناء**. In **حَضَرَ الطُّلَّابُ إِلَّا خَالِدًا**, “The students attended except Khalid,” **الطلابُ** is **المستثنى منه**, the group from which someone is excluded; **خالدًا** is **المستثنى**.

**حضرَ**: past verb. **الطلابُ**: فاعل مرفوع. **إلّا**: أداة استثناء. **خالدًا**: مستثنى منصوب بالفتحة.

This is a complete affirmative exception: the group is stated and the sentence is not negated. Do not infer that every noun after **إلّا** must be accusative. Other exception constructions require their own rules. Even the governor is analysed differently: in a connected exception, **استثناء متصل**, the preceding verb or **إلّا** is identified as governor; in a disconnected exception, **استثناء منقطع**, government is assigned to **إلّا**. For now, practise the clear complete affirmative pattern only.

## Lesson 2: The six particles resembling verbs

### The core rule and meanings

**الحروف المشبّهة بالفعل** enter a subject–predicate construction, assigning **نصب** to their **اسم** and **رفع** to their **خبر**:

**الطَّالِبُ حَاضِرٌ ← إِنَّ الطَّالِبَ حَاضِرٌ.**

| Particle | Essential term | Meaning and example |
|---|---|---|
| إِنَّ | تأكيد، تحقيق | Emphasis or confirmation: **إِنَّ الطَّالِبَ حَاضِرٌ** — The student is indeed present |
| أَنَّ | حرف مصدر للتأكيد | An emphatic “that” clause: **عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ الطَّالِبَ حَاضِرٌ** — I knew that the student was present |
| كَأَنَّ | تشبيه | Resemblance: **كَأَنَّ القَمَرَ مِصْبَاحٌ** — The moon is like a lamp |
| لَكِنَّ | استدراك | Correcting an inference: **مَا زَيْدٌ قَوِيًّا، لَكِنَّهُ شُجَاعٌ** — Zayd is not strong, but he is brave |
| لَعَلَّ | توقّع | Expectation: **لَعَلَّ الغَائِبَ قَادِمٌ** — Perhaps the absent person is coming |
| لَيْتَ | تمنٍّ | A wish without expectation of attainment: **لَيْتَ الشَّبَابَ يَعُودُ** — If only youth would return |

**استدراك** removes an impression suggested by the preceding statement. Being physically weak might suggest being unbrave; **لكنّه شجاعٌ** corrects that inference. **ولكنّ** is also common.

Distinguish two kinds of **توقّع** with **لعلّ**: **ترجٍّ**, hope for something desirable, as in **لعلّ الغائبَ قادمٌ**; **إشفاق**, apprehension about something feared, as in **لعلّ الطفلَ مريضٌ**, “Perhaps the child is ill.” It can also express **تعليل**, a purpose or reason, as in **وافعلوا الخيرَ لعلّكم تفلحون**, “Do good so that you may succeed,” or the sense of **كأنّ**, as in **وتتّخذون مصانعَ لعلّكم تخلدون**, “You build strongholds as though you would live forever.” A shortened form **عَلَّ** also occurs. Learn these as contextual uses, not interchangeable translations in every sentence.

Two further reading details: **أنّ** can occasionally mean **لعلّ**; and **يا** before **ليت**, as in **يا ليتني**, may be analysed as an attention particle or as a vocative whose addressee is omitted.

### Why the name المشبّهة بالفعل

The six resemble verbs in five respects: verbal meanings such as **أؤكّد، أشبّه، أستدرك، أرجو، أتمنّى**; fixed final fatḥah; attachment of object-form pronouns and protective **نون الوقاية** as in **ليتني**; government of رفع and نصب; and forms of at least three letters. This explains the classification; they remain particles.

### Worked tarkīb

**لَعَلَّ المُسَافِرَ يَرْجِعُ.** — Perhaps the traveller will return.

- **لعلَّ**: حرف مشبّه بالفعل للترجّي، مبني على الفتح، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **المسافرَ**: اسم لعلّ منصوب بالفتحة.
- **يرجعُ**: فعل مضارع مرفوع بالضمة؛ فاعله ضمير مستتر تقديره هو يعود إلى المسافر.
- **يرجعُ** with its subject: جملة فعلية في محل رفع خبر لعلّ.

The predicate clause occupies رفع; this does not make its verb a noun or give every word inside it a nominative role.

Read: **تنصب هذه الحروف المبتدأ اسمًا لها، وترفع الخبر خبرًا لها.** Identify the two verbs of government.

## Lesson 3: Choosing between إنّ and أنّ

### Clause or noun equivalent

Both particles govern their own اسم and خبر in the same way. Their difference concerns the larger construction:

- **إِنَّ مكسورة الهمزة** introduces a clause where a clause is required: **موضع الجملة**.
- **أَنَّ مفتوحة الهمزة** and its contents form a noun equivalent, **مصدر مؤوّل**, where a noun-like unit is required: **موضع المفرد**.

Here **مفرد** contrasts with **جملة**; it does not mean that the expression contains only one written word.

**قَالَ خَالِدٌ: إِنَّ الضَّيْفَ صَادِقٌ.** — Khalid said, “The guest is truthful.”

The speech after **قال** is quoted as a clause, **مقول القول**. Within it, **الضيفَ** is اسم إنّ and **صادقٌ** its خبر. The quoted clause occupies **نصب مفعول به** of **قال**. Having an external position does not force a clause to become **أنّ**.

**عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ الضَّيْفَ صَادِقٌ.** — I knew that the guest was truthful.

**علمتُ**: past verb with **تُ** as subject. **أنّ**: حرف مصدريّ ونصب وتأكيد. **الضيفَ**: اسم أنّ منصوب. **صادقٌ**: خبر أنّ مرفوع. The whole **أنّ الضيف صادق** is **مصدر مؤوّل سدّ مسدّ مفعولي علم**: it fills the two object places together.

An **أنّ** construction retains emphasis; replacing it with an ordinary verbal noun or a bare object–complement pair need not preserve the same emphatic force.

### Common clause positions for إنّ

| Position | Example or recognition cue |
|---|---|
| At the beginning, حقيقة | **إِنَّ العِلْمَ نَافِعٌ** |
| Effectively at the beginning, حكمًا, after attention or rebuke | **أَلَا إِنَّ العِلْمَ نَافِعٌ**; likewise after **كلّا** |
| After an oath | **وَاللهِ إِنَّ الصِّدْقَ نَجَاةٌ** — By Allah, truthfulness is deliverance |
| After saying | **قَالَ: إِنَّ البَابَ مَفْتُوحٌ** |
| After واو الحال | **جَاءَ خَالِدٌ وَإِنَّهُ مُتْعَبٌ** — Khalid came while tired |
| After حتّى الابتدائيّة | **تَابَ النَّاسُ حَتَّى إِنَّ المُشْرِكَ تَابَ** — People repented; even the polytheist repented |
| Inside a relative clause, صلة | **جَاءَ الَّذِي إِنَّهُ صَادِقٌ** — The one who is indeed truthful came |
| Inside an adjectival clause, نعت | **نَظَرْتُ إِلَى رَجُلٍ إِنَّهُ صَالِحٌ** — I looked at a man who is indeed righteous |
| Before the lām that suspends a verb’s direct government | **عَلِمْتُ إِنَّ الضَّيْفَ لَصَادِقٌ** |

In the last example **اللام** causes **تعليق**: **علم** does not directly assign endings to the words inside the following clause. The clause still fills its two object places. This is different from ignoring the preceding verb altogether.

### Noun positions for أنّ

| External role of the whole مصدر مؤوّل | Example |
|---|---|
| فاعل | **تَبَيَّنَ أَنَّ الخَبَرَ صَحِيحٌ** — It became clear that the report was correct |
| نائب فاعل | **أُعْلِنَ أَنَّ البَابَ مَفْتُوحٌ** — It was announced that the door was open |
| Object complement | **عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ الخَبَرَ صَحِيحٌ** |
| مبتدأ with a preceding predicate | **مِنَ الوَاضِحِ أَنَّ الخَبَرَ صَحِيحٌ** — That the report is correct is clear |
| خبر of a noun denoting an abstract meaning | **المَرْجُوُّ أَنَّهُ مَغْفُورٌ** — What is hoped for is that he is forgiven |
| Governed by a preposition | **فَرِحْتُ بِأَنَّ الضَّيْفَ حَاضِرٌ** — I was glad that the guest was present |
| مضاف إليه | **هٰذَا دَلِيلُ أَنَّ الخَبَرَ صَحِيحٌ** — This is evidence that the report is correct |

Read: **أنّ مع اسمها وخبرها في تأويل مصدر.** **تأويل** here means construing the whole expression as a noun equivalent.

## Lesson 4: Further positions and genuine alternatives

### Apply the same structural test

These are reading extensions of the previous lesson. Do not decide from the immediately preceding word alone.

| Construction | Treatment |
|---|---|
| After **إلّا للحصر**, restricting the statement | Use **إنّ**: **ما أرسلنا قبلك من المرسلين إلّا إنّهم ليأكلون الطعام**. Here the clause states what is true of the messengers; it is not a noun exception |
| After **إلّا للاستثناء** | Use **أنّ**: **هو شجاعٌ إلّا أنّه ليس بكريمٍ** — He is brave, except that he is not generous |
| After **حيث** | Normally **إنّ**, because حيث is annexed to a clause: **جلستُ حيثُ إنّ عليًّا جالسٌ**. **أنّ** is allowed in the analysis that permits حيث to be annexed to a noun equivalent |
| Predicate concerning a concrete entity, **اسم عين** | A clause with **إنّ**: **خالدٌ إنّه صادقٌ**. Contrast abstract **المرجوّ أنّه مغفورٌ** |
| After **مذ، منذ** in this time construction | **ما تركَ الصلاةَ منذُ أنّه رجعَ من الحجّ**. A past verb commonly occurs within this complement |
| After temporal **ما المصدريّة الظرفيّة** | **صُمْ ما أنّكَ تستطيعُ** — Fast for as long as you are able |
| Coordinated with a noun, **عطف على المفرد** | **فرحتُ بنجاحِ خالدٍ وأنّه سالمٌ** — I rejoiced at Khalid’s success and his being safe; the مصدر مؤوّل shares the genitive construction |
| Replacing a noun, **بدل من المفرد** | **وإذ يعدكم الله إحدى الطائفتين أنّها لكم**: the أنَّ unit is construed as a substitute for **إحدى الطائفتين**, sharing its object position |

**حصر** = restriction; **صلة** = relative clause; **نعت** = description; **تابع** = dependent expression; **بدل** = substitute. Learn the relationship before trying to memorise the label.

### Where either hamzah is possible

The choice changes how the expression fits into the sentence.

1. **Explaining a reason.** **استغفروا اللهَ إنَّهُ غفورٌ رحيمٌ** gives a new explanatory clause. **استغفروا اللهَ أنَّهُ غفورٌ رحيمٌ** is construed with an omitted **لام التعليل**: **لأنّه**.
2. **After فاء الجزاء.** A form such as **فإنّه غفورٌ رحيمٌ** may be a fresh explanatory clause pointing to an understood response. With **فأنّه**, the noun equivalent can be مبتدأ with an omitted خبر, or خبر with an omitted مبتدأ, such as **فالأمرُ أنّه غفورٌ رحيمٌ**. It is this reconstructed nominal sentence that supplies the conditional response. Do not merely exchange vowels without recovering the structure.
3. **After لا جَرَمَ.** Fatḥah is more common. The expression conveys certainty or inevitability; the usage treated here concerns wrongdoing and its consequences, preserving the association of **جَرَمَ** with committing an offence. With **أنّ**, analyse the whole unit as فاعل of a verb-like **لا جرم**, or as خبر in an analysis with **لا لنفي الجنس** and **جرم** as its اسم. With **إنّ**, **لا جرم** is treated like an oath and the following clause as its response. This is a fixed expression for recognition.
4. **After a wāw with two possible attachments.** **فرحتُ بنجاحِ خالدٍ وأنّه سالمٌ** coordinates noun equivalents. **فرحتُ بنجاحِ خالدٍ، وإنّه سالمٌ** adds a clause: “I rejoiced at Khalid’s success; and he is safe.”
5. **After إذا الفجائيّة**, “suddenly / thereupon.” **خرجتُ فإذا إنّ سعيدًا واقفٌ** presents a clause; **أنّ** allows a noun-equivalent construction. Recognise both; begin analysis by identifying which unit the reading supplies.

### Worked analysis of two levels

In **خَالِدٌ إِنَّهُ صَادِقٌ**:

- **خالدٌ**: مبتدأ مرفوع.
- **إنّ** governs **هُ**, **في محل نصب اسم إنّ**, and **صادقٌ**, **خبر إنّ مرفوع**.
- **إنّه صادقٌ**: جملة اسمية في محل رفع خبر المبتدأ.
- **هُ** links the predicate clause back to **خالد**.

## Lesson 5: Order and coordination

### The core restrictions

These particles have **صدر الكلام**, a leading position in their construction; their governed expressions do not freely precede them. **أنّ** forms a complement and does not itself begin independent speech.

The اسم of **إنّ / أنّ** cannot itself require first position, as a conditional word does. Thus **إنّ مَنْ صمتَ نجا** is not a valid way to embed conditional **مَنْ** as اسم إنّ. A valid independent condition is **مَنْ صَمَتَ فَقَدْ نَجَا**, “Whoever kept silent has escaped.” The restriction concerns conditional **مَنْ**, not every use of the written word **من**.

### A preceding شبه جملة predicate

When the خبر is a شبه جملة, it may stand between the particle and a definite اسم; it must do so when the اسم is indefinite in this construction.

| اسم | Example | Order |
|---|---|---|
| معرفة | **إِنَّ خَالِدًا فِي البَيْتِ / إِنَّ فِي البَيْتِ خَالِدًا** | Both orders allowed |
| نكرة | **إِنَّ فِي البَيْتِ رَجُلًا** | Predicate precedes the indefinite اسم |

**إنَّ في البيتِ رجلًا**: **إنّ** governs; **في** is حرف جرّ and **البيتِ** مجرور. **في البيت** attaches to an omitted general predicate, **خبر إنّ مقدّم**. **رجلًا** is **اسم إنّ مؤخّر منصوب**. The kasrah on **البيتِ** and the predicate’s رفع function answer different questions.

### Coordination with اسم إنّ

**العطف على اللفظ** follows the actual accusative form. **العطف على الابتداء** looks back to the nominative subject construction underlying **إنّ**.

- After the predicate is complete, both are allowed: **إنّ خالدًا حاضرٌ وسعيدًا**; **إنّ خالدًا حاضرٌ وسعيدٌ**.
- Before completion, accusative coordination is allowed: **إنّ خالدًا وسعيدًا حاضرانِ**. Nominative **عطف على الابتداء** is also recognised before completion, but do not treat any raised noun as ordinary agreement with an accusative form. Analyse the actual construction.

“After completion” means that the coordinated noun comes after the خبر: compare **إنّ زيدًا قائمٌ وعمروٌ** with **إنّ زيدًا وعمروٌ قائمانِ**. The exceptional nominative readings require analysis; the uncomplicated production pattern before the predicate is accusative coordination.

This underlying رفع is explained either by assigning an underlying nominative position to **إنّ** with its اسم or to the اسم alone. Do not describe it as a visible ḍammah on **خالدًا**. Local nominative coordination is also admitted with **أنّ** in an additional analysis; do not turn that qualified allowance into an unrestricted rule for all six particles.

### Two recognition notes

An extra **باء** can occur on the خبر of **أنّ** when a preceding negative gives it the force of **ليس**: **أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا أَنَّ اللهَ … بِقَادِرٍ**. The predicate is genitive in form but retains its predicate position. This is not permission to add **بـ** to every affirmative predicate.

A clause introduced by **لعلّ** can be coordinated with a preceding purpose complement, whether that purpose is expressed directly or through a preposition. Read **لِتُبَيِّنَ … وَلَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ** as “so that you explain … and so that they may reflect.” Its internal اسم–خبر government remains intact.

Read: **يجوز التوسّط مع المعرفة، ويجب مع النكرة.** **يجوز** = is permitted; **يجب** = is required; **توسّط** = standing between.

## Lesson 6: Lightened إنْ

### إعمال and إهمال

**تخفيف** removes the doubling: **إِنَّ ← إِنْ**. Lightened **إنْ** is usually **مهملة**, without noun government, but **إعمال**, retaining its government, is also allowed.

Reading prerequisite: before **الـ**, the connecting hamzah is not pronounced. In the forms practised here, the preceding consonant with sukūn takes a temporary kasrah to avoid two consecutive vowelless consonants, **التقاء الساكنين**. Thus **إنْ** is read **إنِ العلمُ** in connected speech. It is still the lightened particle; the kasrah is not an inflectional ending.

| Reading | Analysis |
|---|---|
| **إِنْ خَالِدٌ لَصَادِقٌ** | إنْ مخفّفة مهملة؛ خالدٌ مبتدأ؛ صادقٌ خبره |
| **إِنْ خَالِدًا لَصَادِقٌ** | إنْ مخفّفة عاملة؛ خالدًا اسمها؛ صادقٌ خبرها |

### اللام الفارقة

The **لام فارقة** distinguishes affirmative lightened **إنْ** from negative **إنْ**. It is frequent in this construction; its necessity as a distinguishing device concerns the non-governing lightened reading. Retain it in the non-governing examples practised here. When **إنْ** actually governs an accusative اسم, the lām is not required as a distinguishing device; a lām present there may instead be described as **لام الابتداء المزحلقة**.

**إنْ خالدٌ لصادقٌ** therefore affirms truthfulness. Do not confuse it with a negative sentence merely because **إنْ** lacks shaddah. In an alternative grammatical treatment, **إنْ** is analysed as negative and the lām as carrying the sense of **إلّا**; the affirmative meaning of the whole is preserved. Use the lightened-particle analysis in the exercises unless told otherwise.

Lightened **إنْ** can introduce an ordinary nominal sentence or one already altered by a ناسخ:

**إِنْ كُنْتَ لَصَادِقًا.** — You were indeed truthful.

**إنْ**: مخفّفة مهملة. **كنتَ**: كان الناقصة with **تَ** in **رفع اسم كان**. **اللام**: فارقة. **صادقًا**: خبر كان منصوب. The accusative here comes from **كان**, not from **إنْ**. Entry into a verbal sentence is also admitted in another grammatical treatment; do not generalise the nominal patterns into unrestricted new constructions.

Read: **الغالب بعد التخفيف الإهمال، ويجوز الإعمال.** **الغالب** means usual, not obligatory.

## Lesson 7: Lightened أنْ and كأنْ

### An omitted pronoun and a predicate clause

**أَنَّ ← أَنْ** and **كَأَنَّ ← كَأَنْ** retain government in the analysis used here. Their اسم is an omitted **ضمير الشأن**, and their خبر is a sentence.

**ضمير الشأن** introduces the situation explained by the following clause. It does not refer back to a named person. **محذوف** means omitted, not pronounced or written.

**عَلِمْتُ أَنْ قَدْ حَضَرَ خَالِدٌ.** — I knew that Khalid had arrived.

- **علمتُ**: past verb and attached subject.
- **أنْ**: مخفّفة من الثقيلة، عاملة؛ اسمها ضمير الشأن محذوف.
- **قد**: حرف تحقيق. **حضرَ**: past verb; **خالدٌ**: its subject.
- **قد حضر خالدٌ**: في محل رفع خبر أنْ.
- The whole lightened **أنْ** construction fills the two object places of **علم**.

**خالدٌ** is the subject inside the predicate clause, not a delayed اسم أنْ. An alternative analysis treats lightened **أنْ** as a non-governing particle entering a clause and questions supplying the omitted pronoun. Keep the two analyses distinct; use the governing analysis above for practice.

### When a separator occurs

**فاصل** = separator; **جامد** = non-conjugating verb; **متصرّف** = conjugating verb; **دعاء** = supplication.

| Predicate clause after lightened أنْ | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Nominal | Directly follows: **أنِ الحمدُ للهِ**; the nūn has a connecting kasrah, not a case ending |
| Verbal with a جامد verb | Directly follows: **أنْ ليسَ للإنسانِ إلّا ما سعى** |
| Verbal supplication with a conjugating verb | Directly follows: **أنْ غَضِبَ اللهُ عليها**, understood as an invocation |
| Affirmative non-supplicatory past verb | Separated by **قد**: **أنْ قد حضرَ خالدٌ** |
| Affirmative non-supplicatory present verb | Separated by a future marker: **أنْ سيكونُ الأمرُ سهلًا** |
| With **لو** | **أنْ لو نشاءُ أصبناهم** |
| Negated clause | Separated by **لم، لا، لن**: **أنْ لم يرجعْ**; **أنْ لا يرجعُ**; **أنْ لن يرجعَ** |

The negating or future particle keeps its own effect. Thus **لم** makes **يرجعْ** jussive and **لن** makes **يرجعَ** subjunctive. Lightened **أنْ** does not itself make those verbs accusative. **أَلَّا** can represent **أنْ + لا**; spelling alone cannot identify which kind of **أنْ** is present.

### Lightened كأنْ

It too is analysed with an omitted شأن pronoun. Between it and a following verb, **لم** or **قد** commonly appears:

**كَأَنْ لَمْ يَحْضُرْ خَالِدٌ.** — As though Khalid had not attended.

**كأنْ**: حرف تشبيه مخفّف عامل؛ اسمها ضمير الشأن محذوف. **لم** governs **يحضرْ**, whose subject is **خالدٌ**. **لم يحضر خالدٌ** is the predicate clause, **في محل رفع خبر كأنْ**. Another analysis treats lightened **كأنْ** as non-governing.

Apply the same connected-speech rule to **لم يسمعِ الضيفُ**: the underlying ending is the jussive sukūn of **يسمعْ**, temporarily moved with kasrah before **الـ**. Analyse it as **مجزوم بالسكون، وحُرّك بالكسر لالتقاء الساكنين**. The temporary kasrah does not make the verb genitive.

## Lesson 8: ما الكافّة

### Blocking government

**ما الكافّة** is a **ما** that prevents the six verb-like particles from governing. **كفّ** means restraining or stopping. With government blocked, an ordinary following nominal sentence keeps its مبتدأ and خبر in رفع:

**إِنَّ العِلْمَ نَافِعٌ ← إِنَّمَا العِلْمُ نَافِعٌ.**

In the second sentence, **إنّ** is **مكفوفة عن العمل**, **ما** is **كافّة**, **العلمُ** is مبتدأ, and **نافعٌ** is خبر. **إنّما** here also restricts the assertion: “Knowledge is only beneficial.” Use context to judge what is being restricted.

With **ما الكافّة**, the particle can also precede a verbal sentence:

**إِنَّمَا يَقْرَأُ الطَّالِبُ.** — The student only reads.

**يقرأُ** is a present verb with its ordinary indicative ending; **الطالبُ** is its subject. Neither is اسم إنّ.

### The exception with ليت

With **ليتما**, both retaining and blocking government are allowed:

**لَيْتَمَا الشَّبَابَ عَائِدٌ** — إعمال: **الشبابَ** اسم ليت.

**لَيْتَمَا الشَّبَابُ عَائِدٌ** — إهمال: **الشبابُ** مبتدأ.

Both express the wish that youth would return. Also distinguish lightened **لكنْ**, which does not retain the noun government of **لكنّ**; do not extend the governing lightening pattern of **أنْ** to every particle.

Read: **تكفّها عن العمل، وتوسّع لها أن تدخل على الأفعال.** Explain what each verb says that **ما** does.

## Lesson 9: لا لنفي الجنس

### Negating the whole class

**لا لنفي الجنس**, also **لا التبرئة**, excludes every member of the class from the predicate: **لَا رَجُلَ حَاضِرٌ**, “No man at all is present.” It assigns نصب to its اسم and رفع to its خبر.

Its governing use requires class-wide negation, an indefinite اسم directly following it, and no preceding preposition governing that construction.

**لا رجلَ حاضرٌ** does not merely deny that one man is present. Contrast **لا رجلٌ حاضرًا بل رجلانِ**, “Not one man is present, but two.” This second **لا** negates singularity, **نفي الوحدة**, and governs like **ليس**.

### Why رجلَ has no tanwīn

The simple اسم, neither مضاف nor شبيه بالمضاف, is **مبني في محل نصب**. For the singular example:

**لا**: حرف لنفي الجنس. **رجلَ**: اسم لا مبني على الفتح في محل نصب. **حاضرٌ**: خبر لا مرفوع بالضمة.

Two prerequisites prevent false generalisation:

- **مضاف**: **لا طَالِبَ عِلْمٍ مَذْمُومٌ** — No seeker of knowledge is blameworthy. **طالبَ** is an inflected اسم لا منصوب; **علمٍ** is مضاف إليه.
- **شبيه بالمضاف**, completed by something it governs: **لا عَاصِيًا أَبَاهُ نَاجِحٌ** — No one disobeying his father is successful. **عاصيًا** is اسم لا منصوب; it governs **أباه** as an object. **أباه** has accusative alif and **ه** is its possessive complement. Here tanwīn is retained.

Thus “اسم لا is always fixed on fatḥah” is false. In this lesson’s independent practice, use simple singular nouns unless another pattern is specified.

### When government stops

| Condition | Example | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| A preposition precedes لا | **بِلَا حِسَابٍ** — without reckoning | لا does not assign نصب; حسابٍ is genitive after باء |
| The اسم is separated from لا | **لَا فِي الدَّارِ رَجُلٌ وَلَا امْرَأَةٌ** | لا is مهملة and must be repeated; رجلٌ is a delayed مبتدأ |
| A definite noun follows | **لَا خَالِدٌ حَاضِرٌ وَلَا سَعِيدٌ** | Repeat non-governing لا; do not make خالد its accusative اسم |
| لا is repeated directly before the nouns | **لَا خَوْفَ وَلَا حُزْنَ / لَا خَوْفٌ وَلَا حُزْنٌ** | Both governing and non-governing treatments occur |

In the last row, supply the shared contextual predicate **هنا**, “here,” if needed. With governing **لا**, each simple noun is built on fatḥah in نصب. With both particles non-governing, the nouns are nominative. These are two clear allowed patterns, not an exhaustive account of every mixed coordination possibility.

## Lesson 10: Particles resembling ليس

### Reversing the case pattern

**الحروف المشبّهة بليس** resemble **ليس** in negation and in entering a subject–predicate construction. When they govern, they assign **رفع** to the اسم and **نصب** to the خبر. The three are **ما، لا، لات**.

**مَا خَالِدٌ غَائِبًا.** — Khalid is not absent.

**ما**: حرف نفي يعمل عمل ليس. **خالدٌ**: اسم ما مرفوع. **غائبًا**: خبر ما منصوب.

### The three conditions for ما الحجازيّة

1. The اسم precedes the خبر.
2. Extra **إنْ الزائدة** does not accompany ما.
3. **إلّا** does not restrict the predicate and undo the negation.

| Governing construction | When a condition fails |
|---|---|
| **ما خالدٌ غائبًا** | **ما غائبٌ خالدٌ**: advanced predicate, so ما is مهملة |
| **ما خالدٌ غائبًا** | **ما إنْ خالدٌ غائبٌ**: extra إنْ, so ما is مهملة |
| **ما خالدٌ غائبًا** | **ما خالدٌ إلّا ضيفٌ**: restriction, so ما is مهملة |

In the non-governing forms, analyse the nominatives as مبتدأ and خبر. In the reversed order, **غائبٌ** is the advanced خبر and **خالدٌ** the delayed مبتدأ. **إنْ** in the second row is additional, not the lightened affirmative particle of Lesson 6.

This governing usage is called **حجازيّة**. In the non-governing Tamīmī usage, ما does not govern even when these conditions are met: **ما خالدٌ غائبٌ**. Therefore a nominative predicate after ما is not automatically an error; identify the intended usage.

An extra **باء** can mark the predicate: **ما خالدٌ بِغائبٍ**. **غائبٍ** is genitive in form, **مجرور لفظًا**, but in the governing analysis its position remains accusative, **منصوب محلًّا خبر ما**.

### لا and لات

**لا** governing like ليس is chiefly encountered in poetry; the singular-negation distinction was introduced in Lesson 9. Recognise **لا رجلٌ قائمًا بل رجلانِ** without extending this less common pattern indiscriminately.

**لات** is associated with time, **الحين**. Its اسم is usually omitted, understood as **الحينُ**:

**وَلَاتَ حِينَ مَنَاصٍ** — And it is no time for escape.

**لات**: حرف نفي يعمل عمل ليس. Its اسم is omitted, approximately **الحينُ**. **حينَ**: خبر لات منصوب، وهو مضاف. **مناصٍ**: مضاف إليه مجرور. Expanded for analysis: **ولاتَ الحينُ حينَ مناصٍ**.

For recognition: **لات** is analysed either as a single particle or as **لا** with an added tāʾ. **إنْ النافية** is also recognised as governing like ليس in a less common usage, **إنْ زيدٌ قائمًا**, but more commonly does not govern: **إنْ زيدٌ قائمٌ**. This is distinct from lightened affirmative **إنْ** and additional **إنْ** after ما.

### Putting the نواسخ together

**نواسخ** are governors that enter and alter an underlying مبتدأ–خبر construction. The five groups are:

| Group | Basic change |
|---|---|
| أفعال القلوب | The original pair becomes two objects |
| الأفعال الناقصة | اسم مرفوع، خبر منصوب |
| الحروف المشبّهة بالفعل | اسم منصوب، خبر مرفوع |
| لا لنفي الجنس | اسم في نصب، خبر مرفوع |
| الحروف المشبّهة بليس | اسم مرفوع، خبر منصوب |

Read without glosses: **يشترط لعمل ما أن يتقدّم اسمها، وألّا تقترن بإن الزائدة، وألّا يقترن خبرها بإلّا.** Explain the three tests in English, then state them briefly in Arabic.

### E. Independent transfer

**E1.** Supply endings and give full tarkīb:

**علمت أن في القرية طبيبا. إنما يعالج الطبيب المريض. لا مريض مهمل.**

Vocabulary: **قرية** village; **طبيب** doctor; **يُعَالِجُ** treats; **مريض** patient; **مُهْمَل** neglected. The last sentence means “No patient at all is neglected.” Explain why **الطبيب** is the subject of a verb in the second sentence but **طبيب** has a different role in the first.

**E2.** Express each meaning, then name the governing pattern:

1. Perhaps the guard is awake. Use **مستيقظ**.
2. No guest at all is absent. Use **ضيف، غائب**.
3. The door is not closed. Use governing **ما** and **مغلق**.
4. I knew that the traveller had returned. Use lightened **أنْ**, **قد**, **رجع**.
