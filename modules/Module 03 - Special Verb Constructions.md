# Module 3: Special Verb Constructions

الأَفْعَالُ النَّاقِصَةُ وَغَيْرُهَا

## Before you begin

Recall **مبتدأ، خبر، فاعل، مفعول به**, attached and implicit pronouns, and the difference between a whole clause’s grammatical position and its internal endings. You will use **رفع، نصب، جرّ، جزم** and distinguish inflection, **إعراب**, from fixed form, **بناء**.

This module teaches predicates after special verbs, then the constructions of praise, blame, and exclamation. For each example ask: **What does this verb mean here? What structure does that meaning require? What governs each ending?**

Work through the lessons in order. The separate answers explain the reasoning. Add endings for connected speech, not pause-endings. Later exercises supply fewer vowels and less English help. Recognition notes preserve less frequent constructions without requiring you to produce them freely.

## Lesson 1: كان and the predicate it requires

### The core rule

A **فِعْل نَاقِص** (*fiʿl nāqiṣ*) enters a subject–predicate construction. Its **اِسْم** is nominative and its **خَبَر** is accusative:

**البَابُ مَفْتُوحٌ. → كَانَ البَابُ مَفْتُوحًا.**

The door is open. → The door was open.

**ناقص** means that, in this use, the verb needs a predicate to complete its meaning. It does not mean that a letter is missing. A **فِعْل تَامّ** (*tāmm*) instead gives its verbal predication with a **فاعل**, without requiring this extra خبر.

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| نَاسِخ | An element changing the treatment of a nominal construction |
| اِسْم كان | The nominative noun or pronoun of كان |
| خَبَر كان | What is predicated of that noun or pronoun; accusative |
| اِتِّصَاف الاسم بالخبر | The subject’s having the description expressed by the predicate |
| سَمَاعِيّ | A pattern learned from established usage, rather than freely extending the regular verb pattern |

These patterns belong to **كان وأخواتها**, “كان and its sisters.” They do not turn the predicate into a direct object. We continue to classify **كان الباب مفتوحًا** as **جملة اسمية منسوخة**, a nominal construction affected by a ناسخ.

### Worked tarkīb

**كَانَ خَالِدٌ يَكْتُبُ الدَّرْسَ.** — Khalid was writing the lesson.

- **كانَ**: فعل ماضٍ ناقص مبني على الفتح، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **خالدٌ**: اسم كان مرفوع بالضمة.
- **يكتبُ**: فعل مضارع مرفوع بالضمة؛ فاعله ضمير مستتر تقديره هو، يعود على خالد.
- **الدرسَ**: مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة، عامله يكتب.
- **يكتب الدرس**: جملة فعلية في محل نصب خبر كان.

The inner verb is **مرفوع** although the whole predicate clause occupies **نصب**. Compare **كان خالدٌ في البيتِ**: **في البيت** fills the predicate position, while **البيتِ** remains genitive after **في**.

### The forms needed now

| Form | Meaning | Key analysis |
|---|---|---|
| كُنْتُ حَاضِرًا | I was present | تُ: في محل رفع اسم كان; حاضرًا: خبر كان |
| يَكُونُ خَالِدٌ حَاضِرًا | Khalid is/will be present, as context requires | يكونُ: مضارع مرفوع; خالدٌ: اسمه |
| لَنْ يَكُونَ خَالِدٌ حَاضِرًا | Khalid will not be present | يكونَ: منصوب بلن |
| لَمْ يَكُنْ خَالِدٌ حَاضِرًا | Khalid was not present | يكنْ: مجزوم بلم |
| كُنْ صَادِقًا | Be truthful! | كنْ: أمر ناقص; اسمه مستتر تقديره أنت |

**كان → كنتُ** and **يكون → يكنْ** are changes of word form, **صرف**. The missing internal wāw in **يكنْ** does not replace its jussive sign: here **الجزم بالسكون** on the nūn. In **كنتُ**, the past verb is fixed on sukūn before **تُ**, which is its اسم, not a فاعل.

### Time comes from the whole expression

**كان** often locates a state or event in the past. It can also express renewed or continuing activity, **استمرار**, or a habit, **عادة**:

- **كان خالدٌ حاضرًا**: a past state.
- **كان خالدٌ يكتبُ**: writing in progress, or repeated writing if the context indicates a habit.

The combination **كان + مضارع** does not invariably mean repeated habit. A particular past occasion can have the same form.

Context can give **كان** a continuing present truth, with the sense **لم يزل**, or future reference. Learn these as reading possibilities, not as reasons to translate every past form “is” or “will be.”

Some constructions with **ما كان…لـ…** concern **اِنْبِغَاء**, fittingness, or **قُدْرَة**, ability/possibility. For example, **مَا كَانَ لَنَا أَنْ نَكْذِبَ** can mean “It was not fitting for us to lie.” Here **لنا** is the fronted predicate phrase and **أن نكذب** the delayed اسم كان. Do not force the meaning into a report that “we were not lying.”

Read: **الفعل الناقص يرفع اسمه وينصب خبره.** Explain why this does not make **يكتبُ** accusative in the worked example.

## Lesson 2: The sisters of كان

### Time and change

The following share the nominative اسم and accusative خبر pattern. The time meanings shown are their basic uses here.

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| أَصْبَحَ | Had the predicate in the morning | أَصْبَحَ خَالِدٌ نَشِيطًا — Khalid was active in the morning |
| أَضْحَى | Had it in the forenoon | أَضْحَى العَامِلُ مَشْغُولًا — The worker was busy in the forenoon |
| أَمْسَى | Had it in the evening | أَمْسَى العَامِلُ تَعِبًا — The worker was tired in the evening |
| ظَلَّ | Had it through the daytime | ظَلَّ خَالِدٌ عَامِلًا — Khalid continued working during the day |
| بَاتَ | Had it during the night | بَاتَ الضَّيْفُ نَائِمًا — The guest spent the night asleep |
| صَارَ | Became, through change of entity or quality | صَارَ المَاءُ ثَلْجًا — The water became ice |

**غَدَا** can have the sense of **أصبح**. **آضَ، عادَ، غدا، راحَ** also occur among verbs treated as ناقصة in some analyses; recognise these extensions without adding them mechanically to every occurrence of those words.

**صار** covers a change of state or character too: **صَارَ الطَّالِبُ مُتَأَدِّبًا**, “The student became respectful.”

**كان، أصبح، أضحى، أمسى، ظلّ، بات** can convey a becoming-sense approaching **صار**. Their original colouring may remain: **ظلّ** can suggest duration, for example. Likewise, **اِرْتَدَّ، عادَ، اِسْتَحَالَ** can mean “became”; **ارتدّ** may indicate return to a previous state. Translate the actual context rather than treating these as exact substitutes in every sentence.

### Four verbs of continuation

**مَا بَرِحَ، مَا انْفَكَّ، مَا زَالَ، مَا فَتِئَ** express **اِسْتِمْرَار**, continuation. In this ناقص use, the verb must be preceded by **نَفْي** (negation), **نَهْي** (prohibition), or **دُعَاء** (a wish or invocation).

**مَا زَالَ خَالِدٌ يَكْتُبُ.** — Khalid is still writing / continued writing, according to context.

**لَا تَزَلْ صَادِقًا.** — Continue to be truthful.

In the second example **لا** prohibits ceasing: **تزلْ** is jussive; its اسم is implicit **أنت**, and **صادقًا** is its accusative خبر. A negative element with a verb of ceasing can yield the positive meaning of continuation.

These four have **نَاقِص التَّصَرُّف**, restricted conjugation: past and مضارع forms in this construction. This differs from **ناقص** meaning “needs a predicate.” Do not manufacture an imperative form for the continuation use; the prohibition pattern supplies a مضارع instead.

Recognition distinctions:

- **زالَ يَزالُ** in the negated continuation pattern differs from complete **زالَ يَزُولُ**, “ceased, disappeared,” whose verbal noun is **زَوال**.
- **ما زال** can carry an expectation that a change or end may come; it does not promise permanence.
- Literal **برح** concerns leaving a place; **انفكّ** connects with becoming released; **فتئ** has associations of ceasing. These help explain why negating them expresses persistence, but do not replace the constructional rule.
- After an oath, the negative **لا** can be omitted before a مضارع of this type. An apparently affirmative form may therefore still mean “will not cease.” Treat this as contextual omission, not permission to drop negation at will.

### ما دام and ليس

**مَا دَامَ** limits another action to the time during which its اسم has its خبر:

**أَجْلِسُ مَا دَامَ البَابُ مَفْتُوحًا.** — I remain seated as long as the door is open.

**ما** here is **مَصْدَرِيَّة ظَرْفِيَّة**, forming an expression of duration: “during the continuance of…”. It is not negative **ما**. **البابُ** is اسم دام; **مفتوحًا** is خبر دام. The whole **ما دام الباب مفتوحًا** is a noun-equivalent occupying an adverbial position, **في محل نصب ظرف زمان**, attached to **أجلس**.

**ما** can sometimes convey the duration sense of **ما دام** by itself, especially before **لم**. Recognise **ما لم…** as “as long as…not / unless…” where the context calls for it; do not label every **ما** as negation.

**لَيْسَ البَابُ مَفْتُوحًا.** — The door is not open.

**ليس** negates the present state in its basic use here: **نَفْي الحَال**. It has a past-form shape, but that does not make its meaning “was not.” Its اسم is nominative and its خبر accusative.

**ما دام** in the duration construction and **ليس** are **غَيْر مُتَصَرِّفَيْن** here: they do not supply a مضارع or imperative paradigm. Attached pronouns are still possible: **مَا دُمْتُ حَيًّا**, “as long as I am alive,” and **لَسْتُ غَائِبًا**, “I am not absent.” The **تُ** is their اسم in محل رفع.

Count the core group: **كان، أصبح، أضحى، أمسى، ظلّ، بات، صار، ما برح، ما انفكّ، ما زال، ما فتئ، ما دام، ليس** — thirteen.

### Worked tarkīb

**مَا زَالَ الضَّيْفُ عِنْدَ البَابِ.** — The guest is still by the door.

**ما** is a negative particle with no case position. **زالَ** is a past ناقص verb, fixed on fatḥah. **الضيفُ** is اسم زال مرفوع بالضمة. **عندَ** is ظرف منصوب and مضاف; **البابِ** is مضاف إليه مجرور. **عند الباب** attaches to an omitted general governor such as **كائنًا** and occupies **محل نصب خبر زال**. The outer construction is اسمية منسوخة.

Read: **ما في ما دام ليست نافية، وما في ما زال نافية.** Explain how the two constructions nevertheless differ in meaning.

## Lesson 3: Complete uses and omitted elements

### Does this occurrence need a خبر?

Most verbs in the كان group can occur as **تَامّة**: a verb with a فاعل, without a required خبر. The exceptions in this group are continuation **ما زال، ما فتئ**, and **ليس**.

| ناقص use | تامّ use |
|---|---|
| كَانَ البَابُ مَفْتُوحًا — The door was open | كَانَتْ حَرْبٌ — A war occurred |
| أَصْبَحَ خَالِدٌ نَشِيطًا — Khalid was active in the morning | أَصْبَحَ خَالِدٌ — Khalid entered the morning |
| صَارَ المَاءُ ثَلْجًا — The water became ice | صَارَ خَالِدٌ إِلَى البَيْتِ — Khalid went to the house |

The stated meaning determines the analysis. The last expression uses **صار** for going or reaching, with **إلى البيت** attached to the complete verb. It does not mean “became the house.” Likewise **دام** can express lasting as a complete verb in a suitable context; the restriction on the duration construction’s forms does not erase its other uses.

### Worked contrast

**كَانَتِ الحَرْبُ شَدِيدَةً.** — The war was severe.

**كانَ** is ناقص; **تْ** marks feminine gender; **الحربُ** is اسم كان مرفوع; **شديدةً** is خبر كان منصوب.

**كَانَتْ حَرْبٌ.** — A war occurred.

**كانَ** is تامّ; **تْ** is still only the gender marker; **حربٌ** is فاعل مرفوع. There is no missing predicate in the intended occurrence-sense. The sentence is verbal. In **كانتِ الحرب**, the connecting kasrah on **تِ** avoids two successive unvowelled consonants in connected speech; it does not change the particle into a subject-pronoun.

### Order inside the ناقص construction

The خبر may stand between the verb and its اسم:

**كَانَ وَاسِعًا البَيْتُ.** — The house was spacious.

**واسعًا** is خبر كان مقدّم; **البيتُ** is اسم كان مؤخّر. Case and meaning establish the roles.

A dependent of the خبر can also come before the ناقص verb. Compare:

**الكِتَابَ كَانَ خَالِدٌ يَقْرَأُ.** — It was the book that Khalid was reading.

**الكتابَ** is the fronted object of **يقرأ**. It is not اسم كان or خبر كان; the verbal predicate has an object that has been fronted. Distinguish fronting the **خبر** from fronting a **معمول الخبر**, a dependent governed inside that predicate.

### Shortening يكن

The final nūn of jussive **يكن، تكن، أكن، نكن** may be omitted when neither a following silent consonant nor an attached accusative pronoun blocks the omission:

**لَمْ أَكُنْ غَائِبًا → لَمْ أَكُ غَائِبًا.** — I was not absent.

The shorter verb is still jussive; do not call its remaining ḍammah rafʿ. Retain the nūn before **الـ** in **لم يكنِ البابُ مفتوحًا**, and when an accusative pronoun follows, as in **لم أكنْهُ**, “I was not him/it,” in a suitable identifying context. Omission can reinforce a sense of smallness or insignificance in context; it is not a different tense.

### Omitting كان with its اسم

After conditional **إنْ** or **لو**, **كان** and its اسم may be omitted while its accusative predicate remains:

**اِلْتَمِسْ خَاتَمًا وَلَوْ حَدِيدًا.** — Seek a ring, even if it is iron.

In the conditional part understand **ولو كان الخاتمُ حديدًا**. **حديدًا** is the surviving خبر of omitted كان. The expression **وَلَوْ** introduces the concession, “even if.” This is recognition of an established omission pattern, not a command to omit any verb and subject freely.

Omission outside these conditional contexts has alternative analyses. In **آمِنُوا خَيْرًا لَكُمْ**, “Believe; that is better for you,” one analysis supplies **يكن الإيمانُ خيرًا لكم**. Another does not omit كان: **خيرًا** stands for a qualified verbal noun, **إيمانًا خيرًا**, “a good believing,” or is the object of an understood verb such as “seek good.” Recognise the alternatives; the exercises use the clearer conditional pattern.

You may also meet shortened **ظَلْتَ** for **ظَلِلْتَ**, with one lām omitted. Recover the full form before identifying the pronoun and role.

Read: **الفعل التامّ يكتفي بفاعله ولا يحتاج إلى خبر.** Contrast **كانت حربٌ** with **كانت الحربُ شديدةً**.

## Lesson 4: Hope with عسى and its sisters

### The core rule

**أَفْعَال الرَّجَاء** (*afʿāl al-rajāʾ*) express hope or expectation. Their ناقص construction has a nominative اسم and a predicate concerning a prospective action:

**عَسَى خَالِدٌ أَنْ يَحْضُرَ.** — It is hoped that Khalid will attend / Khalid may well attend.

The three verbs are **عَسَى، اِخْلَوْلَقَ، حَرَى**. The latter two suggest that the event is fitting or likely: **صار خليقًا / حريًّا**, “became likely or apt.”

| Verb | Treatment of أنْ in its خبر |
|---|---|
| عسى | Frequent, but omission is possible |
| اخلولق | Required |
| حرى | Required |

Thus both **عسى خالدٌ أن يحضرَ** and the less usual **عسى خالدٌ يحضرُ** are possible. With **أنْ**, the inner مضارع is منصوب; without it, the regular مضارع here is مرفوع. The predicate as a whole remains **في محل نصب** in both constructions.

### Worked tarkīb

**حَرَى الطَّالِبُ أَنْ يَنْجَحَ.** — The student is likely to succeed.

- **حرى**: فعل ماضٍ ناقص مبني على الفتح المقدّر على الألف للتعذّر.
- **الطالبُ**: اسم حرى مرفوع بالضمة.
- **أنْ**: حرف مصدريّ ونصب، لا محل له من الإعراب.
- **ينجحَ**: مضارع منصوب بأن، وعلامة نصبه الفتحة؛ فاعله مستتر تقديره هو، يعود على الطالب.
- **أن ينجح**: مصدر مؤوّل في محل نصب خبر حرى.

The outer اسم and the inner فاعل refer to the same student but occupy different roles.

### The meanings of عسى

**عسى** includes a sense of approaching possibility, **مقاربة**, alongside:

- **تَرَجٍّ**: hope for something welcome.
- **إِشْفَاق**: apprehension about something unwelcome.
- **تَعْلِيل**: presenting an intended purpose or hoped-for result in context.

These overlap with meanings of **لعلّ**, but **عسى** carries the additional approximation sense. When **عسى** is attributed to Allah, its usual force is assurance; the hope belongs to the addressee, rather than uncertainty being attributed to Allah.

### A complete use

Hope verbs can also occur as **تامّة**, with a noun-equivalent supplying the subject. Learn this through **عسى**:

**عَسَى أَنْ يَحْضُرَ خَالِدٌ.** — It is hoped that Khalid will attend.

Here **عسى** can be **تامّة**. Its فاعل is the whole **أن يحضر خالد**, a **مصدر مؤوّل في محل رفع**. Internally **خالدٌ** is the subject of **يحضرَ**. There is no separate اسم عسى plus خبر in this complete analysis.

Compare carefully:

- **عسى خالدٌ أن يحضرَ**: **خالدٌ** اسم عسى; the noun-equivalent is its خبر.
- **عسى أن يحضرَ خالدٌ**: on the complete analysis, the whole noun-equivalent is فاعل عسى; **خالدٌ** belongs to the inner verb.

Read: **يجب اقتران خبر حرى واخلولق بأن، ويكثر ذلك في خبر عسى.** **اقتران** = accompanying; **يجب** = is required; **يكثر** = is frequent. Do not turn frequency into obligation.

## Lesson 5: Near-occurrence with كاد and its sisters

### The core rule

**أَفْعَال المُقَارَبَة** (*afʿāl al-muqārabah*) express the nearness of the predicate’s occurrence: **قُرْب وُقُوع الخَبَر**. The three here are **كَادَ، كَرَبَ، أَوْشَكَ**.

**كَادَ الطِّفْلُ يَسْقُطُ.** — The child almost fell.

**الطفلُ** is اسم كاد مرفوع; **يسقطُ** has its own implicit subject **هو**, and its verbal sentence occupies **نصب خبر كاد**.

**عسى** presents proximity through hope; **كاد** and its sisters present proximity to actual occurrence. A near-event is not the same as an event already begun.

### The distribution of أنْ

| Verb | Usual predicate | Less frequent alternative |
|---|---|---|
| كاد | كاد الطفلُ يسقطُ | كاد الطفلُ أن يسقطَ |
| كرب | كرب الضيفُ يخرجُ | كرب الضيفُ أن يخرجَ |
| أوشك | أوشك الضيفُ أن يخرجَ | أوشك الضيفُ يخرجُ |

**أنْ** places the action prospectively. Its frequent use with hope verbs and **أوشك**, compared with **كاد** and **كرب**, accords with presenting the action as relatively further ahead. This explains a tendency; it does not measure an exact time interval.

### Worked tarkīb

**يُوشِكُ الضَّيْفُ أَنْ يَخْرُجَ.** — The guest is about to leave.

**يوشكُ** is a ناقص مضارع, nominative with ḍammah. **الضيفُ** is its nominative اسم. **أنْ** governs **يخرجَ** in naṣb; its subject is implicit **هو**, referring to the guest. **أن يخرج** is **مصدر مؤوّل في محل نصب خبر يوشك**.

These hope and approximation verbs have restricted conjugation in the constructions studied: they generally retain past forms. **كاد** and **أوشك** also have the مضارع forms **يَكَادُ** and **يُوشِكُ**. Do not invent a matching مضارع for every member of the lists.

### Affirmative and negated كاد

Affirmative **كاد** presents the action as near, while not occurring at the point described:

**كَادَ الطِّفْلُ يَسْقُطُ.** — The child was close to falling; the falling had not occurred at that point.

Negated **كاد** can deny even approaching the action, making the negation stronger:

**لَمْ يَكَدِ الضَّيْفُ يَخْرُجُ.** — The guest was not even close to leaving, in the intended reading here.

**يكدْ** is jussive after **لم**. Its connecting kasrah before **الـ** is not a change of mood; **يخرجُ** remains nominative inside the predicate clause.

Context may establish eventual occurrence after that initial lack of approach. For instance, **لم يكد الطفل يقرأ. ثم قرأ** can describe a child initially far from reading who subsequently read. The second sentence supplies the eventual event; negated **كاد** alone does not automatically assert it.

A predicate may also be omitted when the context recovers it. In **أَخْطَأَ خَالِدٌ أَوْ كَادَ**, “Khalid made a mistake, or nearly did,” understand **كاد يخطئُ**. The اسم of **كاد** is implicit **هو**; its omitted خبر is the recoverable verbal predicate.

Read: **لا يلزم من نفي كاد إثبات وقوع الفعل.** Explain why a later sentence may still establish occurrence.

## Lesson 6: Beginning an action

### The core rule

**أَفْعَال الشُّرُوع** (*afʿāl al-shurūʿ*) express beginning an action. They include **جَعَلَ، أَخَذَ، طَفِقَ، عَلِقَ، أَنْشَأَ** in their inception senses.

**أَخَذَ خَالِدٌ يَكْتُبُ.** — Khalid began writing.

The verb raises its اسم. Its خبر is a **جملة فعلية مضارعية مجرّدة من أنْ**: a verbal sentence with a مضارع, without **أنْ**. The act has begun, so the prospective **أنْ** construction is not used here.

These verbs occur as past forms in the inception construction taught here. That restriction does not prohibit their other forms in other meanings. They are **قياسية** in this classification, grouped with the specially established patterns because they resemble ناقص verbs in government.

### Worked tarkīb

**جَعَلَ الطَّالِبُ يَقْرَأُ الكِتَابَ.** — The student began reading the book.

1. **جعلَ**: فعل ماضٍ للشروع مبني على الفتح، يعمل عمل الفعل الناقص.
2. **الطالبُ**: اسم جعل مرفوع بالضمة.
3. **يقرأُ**: مضارع مرفوع بالضمة؛ فاعله مستتر تقديره هو، يعود على الطالب.
4. **الكتابَ**: مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة، عامله يقرأ.
5. **يقرأ الكتاب**: جملة فعلية في محل نصب خبر جعل.

Compare the transformation use from the previous module: **جعلَ النجارُ الخشبَ بابًا**. There **النجارُ** is فاعل and the verb governs two objects. Here **جعل** means “began,” with an اسم and verbal خبر. Meaning selects the construction.

### When part of the predicate is unexpressed

For recognition, **طَفِقَ مَسْحًا** can stand for **طفق يمسحُ مسحًا**, “He began wiping.” The اسم is implicit **هو**. The verb **يمسح** is understood in the خبر; **مسحًا** is **مفعول مطلق**, a verbal noun connected with that understood action. It does not become an ordinary single-word خبر merely because the verb is unprinted.

### Compare the three groups

| Intended meaning | Representative form | أنْ |
|---|---|---|
| Hope | عسى خالدٌ أن يكتبَ | Frequent; required with حرى and اخلولق |
| Near-occurrence | كاد خالدٌ يكتبُ | Uncommon with كاد and كرب; frequent with أوشك |
| Beginning | أخذ خالدٌ يكتبُ | Excluded from this خبر |

The broad label **أفعال المقاربة** can sometimes encompass all three groups. Identify whether a discussion uses the label broadly or specifically for **كاد وأخواتها**.

Read: **خبر فعل الشروع جملة مضارعية مجرّدة من أنْ.** Explain what both **مضارعية** and **مجرّدة** require.

## Lesson 7: Praise and blame

### The core construction

**أَفْعَال المَدْح وَالذَّمّ** express praise or blame. The principal forms here are **نِعْمَ، حَبَّذَا** for praise, and **بِئْسَ، سَاءَ، لَا حَبَّذَا** for blame.

**نِعْمَ الرَّجُلُ خَالِدٌ!** — What an excellent man Khalid is!

Distinguish two nominatives:

- **الرجلُ** is the **فاعل** of **نعم**.
- **خالدٌ** is the **مَخْصُوص بِالمَدْح**, the particular person praised.

The construction performs praise, **إنشاء المدح**; it is not merely a report of a past event. In the blame construction, use **المخصوص بالذمّ**.

### Three forms of the فاعل

| Subject pattern | Example | Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| A noun with **أل الجنس**, also called **لام الجنس**, the generic article | نِعْمَ الرَّجُلُ خَالِدٌ | الرجلُ: expressed subject; the category includes the particular person praised |
| A noun added to a noun with أل الجنس | نِعْمَ خُلُقُ الرَّجُلِ الصِّدْقُ | خلقُ: subject and مضاف; الرجلِ: مضاف إليه; الصدقُ: مخصوص |
| An implicit pronoun explained by **تمييز** | نِعْمَ رَجُلًا خَالِدٌ | The subject is implicit; رجلًا explains its category; خالدٌ is مخصوص |

**تَمْيِيز** (*tamyīz*) is an explanatory specification. Here it is an indefinite accusative, not an object receiving an action. **ضمير مستتر مفسَّر بتمييز** means “an implicit pronoun explained by a specification.” The specification can be understood as transferred from an expressed-subject construction: **تمييز محوّل عن الفاعل**.

Do not make **رجلًا** a فاعل with an accusative ending. The implicit pronoun occupies **محل رفع فاعل**; **رجلًا** has its own accusative role.

### Worked tarkīb

**بِئْسَ الخُلُقُ الكَذِبُ!** — What an evil character trait lying is!

- **بئسَ**: فعل ماضٍ جامد لإنشاء الذمّ، مبني على الفتح.
- **الخلقُ**: فاعل مرفوع بالضمة.
- **الكذبُ**: مخصوص بالذمّ، مرفوع. For full analysis here: **مبتدأ مؤخّر مرفوع بالضمة**.
- **بئس الخلق**: جملة فعلية في محل رفع خبر مقدّم for that delayed مبتدأ.

The label **مخصوص** identifies what is singled out. In the analysis used here, its nominative sentence role is **مبتدأ مؤخّر**. Another account attributes the raising of both فاعل and مخصوص to the praise/blame verb itself. Keep the two accounts distinct; the exercises use the delayed-مبتدأ analysis.

If the context identifies the praised person or thing, the مخصوص may be omitted. After discussing Khalid, **نِعْمَ الرَّجُلُ!** can praise him with **خالدٌ** understood. The expressed **الرجلُ** remains the subject; omission does not merge the two roles.

### Fixed forms and feminine marking

These are **جَامِدَة / غَيْر مُتَصَرِّفَة** in their praise/blame uses. Do not give them ordinary present and command paradigms. Feminine **تْ** can nevertheless occur: **نِعْمَتْ، سَاءَتْ**.

Both **نِعْمَ المَرْأَةُ هِنْدٌ** and **نِعْمَتِ المَرْأَةُ هِنْدٌ** are possible. The feminine marker may also be licensed by a feminine مخصوص, even when the expressed subject is not itself feminine. The **تْ** is a marker, not a subject-pronoun.

A verb originally on **فَعُلَ**, or transferred to that pattern, can also convey praise or blame. Recognise **حَسُنَ** and the expressive **كَتُبَ زَيْدٌ**, “What an excellent writer Zayd is!” Here **كَتُبَ**, with ḍammah on its middle consonant, differs from ordinary **كَتَبَ**, “wrote.” The same expressive type can convey exclamation, developed in Lesson 9.

Read: **رجلًا تمييز منصوب، والفاعل ضمير مستتر، وخالد مخصوص بالمدح.** Reconstruct the sentence and explain the three roles.

## Lesson 8: حبّذا and ما after praise verbs

### حبّذا has an expressed subject

**حَبَّذَا الصَّبْرُ!** — How excellent patience is!

Analyse **حبّذا** as **حَبَّ + ذَا**:

- **حبَّ**: fixed past verb expressing praise, مبني على الفتح.
- **ذا**: اسم إشارة مبني على السكون في محل رفع فاعل.
- **الصبرُ**: مخصوص بالمدح; in the chosen full analysis, مبتدأ مؤخّر مرفوع.
- **حبّذا**: the preceding verb–subject clause, في محل رفع خبر.

**ذا** remains masculine singular even when the مخصوص is feminine or plural: **حَبَّذَا هِنْدٌ**; **حَبَّذَا الرِّجَالُ**. Do not change it to **هذه** or a plural demonstrative to match the مخصوص.

**لَا حَبَّذَا الكِبْرُ!** — How blameworthy arrogance is! Here **لا** negates the praise, yielding blame. **الكِبْر** means arrogance.

### An added accusative

An accusative before or after the مخصوص can describe a circumstance or specify a category:

| Example | Accusative role |
|---|---|
| حَبَّذَا رَاكِبًا خَالِدٌ / حَبَّذَا خَالِدٌ رَاكِبًا | **حال**: راكبًا is a derived descriptive word, “riding”; خالد is its صاحب الحال |
| حَبَّذَا رَجُلًا خَالِدٌ / حَبَّذَا خَالِدٌ رَجُلًا | **تمييز**: رجلًا is a non-derived category noun, “a man” |

**مُشْتَقّ** means derived descriptive form here; **جَامِد** in this noun distinction means non-derived, not “a verb with no conjugation.” Read the term in its local context.

Specification can also use **مِنْ**: **حَبَّذَا هِنْدٌ مِنْ مُعَلِّمَةٍ!**, praising Hind as a teacher. **معلّمةٍ** is genitive after **من**, though the expression supplies specification. A grammatical function does not always appear as a bare accusative noun.

The formula may be introduced by **يا**, as in **يا حبّذا…**. This does not turn **حبّ** into a noun merely because **يا** precedes it.

### Recognising ما after نعم and بئس

**ما** can be a noun in these constructions. After **نعم**, it is often joined and pronounced **نِعِمَّا**. The verb’s fixed fatḥah is then estimated in the joined form; do not read the visible kasrah as case or mood.

Two structural settings admit alternative analyses:

| Setting | Analysis of ما |
|---|---|
| A single مخصوص follows, or is understood: **نِعِمَّا هُوَ** | A complete definite noun meaning **الشيء**, “the thing,” as فاعل; or an indefinite noun meaning **شيء**, as تمييز with an implicit فاعل |
| A clause follows: **نِعْمَ مَا تَقْرَأُ** | A relative noun as فاعل, followed by its صلة; or an indefinite specifying noun as تمييز, with an implicit فاعل and the following clause as نعت |

In the second pattern, the مخصوص is understood from context. With the relative analysis, the clause **تقرأ** has no case position as صلة; with the descriptive analysis, it occupies **نصب نعت** for the specifying noun. Do not mix the subject analysis of one account with the clause analysis of the other.

### Worked analysis with a relative noun

A book is under discussion: **نِعْمَ مَا تَقْرَأُ!** — Excellent is what you read!

Take **ما** as **اسم موصول مبني على السكون في محل رفع فاعل نعم**. **تقرأُ** is a nominative مضارع with implicit subject **أنت**. Its object-link to **ما** is understood: **تقرأه**. The clause is **صلة الموصول لا محل لها من الإعراب**. The particular book praised is the omitted مخصوص.

Read: **تلزم ذا الإفراد والتذكير بعد حبّ.** **تلزم** = keeps to; **الإفراد** = singular form; **التذكير** = masculine form.

## Lesson 9: Exclamation

### Meaning and regular patterns

**تَعَجُّب** (*taʿajjub*) expresses wonder at the degree of a quality. An exclamation can look like a question or command without seeking information or ordering an action.

The regular types, **قِيَاسِيّ**, include:

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| مَا أَفْعَلَهُ | مَا أَحْسَنَ الصَّبْرَ! | How excellent patience is! |
| أَفْعِلْ بِهِ | أَحْسِنْ بِالصَّبْرِ! | How excellent patience is! |
| A verb originally on فَعُلَ, or transferred to it | حَسُنَ خَالِدٌ! | How fine Khalid is! — in an exclamatory context |

The third type can have **باء زائدة** before its subject: **حَسُنَ بِخَالِدٍ!**. Its expressive meaning comes from the construction and context; ordinary **حسن** can simply report a quality.

### Worked tarkīb of ما أفعله

**مَا أَحْسَنَ الصَّبْرَ!**

There are two accounts of **ما** here: an interrogative, **استفهام**, used for wonder, or a described indefinite, **نكرة موصوفة بمعنى شيء عظيم**, “a great thing.” For the full analyses in this lesson, use the interrogative account consistently:

1. **ما**: اسم استفهام مبني على السكون في محل رفع مبتدأ؛ الاستفهام للتعجّب.
2. **أحسنَ**: فعل ماضٍ لإنشاء التعجّب، مبني على الفتح.
3. Its subject: ضمير مستتر تقديره هو، يعود على ما، في محل رفع فاعل.
4. **الصبرَ**: مفعول به منصوب بالفتحة.
5. **أحسن الصبر**: جملة فعلية في محل رفع خبر.

The meaning is exclamation, not “Patience did not improve.” **ما** is not negative in the intended construction.

### Worked tarkīb of أفعل به

**أَحْسِنْ بِالصَّبْرِ!**

The verb comes in an imperative-shaped form, **صُورَة الأَمْر**, from a past **أَفْعَلَ** origin. Its following noun is the subject, introduced by added **باء**; it is not an ordinary command with an implicit **أنت**.

- **أحسنْ**: فعل تعجّب جاء على صورة الأمر. Its sukūn belongs to that fixed shape, not jussive government by a preceding particle.
- **الباء**: حرف جرّ زائد.
- **الصبرِ**: فاعل مجرور لفظًا بالباء، مرفوع محلًّا.

**زائد** is a grammatical description: the noun has a subject role despite the preposition’s effect on its pronounced ending. It does not mean “meaningless” or give permission to delete the bāʾ from this formula.

The added bāʾ can also carry an exclamatory force in expressions such as **كَفَى بِاللهِ** and **بِحَسْبِكَ زَيْدٌ**, “Zayd is sufficient for you.” Recognise the force; their separate detailed constructions need not be produced here.

### Word formation matters

For forming these exclamations, the base verb here is **ثُلَاثِيّ مُجَرَّد**, an unaugmented three-root-letter verb. This does not mean that every written exclamation has only three letters. Work with the established bases practised here rather than inventing forms from any verb.

Two form-rules are important:

1. Do not replace the middle weak radical through ordinary **إعلال** in these formulas: **مَا أَطْوَلَ النَّهْرَ! / أَطْوِلْ بِالنَّهْرِ!**, “How long the river is!” Retain **و**; do not substitute **أطال** or **أطل**.
2. In **أَفْعِلْ بِهِ**, keep repeated radicals separate: **أَبْرِرْ بِخَالِدٍ!**, “How dutiful Khalid is!” In **مَا أَفْعَلَهُ**, assimilation is permitted: **مَا أَبَرَّ خَالِدًا!**

**إِدْغَام** means merging repeated consonants; the shaddah marks the resulting doubling. **عين الكلمة** is the middle root position in a three-letter base, not necessarily the second written character after extra letters are added.

### Established expressions beyond the regular forms

**سَمَاعِيّ** expressions of wonder include **سُبْحَانَ اللهِ!**, an interrogative such as **كَيْفَ تَكْفُرُونَ؟**, and a call such as **يَا بُشْرَى!** The latter means “Oh, good news!” Context makes the wonder clear. These are not additional templates for mechanically changing a verb into **أفعل**.

Read: **المجرور لفظًا قد يكون مرفوعًا محلًّا.** Explain using the subject of **أحسن بالصبر**.

2. Correct the forms in **ما أطالَ النهرَ!** and **أَبِرَّ بخالدٍ!** when exclamation is intended. Explain the distinct form-rule behind each correction.
3. Give an exclamation about the length of a **طَرِيق**, “route,” using **أَفْعِلْ بِهِ**. Explain the route noun’s pronounced ending and grammatical role.

## Module review

For full tarkīb, identify expressed and implicit components, the governor of each ending, and every clause’s or phrase’s position. State whether a verb is تامّ or ناقص in the intended use; do not guess from its dictionary form alone.

### A. Distinctions

**A1.** Match **اسم الفعل الناقص، خبر الفعل الناقص، مخصوص، تمييز، فاعل، مصدر مؤوّل** to the appropriate elements:

1. **خالدٌ** in **كان خالدٌ صادقًا**.
2. **صادقًا** in that sentence.
3. **حربٌ** in **كانت حربٌ**, “A war occurred.”
4. **خالدٌ** in **نعم الرجلُ خالدٌ**.
5. **رجلًا** in **نعم رجلًا خالدٌ**.
6. **أن يحضر خالد** in **عسى أن يحضر خالد**, on the complete analysis.

**A2.** Explain the difference between **ناقص** and **ناقص التصرّف**. Use **كان** and continuation **ما زال** to illustrate it.

### B. Select and correct

**B1.** Supply endings and explain their governors:

1. **لم يكن الضيفـ… حاضرـ…**.
2. **حرى الطالبـ… أن ينجحـ…**.
3. **أخذ الطفلـ… يقرأـ… الكتابـ…**.
4. **بئس الخلقـ… الكذبـ…**.

**B2.** Correct each error or claim:

1. **ما دام** means “did not last” in every construction.
2. **أخذ خالدٌ أن يكتبَ**, intended as “Khalid began writing.”
3. The خبر of كاد is always a single accusative noun.
4. The demonstrative in **حبذا هندٌ** must become feminine.
5. In **أحسن بالصبر**, the noun is an ordinary object because a preposition precedes it.
6. **لم يكد خالدٌ يكتبُ** necessarily means that Khalid wrote.

### C. Connected passages

**Passage 1**

**كان الضيف عند الباب. لم يكن خالد حاضرا. أوشك خالد أن يحضر. ثم حضر. أخذ يفتح الباب. صار الضيف في البيت.**

Meaning: The guest was by the door. Khalid was absent. Khalid was about to arrive. Then he arrived. He began opening the door. The guest came to be in the house. The implicit subjects in the fourth and fifth sentences refer to Khalid.

**C1.** Add endings. Give full tarkīb of the first, third, and fifth sentences. In the others, identify verb, اسم or فاعل, and any خبر. Explain the contrast between **أوشك** and **أخذ**, and between **حضر** and **صار** in these intended meanings.

**Passage 2**

**كان الطالب يقرأ. ما زال يقرأ. نعم رجلا الطالب. حبذا الطالب قارئا. ما أحسن الصبر!**

Meaning: The student was reading. He continued reading. What an excellent man the student is! How excellent the student is while reading! How excellent patience is! **قارئ** = reading / a reader.

**C2.** Add endings and analyse all five sentences. Distinguish the role of **رجلًا** from **قارئًا**, and the role of **الطالب** in the first, third, and fourth sentences. For **ما أحسن الصبر**, use the interrogative analysis of ما.

### D. Read the Arabic analysis

### E. Final transfer

**E1.** Analyse fully and add vowels:

**حرى المسافر أن يصل. أخذ المسافر يسأل.**

Meanings: The traveller is likely to arrive. The traveller began asking. **مُسَافِر** = traveller; **يَصِلُ** = arrives; **يَسْأَلُ** = asks. Explain the two different inner verb endings and identify every implicit subject.

**E2.** Produce three sentences about **الصِّدْق**, truthfulness:
